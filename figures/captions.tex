% F1 (Introduction) -- figures/out/F1.pdf
\newcommand{\figcapOne}{Stylised renderings, not isometric embeddings, of the pillows
$\mathcal O(2,8,8)$ (a) and $\mathcal O(3,3,12)$ (b), shaded by $4\pi t\,\mathfrak h_t(x,x)$ at
$t=0.02$ on one logarithmic scale, darker meaning larger. Away from the cone points the value is
$1-t/3+O(t^2)$, and at a cone point of order $m$ it rises to $m$. The two pillows have the same
area and the same $c_1,c_2$.}

% F2 (Section 2) -- figures/out/F2.pdf
\newcommand{\figcapTwo}{The tilings of the upper sheet of the hyperboloid model by the triangle
groups $\Gamma(2,8,8)$ (a) and $\Gamma(3,3,12)$ (b), drawn exactly on the cap of points within
distance $2.8$ of the vertex of $T_{p,q,r}$ with angle $\pi/p$. The triangle $T_{p,q,r}$ (dark
tone) and its mirror image in a side (light tone) form one copy of the pillow. The other tiles
alternate between white and grey.}

% F3 (Section 3) -- figures/out/F3.pdf
\newcommand{\figcapThree}{The mirror argument of Theorem~S: discs mark $Z=U^*\uplus(-V^*)$ on
the real axis, stacked by multiplicity, rings mark $-Z$, and sharing $c_1,\dots,c_L$ with
$|Z|\le 2L$ forces $Z=-Z$ (every ring on a disc), hence equal signatures. In (a), $(2,8,8)$ (dark)
against $(3,3,12)$ (light) has $|Z|=6$ and shares exactly $c_1,c_2$; below it is the symmetric
configuration that sharing $c_1,c_2,c_3$ would force, shown for $(2,8,8)$ against itself. In (b),
$(1;15)$ against $(0;3,3,5,5)$, with $U=\{1,15\}$ padded by one order~$1$, the two sides differ in
size by $|V|-|U|=2=2(g-g')$, and the pair shares exactly $c_1,c_2$.}

% F4 (Section 3) -- figures/out/F4.pdf
\newcommand{\figcapFour}{Dots: for each of the $525$ complete area classes with
$s=\mathrm{Area}/2\pi\le 7/5$, the largest $K_{\mathrm{mult}}(\mathcal O;\mathrm{Sig})$ in the
class; the split disc is the class of $(2,8,8)$ and $(3,3,12)$. Solid steps: the upper bound
$\lfloor 2s\rfloor+4$. Dashed steps, from $s=3$: the lower bound $\lfloor\log_4(s+1)\rfloor+2$ for
$\max_{\mathrm{Area}\le 2\pi s}K_{\mathrm{mult}}$; squares: the pairs of Theorem~N for
$L=2,\dots,6$, which share $c_1,\dots,c_L$, so $K_{\mathrm{mult}}\ge L+1$ there.}

% F5 (Sections 4 and 7) -- figures/out/F5.pdf
\newcommand{\figcapFive}{(a) $D(t)=Z_{(2,8,8)}(t)-Z_{(3,3,12)}(t)$ from the computed spectra
(heavy), against the sum of the cone terms of the trace formula (dashed) and its partial sums
$d_3t$, $d_3t+d_4t^2$ and $d_3t+d_4t^2+d_5t^3$ (light to dark grey). The series is asymptotic,
the closed geodesics take over beyond $t\approx0.1$, and $D$ changes sign near $t=0.34$.
(b) For the family $(0;3,3,3,3)$: $|Z_0-Z_\vartheta|$ for $\vartheta=0.4,\dots,2.8$ (light to
dark grey; a shorter systole is darker), the predicted $|\mathrm{Hyp}(0)-\mathrm{Hyp}(\vartheta)|$
(thin dashed), and the largest error budget (dotted); each difference leaves the noise at its
predicted time (circles), which is set by the shorter systole.}

% F6 (Sections 4 and 7) -- figures/out/F6.pdf
\newcommand{\figcapSix}{(a) Four members $\mathcal O(\vartheta)$ of the family $(0;3,3,3,3)$,
$\vartheta=0,0.8,1.6,2.8$ (stylised, not isometric, at one scale), each with its shortest closed
geodesic, of length $2.634$, $1.831$, $1.250$ and $0.694$. (b) The eigenvalues

% F7 (Section 5) -- figures/out/F7.pdf
\newcommand{\figcapSeven}{The least-order strata of hyperbolic triads in the $(S,R)$ plane,
$p=2,\dots,8$ (light to dark grey): at each sum $S$ the reciprocal sums of stratum $p$ lie between
$R^-_{S,p}$ and $R^+_{S,p}$. Dots mark the first overlap $S^*(p)$ of strata $p$ and $p+1$, and
circles mark their first two-coefficient collision (the same point for $p=2,4$). The split disc is
the first collision of all, $(2,8,8)\sim(3,3,12)$ at $(18,3/4)$.}

% F8 (Section 6) -- figures/out/F8.pdf
\newcommand{\figcapEight}{Recovery error of the cone orders against an error $\delta$ in every
heat coefficient, worst case over the signs of the errors. The curves are the simple orders
$(2,3,7)$ (solid), the double order of $(2,8,8)$ (heavy, dark) and the triple order $(4,4,4)$
(dashed), and, dotted, $(4,4,4)$ under errors that come from real multisets; their slopes are
$1$, $1/2$, $1/3$ and $1/2$. Diamonds mark the certified thresholds $\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$, below
which rounding recovers the orders exactly; the thin line is the rounding limit $1/2$.}
