Defines a census-taker number (CTN) N as a positive integer with EXACTLY ONE pair of distinct unordered triples {a,b,c},{d,e,f} of positive integers with abc=def=N (equal PRODUCT) AND a+b+c=d+e+f (equal SUM) -- this is the classical puzzle where a mother's product-and-sum clues plus 'oldest daughter' uniqueness pin down ages. Smallest CTN is 36, with mysterious triples {9,2,2} and {6,6,1}, magic sum 13 (example: also gives 40,72,96 as the four two-digit CTNs). Proves: Theorem 1, mysterious triples of a CTN share no common entry; Theorem 2, a CTN is never a prime power; Theorem 3, every CTN is a product of >=4 primes. Theorem 7/Corollary 8: if p and 2p-1 are both prime, N=p^2(2p-1)^2 is a CTN, giving infinitely many CTNs conditional on infinitely many such primes (open). States there are 0 one-digit, 4 two-digit, 29 three-digit, and 277 four-digit CTNs (largest 9996).
