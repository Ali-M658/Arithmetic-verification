- [[inverse-spectral-problems-for-collapsing-manifolds-ii-quantitative-stability-of]] — Inverse Spectral Problems for Collapsing Manifolds II: Quantitative Stability of Reconstruction for Orbifolds (full text, arXiv:2404.16448v1) — Lassas-Lu-Yamaguchi (arXiv:2404.16448v1, dedicated to Y. Kurylev), full text. Proves the ONLY known quantitative-stability theorem for a spectral inverse problem on Riemannian orbifolds. Theorem 1.2: for (X,mu) a collapsed limit space (X in class MM(n,Lambda,Lambda3,D)) with dim(X)=n-1 (i.e. X a Riemannian orbifold), finite interior spectral data {lambda_j, phi_j|_U}_{j=0}^{delta^-1} for delta < hat-delta determine a finite metric space X-hat with Gromov-Hausdorff bound d_GH(X\S_{sigma,delta}, X-hat) < C1(X,sigma) / (log log |log delta|)^{C2}, where S_{sigma,delta} is a neighborhood of the singular set, C1(X,sigma)->infinity as sigma->0, C2 depends only on n. This is an extremely weak (TRIPLE-log, i.e. worse than logarithmic) stability modulus, reflecting the severe ill-posedness of geometric unique-continuation. It rests on Theorem 1.3, a quantitative unique-continuation estimate for the wave operator giving an exp(h^{-C5}) bound (removing prior assumptions on covariant derivatives of curvature). Part I (arXiv:1209.5875, Kurylev-Lassas-Lu-Yamaguchi) proves uniqueness and only a qualitative/continuity stability statement (no explicit modulus) for the same collapsing-manifold-to-orbifold limit problem in the measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology; the quantitative bound is new to Part II. Setting is abstract Riemannian-orbifold reconstruction from interior spectral data (eigenvalues + eigenfunctions on an open subset), NOT recovery of finitely many discrete cone-angle/cone-order invariants from finitely many heat-trace or Laplace-spectrum symmetric functions -- methodologically and structurally disjoint from a Vandermonde/Newton-identities-style polynomial stability theorem, though it is the closest documented precedent for the phrase 'quantitative stability for a spectral inverse problem on an orbifold.' `evergreen`
