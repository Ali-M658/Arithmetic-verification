summary: 'J-STAGE landing page (free access) for Kisao Takeuchi, ''Arithmetic triangle
  groups,'' J. Math. Soc. Japan 29(1) (1977), 91-106, DOI 10.2969/jmsj/02910091, received
  13 Jan 1976. This is the PRIMARY SOURCE paper proving there are only finitely many
  arithmetic triangle groups and giving Theorem 3, the complete list of all 85 arithmetic
  triples (e1,e2,e3) (compact + noncompact types). The full PDF was independently
  retrieved via the J-STAGE direct-PDF endpoint (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmath1948/29/1/29_1_91/_pdf,
  which succeeds where the projecteuclid.org DOI/PDF links are blocked by Imperva
  bot protection) and read in full. VERIFIED DIRECTLY FROM THE PRIMARY SOURCE: Theorem
  3(i)''s list of 85 compact-type arithmetic triples explicitly includes both (2,6,6),(2,6,8),(2,6,12),(2,7,7),(2,7,14),(2,8,8),(2,8,16),(2,9,18),...
  and (3,3,4),(3,3,5),(3,3,6),(3,3,7),(3,3,8),(3,3,9),(3,3,12),(3,3,15),... -- so
  BOTH (2,8,8) and (3,3,12) are confirmed, from the primary source, to be arithmetic
  triangle groups on Takeuchi''s list. This paper (Theorem 3) does NOT itself classify
  the 85 triples into commensurability classes by quaternion algebra -- that classification
  is in Takeuchi''s companion paper ''Commensurability classes of arithmetic triangle
  groups,'' J. Fac. Sci. Univ. Tokyo Sect. IA Math. 24 (1977), 201-212, which could
  not be located/fetched online in the time available (logged as outstanding fetch).
  Note: proposition 4/Theorem 1 gives the arithmeticity criterion via the trace field
  k0=Q((cos(pi/e1))^2,(cos(pi/e2))^2,(cos(pi/e3))^2, cos(pi/e1)cos(pi/e2)cos(pi/e3))
  and an embedding-positivity inequality (15); this machinery, not sum-of-reciprocals,
  is what actually determines the 85-triple list -- so membership on Takeuchi''s list
  is logically independent of (although in this case coincident with) the paper''s
  own equal-reciprocal-sum coincidence.'
