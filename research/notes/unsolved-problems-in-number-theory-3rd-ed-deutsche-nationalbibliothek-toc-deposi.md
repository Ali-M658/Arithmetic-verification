## Key finding for novelty determination

Section D (Diophantine Equations, pp.209-310) subsections D11-D29 are the exhaustive, verbatim, page-numbered list of every Diophantine-equation topic in the 3rd edition (2004). The only two subsections whose titles could conceivably bear on "pairs of triples of positive integers with equal sum AND equal sum of reciprocals" are:

1. **D11. Egyptian fractions. p.252** (ends at D12's start, p.263, so D11 spans pp.252-262) — this is about representing a SINGLE given fraction (typically 1, or 4/n, or 5/n) as a sum of distinct unit fractions (the Erdos-Straus / Sierpinski territory). It is NOT about pairs of triples.

2. **D16. Triples with the same sum and same product. p.271** — this is the census-taker-number territory (equal SUM and equal PRODUCT, per Kelly 1964 and Meyers-See 1990, cf. arXiv:1204.2071 fetched separately in this batch). This is equal SUM + equal PRODUCT, not equal SUM + equal RECIPROCAL SUM. Structurally close in spirit (a pair-of-triples coincidence problem living in section D) but a different pair of invariants.

3. **D28. A reciprocal diophantine equation. p.309** — title suggests a single reciprocal-type Diophantine equation (likely 1/x+1/y=1/z or similar, consistent with placement just before "D29 Diophantine m-tuples"), not a pairs-of-triples coincidence. Body text not accessible from this TOC-only source; flagged as an outstanding open question — cannot confirm its exact content without the book's actual page 309.

**No subsection title among D1-D29 (all 29 titles transcribed above) matches "equal sum and equal sum of reciprocals" for PAIRS of triples.** The nearest structural analog by pattern (a pair-of-triples-coincidence problem in Section D) is D16, but its second invariant is PRODUCT, not RECIPROCAL SUM. This is strong (though not fully dispositive, since E/F subsections were only partially transcribed and body text of D11/D16/D28 was not read) evidence that Guy's UPINT does not contain this exact problem as a titled subsection.
