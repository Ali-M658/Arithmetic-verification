summary: 'Huang and Vaughan, ''Mean Value Theorems for Binary Egyptian Fractions''
  (arXiv:1108.0096; J. Number Theory 131 (2011), 1641-1656, DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2011.04.001).
  Establishes two mean-value (average-order, not pointwise) theorems for the number
  of solutions of a/n = 1/x+1/y: one with numerator a fixed and n varying, one with
  both a and n varying. A companion ''Mean value theorems for binary Egyptian fractions
  II'' exists (arXiv:1109.2274; Acta Arith. 155 (2012), DOI 10.4064/aa155-3-5). Both
  papers concern the TWO-term case, i.e. one order lower than the manuscript''s three-term
  problem, and both count single representations of a single value, not pairs of representations
  matched across two values on two symmetric functions. Establishes that ''mean value
  theorem'' (average count over a range of n) is the standard asymptotic-counting
  register in this literature, distinct from the manuscript''s proposed birthday-paradox
  heuristic, which would count coincidences BETWEEN independently-drawn triples rather
  than the average multiplicity of a single triple-equation.'

Abstract:
In this paper, we establish two mean value theorems for the number of solutions of the Diophantine equation $\frac{a}{n}=\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}$, in the case when $a$ is fixed and $n$ varies and in the case when both $a$ and $n$ vary.
