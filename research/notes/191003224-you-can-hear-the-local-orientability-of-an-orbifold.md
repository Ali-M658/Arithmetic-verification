  DOI 10.1016/j.difgeo.2019.101577 (confirmed via CrossRef; arXiv:1910.03224 abstract
  page shows no journal-ref). Full-text-verified THEOREM 4.7: ''A locally orientable
  orbifold cannot be isospectral to an orbifold that is not locally orientable.''
  COROLLARY 4.8 (equivalent to Dryden-Gordon-Greenwald-Webb Thm 5.1): an orbifold
  with any orientation-reversing local chart element cannot be isospectral to a manifold.
  Mechanism: parity argument on the heat-trace expansion -- integer-power terms vanish
  identically for locally-orientable orbifolds when dim is odd (and half-integer terms
  vanish when dim is even), while the corresponding term is a sum of STRICTLY POSITIVE
  b_0(gamma,x) contributions (Lemma 4.2) for non-locally-orientable orbifolds, forcing
  a term-by-term mismatch. This is QUALITATIVE (existence of ANY nonzero distinguishing
  term), not a count of how many heat coefficients are needed -- does not establish
  or claim a finite-coefficient determinacy threshold for cone-order recovery.'

Title:
You can hear the local orientability of an orbifold
Authors:
Sean Richardson
,
Elizabeth Stanhope
View a PDF of the paper titled You can hear the local orientability of an orbifold, by Sean Richardson and Elizabeth Stanhope
View PDF
Abstract:
A Riemannian orbifold is a mildly singular generalization of a Riemannian manifold which is locally modeled on the quotient of a connected, open manifold under a finite group of isometries. If all of the isometries used to define the local structures of an entire orbifold are orientation preserving, we call the orbifold locally orientable. We use heat invariants to show that a Riemannian orbifold which is locally orientable cannot be Laplace isospectral to a Riemannian orbifold which is not locally orientable. As a corollary we observe that a Riemannian orbifold that is not locally orientable cannot be Laplace isospectral to a Riemannian manifold.
