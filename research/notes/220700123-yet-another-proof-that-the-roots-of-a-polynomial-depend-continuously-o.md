summary: 'Ross, ''Yet another proof that the roots of a polynomial depend continuously
  on the coefficients,'' arXiv:2207.00123v2 (math.CA), DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2207.00123.
  Full PDF read (6 pages). Gives a nonstandard-analysis (infinitesimal) proof of the
  qualitative continuity-of-roots theorem: if f(z)=sum a_i z^i and g(z)=sum b_i z^i
  with |a_i-b_i|<delta for all i, then for every eps>0 there is delta>0 such that
  the roots of f and g can be paired (with multiplicity) so that |r_i-s_i|<eps for
  all i (Theorem 2, stated on p.5). This is qualitative continuity, NOT a quantitative
  rate. Its reference list (refs [7],[9],[11]) supplies the standard citable sources
  for the classical QUANTITATIVE root-continuity/perturbation theorem: M. Marden,
  Geometry of Polynomials, AMS Math Surveys vol. 3 (1966); A. Ostrowski, Solution
  of Equations in Euclidean and Banach Spaces, Academic Press, 1973 (the modern English
  edition containing Ostrowski''s root-perturbation bounds, originally from his 1940
  Acta Math. paper ''Recherches sur la methode de Graeffe''); Q.I. Rahman & G. Schmeisser,
  Analytic Theory of Polynomials, Clarendon Press, 2002. Useful for citing the modern
  lineage of the Ostrowski continuity-of-roots result and for a rigorous but non-quantitative
  alternative proof.'

Yet another proof that the roots of a polynomial depend continuously on the coefficients

Abstract:
The roots of a complex polynomial depend continuously on the coefficients; that is, an infinitesimal perturbation of the coefficients results in an infinitesimal perturbation of the roots. A short, straightforward proof of this is possible using infinitesimals.
