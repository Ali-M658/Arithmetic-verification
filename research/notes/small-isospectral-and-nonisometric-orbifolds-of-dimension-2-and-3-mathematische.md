summary: 'Linowitz-Voight, Math. Z. 281 (2015) 523-569, DOI 10.1007/s00209-015-1500-1;
  open arXiv version arXiv:1408.2001 (48pp) fetched directly for full text. THEOREM
  A / Theorem 4.1 (verbatim): ''The minimal area of an isospectral-nonisometric pair
  of 2-orbifolds associated to maximal arithmetic Fuchsian groups is 23*pi/6, and
  this bound is achieved by exactly three pairs, up to isomorphism.'' All three pairs
  have orbifold signature (0; 2,2,2,2,2,3,4) -- genus 0, 7 cone points (five order-2,
  one order-3, one order-4) -- and ARE hyperbolic (constant curvature -1, quotients
  of H^2 by arithmetic Fuchsian groups). Construction is Vigneras'' arithmetic method
  (quaternion-algebra unit groups / spinor genus of ternary quadratic forms), NOT
  Sunada''s method; the pairs are representation-equivalent (isospectral on all p-forms),
  proved non-isometric via the selectivity criterion (Chinburg-Friedman). These are
  GENUINE published isospectral non-isometric hyperbolic 2-orbifold pairs in print
  -- directly relevant to the manuscript''s framing. They are NOT triangle orbifolds
  (7 cone points, not 3) so do not bear on triangle-orbifold rigidity/isospectrality.
  Paper explicitly narrates the history of ERRONEOUS earlier claims: Maclachlan-Rosenberger
  (1994, Arch. Math. 62) claimed a genus-0 signature-(0;2,2,3,3) isospectral pair
  that Buser-Flach-Semmler later disproved (selectivity obstruction). Also gives Theorem
  B (genus-6 isospectral manifold pair) and Theorem C (3-orbifold volume 2.8340...).
  NOTE: a signature-(0;2,2,2,3,3) area-5*pi/6 example at line ~1807 of the arXiv text
  is a NEGATIVE illustration (proved NOT isospectral) used to demonstrate the selectivity-detection
  method -- must not be miscited as a positive isospectral example.'

Abstract
Revisiting a construction due to Vignéras, we exhibit small pairs of orbifolds and manifolds of dimension 2 and 3 arising from arithmetic Fuchsian and Kleinian groups that are Laplace isospectral (in fact, representation equivalent) but nonisometric.
