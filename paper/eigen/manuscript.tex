\abstract{The Laplace spectrum of a closed orientable hyperbolic $2$-orbifold determines its signature, the genus and the cone orders, but a computation yields only finitely many eigenvalues, each to some accuracy. Among orbifolds of area at most $A$, systole at least $\varepsilon$ and cone orders at most $M$, the first $N$ eigenvalues, each known to within $\delta$ and none omitted, determine the signature, with $N$ and $\delta$ explicit and also computable by a certified procedure that always terminates. With gaps certified in ball arithmetic, $10^2$ to $10^4$ eigenvalues, known to within about $6\times10^{-4}$ to $2.5\times10^{-6}$, suffice for the classes of our computed examples. The bounds on the orders and on the area cannot be dropped; at fixed accuracy, $N$ must grow at least like $\log M/\log\log M$. Whether the systole bound is needed is open. An a-posteriori version of the test, run on ten computed spectra, gives consistency checks, not determinations.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A closed hyperbolic surface or orbifold is heard through the eigenvalues $0=\lambda_0<\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots$ of its Laplacian. For a closed orientable hyperbolic $2$-orbifold $\Orb$, the whole spectrum determines the \emph{signature} $\sigma(\Orb)=(g;m_1,\dots,m_n)$, the genus of the underlying surface and the multiset of cone orders \cite[Thm~1.1, Prop.~3.3]{drydenstrohmaier2009}, and at constant curvature the heat invariants already determine the cone orders \cite[Thm~4.20, Cor.~4.21(iv)]{ucar2017}; isospectral orbifolds of the same signature need not be isometric \cite[Thm~A]{linowitzvoight2015}. An experiment or a computation, however, delivers finitely many eigenvalues, each with an error. This paper asks whether such data determine the signature, and with how many eigenvalues and what accuracy.

The answer is yes, with explicit numbers, once three quantities are bounded: the area, from below the systole (the least length of a closed geodesic), and from above the cone orders. Write $\Sig$ for the class of closed orientable hyperbolic $2$-orbifolds, and, for $A,\varepsilon>0$ and an integer $M\ge2$,
\[
  \Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)=\{\Orb\in\Sig:\ \Area(\Orb)\le A,\ \ell(\Orb)\ge\varepsilon,\ \text{all cone orders at most }M\},
\]
with $\ell(\Orb)$ the systole; $\Sig(A,M)$ denotes the finite set of hyperbolic signatures of area in $(0,A]$ with orders at most $M$; Theorem~\ref{thm:E} compares against all of it, not only against the signatures of the area of $\Orb$.

\begin{theorem}[Finitely many eigenvalues]\label{thm:intro}
For $A>0$, $\varepsilon>0$ and an integer $M\ge2$, the numbers $N\ge1$ and $\delta>0$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}, explicit in $A$, $\varepsilon$ and $M$ (they are given in terms of an explicit time $t_*$ and gap $\Theta_*$: $N$ is of order $t_*^{-1}$ up to a logarithmic factor, and $\delta=\Theta_*/(8eNt_*)$), have the following property. If $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ and $\tilde\lambda_0,\dots,\tilde\lambda_{N-1}\ge0$ satisfy $|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j(\Orb)|\le\delta$, where $\lambda_0\le\lambda_1\le\dots$ are all the eigenvalues of $\Orb$, counted with multiplicity, so that none of the first $N$ is omitted, then $\sigma(\Orb)$ is the unique signature $\sigma\in\Sig(A,M)$ that minimises $|\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt_*}-G_\sigma(t_*)|$, where $G_\sigma$ is the signature term of the trace formula (Section~\ref{sec:heat}) and $t_*$ the time of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}. In particular two orbifolds of $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ whose first $N$ eigenvalues agree to within $\delta$ have the same signature. Moreover, if $A/2\pi$ is rational and $\varepsilon$ is computable, then at every rational time $t<t_*$ a certified computation of the gaps between the signature terms over $\Sig(A,M)$ terminates and yields numbers $N$ and $\delta$ with the same property at $t$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:computable}).
\end{theorem}

\emph{What is new.} Given the heat-invariant results of the companion paper \cite{companionA}, the paper adds three things. (i) The passage from finitely many eigenvalues with explicit errors to the signature: a tail bound read off the heat trace (Proposition~\ref{prop:counttail}), a minimum-distance decision rule, and a lower bound for the gap between signature terms through the integrality of the first heat invariant at which two signatures differ (Lemmas~\ref{lem:int} and~\ref{lem:gap}); it gives $N$ and $\delta$ in closed form (Theorem~\ref{thm:E}) and by a certified procedure that always terminates (Theorem~\ref{thm:computable}). (ii) Certified instances of moderate size (Proposition~\ref{prop:certgap}); its hypothesis, a complete enclosed list of the first $N$ eigenvalues, is not met by our computed spectra. (iii) The necessity of the bound on the orders (Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-cusp}, Corollary~\ref{cor:Nlower}) and of the bound on the area (Proposition~\ref{prop:area}). \emph{What effectivity adds.} Without constants the statement follows from compactness: for two signatures in $\Sig(A,M)$ the orbifolds of each with systole at least $\varepsilon$ form compact sets, by Mumford's theorem \cite[Cor.~3]{mumford1971} and Bers's extension to Fuchsian groups with elliptic elements \cite{bers1972}; each $\lambda_j$ is continuous there (a $K$-bi-Lipschitz orbifold map changes each $\lambda_j$ by a factor in $[K^{-4},K^4]$, by the min-max principle); and different signatures have different spectra \cite{drydenstrohmaier2009}, \cite[Thm~1]{doylerossetti2011}. So some $N$ and $\delta$ exist, but the argument gives no value. Theorem~\ref{thm:E} gives both as formulas. They are enormous (Table~\ref{tab:E}), because the gap between signature terms is bounded through heat invariants, valid only at very small times, and with worst cases over the class (Remark~\ref{rem:sharper}); with the gap computed over $\Sig(A,M)$ at a moderate time and certified in ball arithmetic, $N$ of order $10^2$ to $10^4$, with accuracies of about $6\times10^{-4}$ to $2.5\times10^{-6}$, suffice for the classes of our computed examples (Proposition~\ref{prop:certgap}).

The same mechanism, run at one moderate time on the data, gives an a-posteriori test (Theorem~\ref{thm:post}): given an upper bound for the area, a lower bound for the systole, a count $Ce^x$ of the closed geodesics, a finite set $\mathcal S$ of candidate signatures and approximations $\tilde\lambda_0,\dots,\tilde\lambda_N$ of the first $N+1$ eigenvalues, none omitted, with errors at most $\epsilon_j$, an explicit error budget $E_N(t)$ identifies $\sigma(\Orb)$ when exactly one $\sigma\in\mathcal S$ satisfies $|\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}-G_\sigma(t)|\le E_N(t)$. We ran it on computed spectra of $\Orb(2,8,8)$, $\Orb(3,3,12)$ and eight orbifolds of signature $(0;3,3,3,3)$ (Section~\ref{sec:practice}), with certified systole bounds and estimated eigenvalue errors: with the class-level count of Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}, criterion (C1) holds for all ten with $N=24$ to $842$, where Theorem~\ref{thm:E} asks for $4.32\times10^4$ to $7.44\times10^{12}$; with a diameter bound for the instance the counts are $18$ to $847$ (Table~\ref{tab:practice}).


Finite spectral data are limited in three ways. Neither $A$ nor $M$ is read off finitely many eigenvalues (Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-cusp}, Proposition~\ref{prop:area}); for $\varepsilon$ we do not know (Problem~\ref{prob:systole}). The accuracies are absolute and, in Theorem~\ref{thm:E}, far below any measurement. And the data must contain every eigenvalue up to the last one used: an omitted eigenvalue $\lambda$ lowers $\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}$ by $e^{-\lambda t}$, which no error budget here covers, and in our experiments a single omission or duplication can make the test accept a wrong signature (Section~\ref{sec:practice}).

The proof runs through the heat trace. By the Selberg trace formula the heat trace of $\Orb$ is a term $G_\sigma(t)$ fixed by the signature plus a positive contribution of the closed geodesics (Theorem~\ref{thm:trace}); the small-$t$ expansion of $G_\sigma$ has coefficients, the heat invariants, that are integer combinations of explicit rationals. At the first heat invariant where two signatures differ, the difference is an integer multiple of an explicit rational number (Lemma~\ref{lem:int}), and with orders at most $M$ that invariant is the $M$-th one at the latest (Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded}). Explicit, enveloping remainders for both expansions (Proposition~\ref{prop:remcone}) turn this into a lower bound for $|G_\sigma(t)-G_{\sigma'}(t)|$ at a small explicit time; a count of the closed geodesics in terms of $A$, $\varepsilon$, $M$ alone (Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}), from the separation of the cone points and a packing argument, controls them there, and the counting function controls the eigenvalues beyond the $N$-th.

\begin{theorem}[High orders behave like cusps]\label{thm:intro-cusp}
The triangle orbifolds $\Orb(2,3,m)$, $m\ge7$, have area less than $\frac\pi3$ and systole at least $\ell_{2,3}=0.56206\ldots$ (defined in Proposition~\ref{prop:233}), and $\lambda_j(\Orb(2,3,m))\le\frac14+\pi^2(j+1)^2/h_m^2$ for $j\ge1$, with $h_m=\operatorname{arccosh}\frac1{2\sin(\pi/m)}\ge\log\frac m{2\pi}$. Consequently, for $A\ge\frac\pi3$ and $\varepsilon\le\ell_{2,3}$, there are no $N$ and $\delta$, depending only on $A$ and $\varepsilon$, such that any two orbifolds of area at most $A$ and systole at least $\varepsilon$ whose first $N$ eigenvalues agree to within $\delta$ have the same signature.
\end{theorem}

The cone point of order $m$ sits at the tip of a cone of angle $2\pi/m$ and radius $h_m$, which for large $m$ looks like the end of a cusp, and the eigenvalues below any fixed level above $\frac14$ multiply (Figure~\ref{fig:E1}). The area bound is needed as well: closed hyperbolic surfaces of systole at least $\varepsilon$ occur in infinitely many genera, and Cheng's comparison theorem \cite[Thm~2.1]{cheng1975} with pigeonhole gives two of different genus whose first $N$ eigenvalues agree to within $\delta$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:area}).

\emph{Relation to prior work.} The closest result is the theorem of Buser and Courtois \cite[(1.1)]{busercourtois1990}: among closed hyperbolic surfaces of genus $g\ge2$ with injectivity radius at least $\varepsilon$ there is an integer $m(g,\varepsilon)$ such that two surfaces with the same first $m$ eigenvalues are isospectral; the proof uses compactness and gives no value of $m$, and the authors conjecture that $m$ depends only on $g$ \cite[pp.~523--524]{busercourtois1990}. Theorem~\ref{thm:intro} is an effective counterpart at the level of the signature, for orbifolds and approximate eigenvalues, and Problem~\ref{prob:systole} is a loose analogue of their conjecture. Isospectral sets of closed hyperbolic surfaces and orbisurfaces are finite \cite{mckean1972,dryden2004}, isospectral metrics on a surface form a compact set \cite{ops1988}, isospectral manifolds under geometric bounds have finitely many diffeomorphism types \cite{bpp1992}, and for orbifolds the spectrum with a lower curvature bound controls the isotropy types, the singular points and the diffeomorphism types \cite{stanhope2005,proctorstanhope2010}, but not always the maximal isotropy order \cite{rsw2008}; Theorem~\ref{thm:diam} bounds the diameter in terms of the area, the systole and the orders instead of the spectrum. Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-cusp} is an instance of elliptic degeneration, in which cone points of growing order become cusps: Garbin and Jorgenson prove that along such sequences the eigenvalues below $\frac14$ converge to those of the cusped limit and that the number below a fixed level above $\frac14$ grows without bound \cite[Cor.~5.5, Thm~5.7]{garbinjorgenson2018}, \cite[Thm~6.5]{garbinjorgenson2020}; for the Hecke triangle groups this accumulation is attributed to Selberg \cite[p.~579]{hejhal1983}, and Judge proved monotonicity in the cone angles \cite{judge1995}. What Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-cusp} adds is a systole bound uniform in $m$, with an explicit constant (Proposition~\ref{prop:233}), and a lower bound for $N$ in terms of $M$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:Nlower}). Under pinching of geodesics, small eigenvalues converge to those of the cusped limit and eigenvalues cluster in $[\frac14,\infty)$ \cite{hejhal1990,ji1993,wolpert1992a,wolpert1992b}, the background of Section~\ref{sec:systole}. Computed spectra have been checked against the Selberg trace formula before \cite[\S7]{strohmaieruski2013}, \cite{bsv2006}, and our completeness check follows Strohmaier and Uski. Linear programming and bootstrap methods bound eigenvalues given the topology \cite{fortierbourquepetri2023,bonifacio2022,kmp2024}; here finitely many eigenvalues bound the topology. Effective rigidity of other kinds is known for the length sets of arithmetic surfaces \cite{lmpt2018} and for the prime geodesic theorem \cite{fjk2011}, and Buser bounds the number of closed geodesics in terms of the genus \cite[Lemma~6.6.4]{buser1992}, while Lemma~\ref{lem:countK} counts them in terms of the area, the systole and the orders. For Euclidean triangles, finitely many Dirichlet eigenvalues determine the triangle \cite[Thm~1]{changdeturck1989}.

\section{The heat trace and its expansion}\label{sec:heat}

This section and the next state the results shared with the companion paper (Table~\ref{tab:shared}), where they are proved; Lemmas~\ref{lem:counting} and~\ref{lem:sigdata} are also proved here.

\subsection{Signatures and the trace formula}

Let $\Orb\in\Sig$ have signature $\sigma=(g;m_1,\dots,m_n)$, $m_i\ge2$. It is hyperbolic exactly when $s(\sigma)=2g-2+\sum_i(1-\frac1{m_i})>0$, and then $\Area(\Orb)=2\pi s(\sigma)$ \cite[13.3.4--13.3.6]{thurston1980}, \cite[Thm~3.2]{drydenstrohmaier2009}. Write $\Orb=\Gamma\backslash\Hb$ with $\Gamma\subset PSL(2,\R)$ cocompact, so $\Gamma$ has no parabolic elements. Put $\hat g_t(r)=e^{-t(1/4+r^2)}$ and $g_t(u)=e^{-t/4}e^{-u^2/4t}/\sqrt{4\pi t}$, so that $\hat g_t(r)=\int g_t(u)e^{iru}du$. For hyperbolic $\gamma\in\Gamma$ let $\ell(\gamma)$ be its translation length and $\gamma=\gamma_0^k$ with $\gamma_0$ primitive; a \emph{closed geodesic} is a hyperbolic conjugacy class, and the systole $\ell(\Orb)$ is the least $\ell(\gamma)$. $\cZ_\Orb(t)=\sum_je^{-\lambda_jt}$ is the heat trace.

\begin{theorem}[Trace formula for the heat function; classical, {\cite[Thm~\ref*{A-thm:IEH}]{companionA}}]\label{thm:trace}
For every $t>0$, $\cZ_\Orb(t)=\mathrm I(t)+\sum_i\mathrm E_{m_i}(t)+\Hyp(t)$, all series and integrals converging absolutely, where
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm I(t)&=\frac{\Area(\Orb)}{4\pi}\int_\R r\tanh(\pi r)\,\hat g_t(r)\,dr,\qquad
  \Hyp(t)=\sum_{[\gamma]}\frac{\ell(\gamma_0)\,g_t(\ell(\gamma))}{2\sinh(\ell(\gamma)/2)}>0,\\
  \mathrm E_m(t)&=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac1{2m\sin(\pi j/m)}\int_\R\frac{e^{-2\pi jr/m}\,\hat g_t(r)}{1+e^{-2\pi r}}\,dr .
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

This is the Selberg trace formula for cocompact Fuchsian groups with elliptic elements \cite[eq.~(1)]{drydenstrohmaier2009}, after \cite{hejhal1976,iwaniec2002}, extended to the heat function by a standard approximation argument \cite[Lemma~\ref*{A-lem:admissible}]{companionA}; Garbin and Jorgenson state it for the heat function directly \cite[Rem.~2.7, (2.8)]{garbinjorgenson2020}. Each nontrivial element of the stabiliser of a cone point is counted once, and $\mathrm E_m(t)\to b_0(m)=\frac{m^2-1}{12m}$ as $t\downarrow0$. For a signature $\sigma$ put $G_\sigma(t)=\mathrm I(t)+\sum_i\mathrm E_{m_i}(t)$, the part of $\cZ_\Orb$ fixed by $\sigma$.

\begin{lemma}[Counting closed geodesics and the hyperbolic term]\label{lem:counting}\label{lem:hyp}
Let $n_\Orb(x)$ be the number of closed geodesics of $\Orb$ (hyperbolic conjugacy classes, not necessarily primitive; a class and its inverse are counted separately unless they coincide, as they can when $\Gamma$ has elements of order $2$) of length at most $x$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $n_\Orb(x)\le\pi e^{x+3\diam}/\Area(\Orb)$, with $\diam$ the diameter of $\Orb$.
\item Suppose $n_\Orb(x)\le Ce^x$ for all $x>0$, and let $\ell>0$ be at most the systole. For $0<t\le\ell^2/(2(1+\ell))$,
\[
  0<\Hyp(t)\le B_C(\ell,t):=\frac{C\,\ell e^{\ell/2}}{1-e^{-\ell}}\Big(1+\frac{2t}{\ell-t}\Big)\frac{e^{-\ell^2/4t}}{\sqrt{4\pi t}},
\]
and, for fixed $t$, $B_C$ decreases with $\ell$ on the set of $\ell$ with $t\le\ell^2/(2(1+\ell))$. For every $t>0$, $0<\Hyp(t)\le\frac{2C}{\sqrt\pi(1-e^{-\ell})}\,e^{\frac{17t}4-\frac12-\ell}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Let $F$ be the Dirichlet domain at a point $\tilde x_0$ fixed by no nontrivial element; $F\subset\bar B(\tilde x_0,\diam)$. Every hyperbolic class has a representative $\gamma$ whose axis meets $F$, at $\tilde y$ say, and $d(\tilde x_0,\gamma\tilde x_0)\le2d(\tilde x_0,\tilde y)+d(\tilde y,\gamma\tilde y)\le\ell(\gamma)+2\diam$. The translates $\gamma F$ with $d(\tilde x_0,\gamma\tilde x_0)\le x+2\diam$ have disjoint interiors, area $\Area(\Orb)$ each, and lie in $B(\tilde x_0,x+3\diam)$, of area $2\pi(\cosh(x+3\diam)-1)\le\pi e^{x+3\diam}$.
(ii) With $\ell(\gamma_0)\le\ell(\gamma)$, $e^{-t/4}\le1$ and $2\sinh(x/2)\ge e^{x/2}(1-e^{-\ell})$ for $x\ge\ell$, $\Hyp(t)\le((1-e^{-\ell})\sqrt{4\pi t})^{-1}\int_{[\ell,\infty)}\phi\,dn_\Orb$ with $\phi(x)=xe^{-x/2-x^2/4t}$, and $n_\Orb=0$ below $\ell$. On the range, $(\log\phi)'=\frac1x-\frac12-\frac x{2t}<0$ for $x\ge\ell$, so integrating by parts, $\int\phi\,dn_\Orb=\int_\ell^\infty n_\Orb(-\phi')\,dx\le C\int_\ell^\infty e^x(-\phi')\,dx=C\big(e^\ell\phi(\ell)+\int_\ell^\infty xe^{x/2-x^2/4t}dx\big)$. Since $xe^{x/2-x^2/4t}=-\frac{2tx}{x-t}\frac d{dx}e^{x/2-x^2/4t}$ and $\frac x{x-t}\le\frac\ell{\ell-t}$ for $x\ge\ell$, the integral is at most $\frac{2t\ell}{\ell-t}e^{\ell/2-\ell^2/4t}$, which gives $B_C$. The logarithmic derivative of $B_C$ in $\ell$ is $-\frac12-(\frac\ell{2t}-\frac{1+\ell}\ell)-\frac{e^{-\ell}}{1-e^{-\ell}}-\frac{2t}{\ell^2-t^2}<0$, the bracket being nonnegative exactly on the range. For every $t>0$, $xe^{-x/2-x^2/4t}=(xe^{-x^2/8t})(e^{3x/2-x^2/8t})e^{-2x}\le2\sqrt t\,e^{-1/2}e^{9t/2}e^{-2x}$ for $x\ge0$ (maxima at $x=2\sqrt t$ and $x=6t$), so $\Hyp(t)\le\frac{2\sqrt t\,e^{-1/2}e^{9t/2}e^{-t/4}}{(1-e^{-\ell})\sqrt{4\pi t}}\sum_{[\gamma]}e^{-2\ell(\gamma)}$, and $\sum_{[\gamma]}e^{-2\ell(\gamma)}=2\int_\ell^\infty n_\Orb(x)e^{-2x}dx\le2Ce^{-\ell}$.
\end{proof}

The range $t\le\ell^2/(2(1+\ell))$ in (ii) is what the monotonicity of $B_C$ in $\ell$ needs; the bound itself holds whenever $t<\ell$ and $t(2-\ell)<\ell^2$, which is what the proof uses ($(\log\phi)'<0$ for $x\ge\ell$, and $\ell>t$). Part (i) with $C=\pi e^{3\diam}/\Area(\Orb)$ is the count used when a diameter bound for the instance is known (Section~\ref{sec:practice}); Lemma~\ref{lem:countK} gives a count in terms of the area, the systole and the orders alone, which is the one Theorem~\ref{thm:E} uses.

\subsection{The cone and area terms}

Write $\theta_j=\pi j/m$ and, for an integer $m\ge1$, $\Phi_m(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(4m\sin\theta_j\sin(\theta_j-u))^{-1}=\sum_k\phi_k(m)u^{2k}$, analytic and even in $|u|<\pi/m$. Every $\phi_k(m)$ is a positive combination of the numbers $m^{2n-1}-m^{-1}$, $1\le n\le k+1$, hence positive and strictly increasing in $m>1$ \cite[Lemma~\ref*{A-lem:Phi}]{companionA}. Put
\[
  b_l(m)=\frac{(-1)^lp_l(m)}m,\quad p_l(m)=\frac1{4^l}\sum_{k=0}^l\frac{(2k)!}{k!\,(l-k)!}\,m\,\phi_k(m),
\]
\[
  \alpha_k=\frac{(-1)^k}{k!\,4^k}\sum_{l=0}^k\binom kl(-4)^lB_{2l}\big(\tfrac12\big),
\]
with $B_{2l}(x)$ the Bernoulli polynomials; $\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_3=1,-\frac13,\frac1{15},-\frac4{315}$.

\begin{proposition}[Enveloping remainders {\cite[Prop.~\ref*{A-prop:envelope}]{companionA}}]\label{prop:remcone}\label{prop:remarea}
Let $K\ge0$ and $t>0$. (a) For every integer $m\ge2$, $\mathrm E_m(t)-\sum_{l<K}b_l(m)t^l$ has the sign $(-1)^K$, and its modulus is at most $|b_K(m)|\,t^K$; and $|b_K(m)|$ is strictly increasing in $m$. (b) $\frac{4\pi}{\Area}\mathrm I(t)-\sum_{k=0}^K\alpha_kt^{k-1}$ has the sign $(-1)^{K+1}$, and its modulus is at most $|\alpha_{K+1}|\,t^K$.
\end{proposition}

So $\cZ_\Orb(t)\sim\sum_{j\ge1}c_j(\Orb)t^{j-2}$ as $t\downarrow0$, with the \emph{heat invariants}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cj}
  c_1=\frac{\Area}{4\pi},\qquad c_j=\alpha_{j-1}\frac{\Area}{4\pi}+\sum_{i=1}^nb_{j-2}(m_i)\quad(j\ge2),
\end{equation}
functions $c_j(\sigma)$ of the signature, and for $K\ge0$ and every $t>0$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Grem}
  \Big|G_\sigma(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{K+1}c_j(\sigma)\,t^{j-2}\Big|\le t^K\Big(\frac{\Area}{4\pi}|\alpha_{K+1}|+\sum_i|b_K(m_i)|\Big).
\end{equation}
The polynomial $p_l$ is even, with rational coefficients, of degree exactly $2l+2$, with $p_l(1)=0$ and leading coefficient $a_l=|B_{2l+2}|/(2(l+1)!\,(2l+1))$; writing $p_l(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{l+1}a_{l,k}x^{2k}$, $p_l(x)/x=\sum_{k=1}^{l+1}a_{l,k}\psi_k(x)$ with $\psi_k(x)=x^{2k-1}-x^{-1}$ and $a_{l,l+1}=a_l$ \cite[Lemmas~\ref*{A-lem:conepoly} and~\ref*{A-lem:triangular}]{companionA}. This triangular structure is the mechanism of U\c{c}ar's induction for polygons \cite[Thm~3.40]{ucar2017}, which gives the orbisurface statement \cite[Cor.~4.21(iv)]{ucar2017}.

\emph{Relation to U\c{c}ar's formulas.} The coefficients in \eqref{eq:cj} coincide with U\c{c}ar's closed forms at curvature $-1$ \cite[Thm~4.20(ii), (4.25), (4.33)--(4.35)]{ucar2017}, both being coefficients of the small-$t$ expansion of the same heat trace; we have also checked the agreement in exact rational arithmetic for $l\le8$. Not in \cite{ucar2017} are the remainders of Proposition~\ref{prop:remcone}, valid for every $t>0$ with a definite sign, and the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:sep}(i), both from the companion paper, and Lemma~\ref{lem:int}, which is new here.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:remcone} the area remainder in \eqref{eq:Grem} and the cone remainders have opposite signs, so \eqref{eq:Grem} also holds with the sum on its right-hand side replaced by $t^K\max\{\frac{\Area}{4\pi}|\alpha_{K+1}|,\sum_i|b_K(m_i)|\}$; this would improve the constant $Q(K)$ of Section~\ref{sec:E} by at most a factor $2$, and we keep the sum.

\section{Heat invariants of a signature}\label{sec:sig}

Write $R=\sum_i1/m_i$, $P_k=\sum_im_i^k$, $\Psi_k(m)=\sum_i\psi_k(m_i)$ and $H_L(\sigma)=(c_1,\dots,c_L)$.

\begin{lemma}[Heat data of a signature {\cite[Lemma~\ref*{A-lem:sigdata}]{companionA}}]\label{lem:sigdata}
Let $\sigma=(g;m)$, $\sigma'=(g';m')$ be hyperbolic, with $n$ and $n'$ cone points, and $L\ge1$. Then $H_L(\sigma)=H_L(\sigma')$ if and only if $2g+n-R(m)=2g'+n'-R(m')$ and $\Psi_k(m)=\Psi_k(m')$ for $1\le k\le L-1$. In that case $d=R(m')-R(m)\in\Z$, and the paddings $U=m\uplus\{1\}^{\max(d,0)}$, $V=m'\uplus\{1\}^{\max(-d,0)}$ satisfy $R(U)=R(V)$ and $P_j(U)=P_j(V)$ for odd $j\le2L-3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $C_l(m)=\sum_ib_l(m_i)$; by the triangular expansion of $p_l(x)/x$ in the $\psi_k$, $C_l(m)=C_l(m')$ for $0\le l\le L-2$ if and only if $\Psi_k(m)=\Psi_k(m')$ for $1\le k\le L-1$. $c_1$ agrees exactly when the areas do, that is, when $2g+n-R(m)=2g'+n'-R(m')$; then the terms $\alpha_{j-1}\Area/4\pi$ of \eqref{eq:cj} agree, and $c_2,\dots,c_L$ agree exactly when $C_0,\dots,C_{L-2}$ do, that is, when $\Psi_k(m)=\Psi_k(m')$ for $k\le L-1$. An order $1$ changes neither $n-R$ nor any $\Psi_k$, as $\psi_k(1)=0$; so the paddings have $R(U)=R(V)$, by the area equation and $d\in\Z$, and $\Psi_k(U)=\Psi_k(V)$, hence $P_{2k-1}(U)=P_{2k-1}(V)$ for $k\le L-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Separation and bounded orders {\cite[Cor.~\ref*{A-cor:sigarea}, Thm~\ref*{A-thm:bounded}(i)]{companionA}}]\label{thm:sep}\label{thm:bounded}
(i) Two hyperbolic signatures of area $A$ that share $c_1,\dots,c_L$ with $L\ge\lfloor A/\pi\rfloor+4$ are equal. (ii) Two hyperbolic signatures with all cone orders at most $M$ that share $c_1,\dots,c_M$ are equal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Integrality at the first difference]\label{lem:int}
Let $\sigma\ne\sigma'$ be hyperbolic signatures with orders at most $M$, $k$ the least index with $c_k(\sigma)\ne c_k(\sigma')$, and $d_k=c_k(\sigma)-c_k(\sigma')$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If the areas differ, $k=1$ and $|d_1|\ge1/(2L_{\sigma\sigma'})$, where $L_{\sigma\sigma'}$ is the least common multiple of the orders of $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ ($L_{\sigma\sigma'}=1$ if neither has a cone point).
\item If the areas are equal, to $A$ say, then $2\le k\le\min(\lfloor A/\pi\rfloor+4,M)$ and $d_k=(-1)^ka_{k-2}(P_{2k-3}(U)-P_{2k-3}(V))$, with $U,V$ the paddings of Lemma~\ref{lem:sigdata} for $L=k-1$; the difference of power sums is a nonzero integer, so $|d_k|\ge a_{k-2}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) $c_1=\frac12(2g-2+n-R)$ and $R\in\frac1{L_{\sigma\sigma'}}\Z$. (ii) The bound on $k$ is Theorem~\ref{thm:sep}. Put $l=k-2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:sigdata} with $L=k-1$, $R(U)=R(V)$ and $\Psi_j(U)=\Psi_j(V)$ for $j\le l$, padding by $1$ changing no $C_l=\sum_ib_l(m_i)$ as $p_l(1)=0$. The terms $\alpha_{k-1}A/4\pi$ cancel, and with the triangular expansion of $p_l(x)/x$ in the $\psi_k$ (Section~\ref{sec:heat}), $d_k=(-1)^l\sum_{j\le l+1}a_{l,j}(\Psi_j(U)-\Psi_j(V))=(-1)^la_l(P_{2l+1}(U)-P_{2l+1}(V))$, using $R(U)=R(V)$; this is nonzero as $d_k\ne0$.
\end{proof}

For $k=2$ the bound $|d_k|\ge a_{k-2}$ is attained, for instance by $(0;4,4,4)$ and $(0;3,4,6)$, of equal area, with $d_2=-\frac1{12}$.

\section{Separation of cone points and the count of closed geodesics}\label{sec:geom}

The results used later are the separation of cone points (Lemma~\ref{lem:sep}), the area of balls (Lemma~\ref{lem:balls}) and the count of closed geodesics without the diameter (Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}). The diameter bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:diam}) is used in no proof of a main result; it is kept as an explicit bound and for comparison.

\subsection{Three facts of plane hyperbolic geometry}

We use the disc model, with $\cosh d(z,w)=1+2|z-w|^2/((1-|z|^2)(1-|w|^2))$, the upper half-plane, with $\cosh d(z,w)=1+|z-w|^2/(2\operatorname{Im}z\operatorname{Im}w)$, and the hyperboloid $\{x\in\R^{1,2}:\langle x,x\rangle=-1,\ x_0>0\}$ with $\langle x,y\rangle=-x_0y_0+x_1y_1+x_2y_2$, in which lines are the intersections with planes $n^\perp$, $n$ a unit spacelike normal \cite{beardon1983}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:H}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(H\arabic*)]
\item A rotation by $\phi$ about $q$ moves a point at distance $r$ from $q$ by $d$ with $\sinh\frac d2=\sinh r\,|\sin\frac\phi2|$. A hyperbolic $\gamma$ moves a point at distance $r$ from its axis by $d$ with $\sinh\frac d2=\sinh\frac{\ell(\gamma)}2\cosh r$; in particular by at least $\ell(\gamma)$.
\item The product of the reflections in two distinct lines is a rotation by twice their angle about their intersection if they meet, a translation by twice their distance along their common perpendicular if they are ultraparallel, and is parabolic, fixing their common ideal point, if they are asymptotic (in the upper half-plane with that point at $\infty$ the two reflections are $x\mapsto2c_i-\bar x$, and their product is a translation).
\item Let $\lambda$ be the line through $p\ne q$, $d=d(p,q)$, $0<\alpha,\beta\le\frac\pi2$, and $L_1\ni p$, $L_3\ni q$ the lines making the angles $\alpha$, $\beta$ with the segment $[p,q]$, on the same side of $\lambda$. Put $X=\sin\alpha\sin\beta\cosh d-\cos\alpha\cos\beta$. If $X<1$, $L_1$ and $L_3$ meet, on that side, in a point $w$, and the triangle $pqw$ has angles $\alpha,\beta,\theta$ with $\cos\theta=X$; if $X>1$ they are ultraparallel at distance $h$ with $\cosh h=X$; if $X=1$ they are asymptotic.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(H1) In the disc, rotate about $0$ a point $z$ with $|z|=\tanh\frac r2$: $|z-e^{i\phi}z|=2\tanh\frac r2|\sin\frac\phi2|$ and $1-|z|^2=\cosh^{-2}\frac r2$, so $\cosh d-1=8\sinh^2\frac r2\cosh^2\frac r2\sin^2\frac\phi2=2\sinh^2r\sin^2\frac\phi2$, and $\cosh d-1=2\sinh^2\frac d2$. In the half-plane let $\gamma(z)=e^Lz$, with axis $i\R_{>0}$; a point $z$ at distance $r$ from the axis has $|z|/\operatorname{Im}z=\cosh r$ (the distance to the axis is attained along the circle $|w|=|z|$), and $\cosh d(z,e^Lz)-1=(e^L-1)^2|z|^2/(2e^L(\operatorname{Im}z)^2)=2\sinh^2\frac L2\cosh^2r$.
(H2) Let $s_1,s_2$ be the reflections. If the lines meet at $p$ at the angle $\theta$, $s_2s_1$ fixes $p$, preserves orientation, and its differential at $p$ is the product of the linear reflections in two lines at the angle $\theta$, a rotation by $2\theta$; an isometry is determined by its value and differential at one point. If they are ultraparallel with common perpendicular $\mu$, meeting them at $q_1,q_2$, both reflections preserve $\mu$ and each side of it, and act on $\mu$ as the reflections in $q_1,q_2$; so $s_2s_1$ preserves the sides of $\mu$ and translates $\mu$ by $2d(q_1,q_2)$, which determines it as that translation. If they are asymptotic, $s_2s_1$ fixes their common ideal point. If $s_2s_1z=z$ for some $z\in\Hb$, then $s_1z=s_2z$; were this point different from $z$, both lines would be the perpendicular bisector of the segment from $z$ to it, so $z$ lies on both lines, which do not meet. So $s_2s_1$ has no fixed point in $\Hb$.
(H3) For two lines with unit normals $n,n'$: if they meet at $w$, then $n,n'\in T_w=w^\perp$, a Euclidean plane, are normal to the lines there, so $|\langle n,n'\rangle|=\cos\theta'<1$ with $\theta'$ the acute angle between them, and if they are oriented outwards from a convex region with a vertex at $w$ of interior angle $\theta$, $\langle n,n'\rangle=\cos(\pi-\theta)=-\cos\theta$. If they are ultraparallel with common perpendicular $\mu(s)=\cosh s\,x+\sinh s\,v$ meeting them at $s=0,h$, then $n,n'$ are $\pm\mu'(0)$, $\pm\mu'(h)$, and $|\langle\mu'(0),\mu'(h)\rangle|=\cosh h>1$. So the asymptotic case is $|\langle n,n'\rangle|=1$. Now take $p=(1,0,0)$, $\lambda=\{x_2=0\}$, $q=(\cosh d,\sinh d,0)$, the triangle on the side $x_2>0$, and $u_q=(\sinh d,\cosh d,0)$ the unit tangent of $\lambda$ at $q$. Outward unit normals (away from the side $x_2>0$ and from $q$, respectively $p$) of $\lambda$, $L_1$, $L_3$ are $-e_2$, $n_1=-\sin\alpha\,e_1+\cos\alpha\,e_2$, $n_3=\sin\beta\,u_q+\cos\beta\,e_2$; indeed $\langle-e_2,n_1\rangle=-\cos\alpha$ and $\langle-e_2,n_3\rangle=-\cos\beta$, as the rule above requires at $p$ and $q$. Then $\langle n_1,n_3\rangle=-\sin\alpha\sin\beta\cosh d+\cos\alpha\cos\beta=-X$, and $X\ge-\cos(\alpha+\beta)>-1$, since $0<\alpha+\beta\le\pi$. So if $X<1$ the lines meet; not on the side $x_2<0$, where the triangle would have angles $\pi-\alpha$ and $\pi-\beta$ summing to at least $\pi$; so on the side $x_2>0$, with $-\cos\theta=\langle n_1,n_3\rangle=-X$. The other two cases follow from $|\langle n_1,n_3\rangle|=|X|$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The diameter bound}

A point $\tilde x\in\Hb$ with nontrivial stabiliser $\Gamma_{\tilde x}$ is \emph{elliptic}; $\Gamma_{\tilde x}$ is cyclic of order $m$, generated by the rotation by $2\pi/m$ about $\tilde x$, and its image in $\Orb$ is a cone point of order $m$.

\begin{lemma}[Cone points are separated]\label{lem:sep}
For $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$, distinct elliptic points satisfy $d(\tilde p,\tilde q)\ge d_0(\varepsilon,M):=\min\{\frac\varepsilon2,\operatorname{arccosh}(1+\frac2{\pi^2M^2})\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $a,b\le M$ be the orders of $\Gamma_{\tilde p},\Gamma_{\tilde q}$, $\alpha=\pi/a$, $\beta=\pi/b$, $d=d(\tilde p,\tilde q)$, and $\lambda,L_1,L_3,X$ as in (H3), with reflections $s_\lambda,s_1,s_3$. By (H2) $s_1s_\lambda$ and $s_\lambda s_3$ are rotations about $\tilde p$, $\tilde q$ by $2\alpha$, $2\beta$, which lie in the cyclic stabilisers (both senses do), so $\gamma=s_1s_3=(s_1s_\lambda)(s_\lambda s_3)\in\Gamma$. As $\Gamma$ has no parabolic element and $\gamma\ne1$ ($L_1\ne L_3$, since the only line through $\tilde p$ and $\tilde q$ is $\lambda$, and $L_1\ne\lambda$ as $\alpha>0$), $X\ne1$ by (H2). If $X>1$, $\gamma$ is hyperbolic with $\ell(\gamma)=2h$ and $\cosh h=X\le\cosh d$, so $\varepsilon\le2d$. If $X<1$, $\gamma$ is a rotation by $2\theta$ about $w$, so $w$ is elliptic of some order $c\le M$ and $\theta=\pi k/c$, $1\le k<c$. By (H3)
\[
  \cosh d-1=\frac{\cos\theta+\cos(\alpha+\beta)}{\sin\alpha\sin\beta}=\frac{2\cos\frac{\theta+\alpha+\beta}2\cos\frac{\theta-\alpha-\beta}2}{\sin\alpha\sin\beta}.
\]
The defect $\pi-\alpha-\beta-\theta=\pi(abc-bc-ac-kab)/(abc)$ is positive with integer numerator, so at least $\pi/(abc)$, and $\cos\frac{\theta+\alpha+\beta}2\ge\frac1{abc}$ by $\sin x\ge\frac{2x}\pi$. As $\pi/M\le\theta$ and $\pi/M\le\alpha,\beta\le\frac\pi2$, $|\theta-\alpha-\beta|\le\pi-\frac\pi M$, so $\cos\frac{\theta-\alpha-\beta}2\ge\sin\frac\pi{2M}\ge\frac1M$; with $\sin\alpha\sin\beta\le\pi^2/(ab)$, $\cosh d-1\ge\frac2{\pi^2cM}\ge\frac2{\pi^2M^2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Balls have area bounded below]\label{lem:balls}
For $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$, $r_0=\frac12d_0(\varepsilon,M)$ and $\rho_1=\operatorname{arsinh}(\sinh r_0\sin\frac\pi M)$, every ball of radius $2r_0$ in $\Orb$ has area at least $v_0=2\pi\min\{(\cosh r_0-1)/M,\ \cosh\rho_1-1\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $d(\tilde y,\gamma\tilde y)\ge2r$ for every $\gamma\notin\Gamma_{\tilde y}$, the projection maps $\Gamma_{\tilde y}\backslash B(\tilde y,r)$ injectively onto the ball of radius $r$ about the image of $\tilde y$, of area $2\pi(\cosh r-1)/|\Gamma_{\tilde y}|$. Let $x\in\Orb$. If some cone point $p$ has $d(x,p)<r_0$, the ball of radius $2r_0$ about $x$ contains the ball of radius $r_0$ about $p$, and for $\gamma\notin\Gamma_{\tilde p}$, $\gamma\tilde p\ne\tilde p$ is elliptic, so $d(\tilde p,\gamma\tilde p)\ge2r_0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sep}). Otherwise $\Gamma_{\tilde x}=1$; a hyperbolic $\gamma$ moves $\tilde x$ by at least $\varepsilon\ge2\rho_1$ (H1), and an elliptic one is a rotation by $2\pi j/k$, $k\le M$, about a point at distance at least $r_0$, $1\le j<k$, and $\sin\frac{\pi j}k\ge\sin\frac\pi k\ge\sin\frac\pi M$, so it moves $\tilde x$ by at least $2\operatorname{arsinh}(\sinh r_0\sin\frac\pi M)=2\rho_1$ (H1); and $\rho_1\le r_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Diameter bound]\label{thm:diam}
Every $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ has $\diam\Orb<D(A,\varepsilon,M):=4r_0A/v_0\le\frac A\pi\max(4M,M^2)\max(\frac4\varepsilon,\frac{10M}3)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\diam\Orb=d(x,y)=\Delta$ and lift to $\tilde x,\tilde y$ with $d(\tilde x,\tilde y)=\Delta$. The image $c$ of the segment, by arclength, satisfies $d(c(s),c(s'))=|s-s'|$: otherwise $d(x,y)<\Delta$. The points $c(4r_0i)$, $0\le i\le\lfloor\Delta/4r_0\rfloor$, are $4r_0$ apart, so the balls of radius $2r_0$ about them are disjoint, and Lemma~\ref{lem:balls} gives $(\lfloor\Delta/4r_0\rfloor+1)v_0\le A$. For the closed form, $\cosh r-1\ge r^2/2$, $\cosh\rho_1-1\ge(\cosh r_0-1)\sin^2\frac\pi M$ and $\sin\frac\pi M\ge\frac2M$ give $v_0\ge\pi r_0^2/\max(M,M^2/4)$, and $\operatorname{arccosh}(1+x)\ge\sqrt{2x/(1+x)}$ gives $d_0\ge\min(\frac\varepsilon2,\frac{0.6}M)$.
\end{proof}

Table~\ref{tab:D} compares $D$ with computed diameters. The bound grows like $AM^3$, and like $A/\varepsilon$ when $\varepsilon/2<\operatorname{arccosh}(1+2/(\pi^2M^2))$, that is, when the systole term of $d_0$ binds. By Proposition~\ref{prop:233} below some growth in $M$ is necessary; the factor $1/M$ in $v_0$ is intrinsic for balls of radius $2r_0$ about a cone point, since on $\Orb(2,3,M)\in\Cl(\frac\pi3,\ell_{2,3},M)$ the ball of radius $2r_0=d_0<h_M$ about the cone point of order $M$ is an embedded cone of area $2\pi(\cosh2r_0-1)/M$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:233}); we expect the factor $1/M$ in $d_0$ not to be intrinsic, but do not prove it, and do not optimise it. Jørgensen's inequality \cite{jorgensen1976}, \cite[\S5.4]{beardon1983} points that way: for rotations $f,g$ by $\frac{2\pi}p$ and $\frac{2\pi}q$ about points at distance $d$, $\operatorname{tr}[f,g]-2=4\sin^2\frac\pi p\sin^2\frac\pi q\sinh^2d$, so if they generate a non-elementary discrete group, $4\sin^2\frac\pi p\,(1+\sin^2\frac\pi q\sinh^2d)\ge1$, a lower bound for $d$ that grows with the orders once $p\ge7$. It is attained in every $(2,3,p)$ triangle group with $p\ge7$, by the rotations of orders $p$ and $2$ about the vertices $v_p$ and $v_2$ of the triangle, at distance $h_p$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:233}): with $q=2$ and $\cosh h_p=1/(2\sin\frac\pi p)$, $4\sin^2\frac\pi p\,(1+\sinh^2h_p)=4\sin^2\frac\pi p\cosh^2h_p=1$; embedded collars and cone neighbourhoods for cone angles below $\pi$ are in \cite{dryden2007collars}. We have not pursued an order-free separation from it, because $C_*$ enters Theorem~\ref{thm:E} only through $\log C_*$ in $t_3$, and $t_1$ binds in all but one row of Table~\ref{tab:E}; it enters the test of Section~\ref{sec:practice} linearly, through the bound $\mathcal H$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:post} when $C=C_*$, and there a better separation could only lower the counts with $C_*$ of Table~\ref{tab:practice}. Theorem~\ref{thm:diam} gives a count of closed geodesics through Lemma~\ref{lem:counting}(i), with the factor $e^{3D}$. The same lemmas give a count without the diameter.

\begin{lemma}[Counting closed geodesics without the diameter]\label{lem:countK}
Every $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ has $n_\Orb(x)\le C_*\,e^x$ for all $x>0$, with $r_0,\rho_1,v_0$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:balls} and
\[
  C_*=C_*(A,\varepsilon,M)=\frac{A\,M\,e^{10r_0+\rho_1}}{2\,v_0\,(\cosh\rho_1-1)} .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $Y\subset\Orb$ be a maximal set of points at pairwise distance at least $4r_0$. The balls of radius $2r_0$ about the points of $Y$ are disjoint, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:balls} each has area at least $v_0$, so $|Y|\le A/v_0$; by maximality every point of $\Orb$ is within $4r_0$ of $Y$. For $y\in Y$ let $b(y)$ be a cone point at distance less than $r_0$ from $y$ if there is one, and $y$ otherwise; every point of $\Orb$ is within $5r_0$ of some $b(y)$. Fix lifts $\tilde b$. For $\gamma\notin\Gamma_{\tilde b}$, $d(\tilde b,\gamma\tilde b)\ge2\rho_1$: if $b$ is a cone point, $\gamma\tilde b$ is another elliptic point, at distance at least $d_0=2r_0\ge2\rho_1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sep}); otherwise $\Gamma_{\tilde b}=1$, a hyperbolic $\gamma$ moves $\tilde b$ by at least $\varepsilon\ge2d_0\ge2\rho_1$ and an elliptic one by at least $2\rho_1$, as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:balls}. Hence the orbit points $\gamma\tilde b$ of distinct cosets of $\Gamma_{\tilde b}$ are at least $2\rho_1$ apart, the balls of radius $\rho_1$ about them are disjoint, and as $|\Gamma_{\tilde b}|\le M$,
\[
  \#\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\ d(\tilde b,\gamma\tilde b)\le R\}\le M\,\frac{\cosh(R+\rho_1)-1}{\cosh\rho_1-1}.
\]
A closed geodesic of length at most $x$ passes through a point $z$ within $5r_0$ of some $b=b(y)$; a lift $\tilde z$ with $d(\tilde z,\tilde b)\le5r_0$ lies on the axis of some $\gamma$ in its class, and $d(\tilde b,\gamma\tilde b)\le2d(\tilde b,\tilde z)+\ell(\gamma)\le x+10r_0$. Counting such $\gamma$ at every base point, $n_\Orb(x)\le\frac A{v_0}\,M\,\frac{\cosh(x+10r_0+\rho_1)-1}{\cosh\rho_1-1}\le C_*e^x$.
\end{proof}

For the classes of Table~\ref{tab:E}, $C_*$ lies between $4\times10^5$ and $7\times10^{10}$, against $\pi e^{3D}/A$ between about $3.4\times10^{196}$ and $2.4\times10^{29325}$ with $D$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:diam}; Theorem~\ref{thm:E} uses $C_*$, and the diameter bound is not needed there. Theorem~\ref{thm:diam} is used in no proof of a main result: the finiteness statement of Section~\ref{sec:intro} rests on Mumford's and Bers's compactness, and the instances of Section~\ref{sec:practice} use the proved bound $2\diam P$ with Lemma~\ref{lem:counting}(i). It is kept as an explicit bound and for comparison with the lower bound $\log\frac m{2\pi}$ for $\diam\Orb(2,3,m)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:233}.

\section{Counting eigenvalues}\label{sec:count}

\begin{lemma}[Elementary bounds]\label{lem:elem}
(i) Every $\Orb\in\Sig$ has $\Area(\Orb)\ge\pi/21$ and $n+4g\le\Area(\Orb)/\pi+4$. (ii) For $t>0$ and $m\ge2$, $0<\mathrm E_m(t)\le b_0(m)=\frac{m^2-1}{12m}$ and $0<\mathrm I(t)\le e^{-t/4}\Area/4\pi t$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) With $s=\Area/2\pi$ (the signatures with $g=0$, $n\le2$ and with $g=1$, $n=0$ are not hyperbolic): $g\ge2$ gives $s\ge2$; $g=1$ needs $n\ge1$, $s\ge\frac12$; $g=0$, $n\ge5$ gives $s\ge\frac12$; $g=0$, $n=4$ has an order at least $3$, $s\ge\frac16$; and for $g=0$, $n=3$, $p\le q\le r$: $p\ge3$ forces $r\ge4$, $s\ge\frac1{12}$; $p=2$, $q=3$ forces $r\ge7$, $s\ge\frac1{42}$; $p=2$, $q=4$ forces $r\ge5$, $s\ge\frac1{20}$; $p=2$, $q\ge5$ gives $s\ge\frac1{10}$. The second statement holds as each cone point adds at least $\frac12$ to $s$. (ii) Proposition~\ref{prop:remcone}(a) with $K=0$ gives $0<\mathrm E_m(t)\le b_0(m)$, and $0<\hat g_t\le e^{-t/4}$ and $0\le r\tanh\pi r\le|r|$ give the bound on $\mathrm I$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Counting and tails]\label{prop:counttail}
For every $\Orb$, $x>0$, $\Lambda>0$ and $0<s<t$: $\#\{j:\lambda_j\le x\}\le e\,\cZ_\Orb(1/x)$ and $\sum_{\lambda_j>\Lambda}e^{-\lambda_jt}\le e^{-\Lambda(t-s)}\cZ_\Orb(s)$. If $N\ge e\,\cZ_\Orb(1/\Lambda)$ is an integer, then $\lambda_j>\Lambda$ for $j\ge N$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\cZ(1/x)\ge\sum_{\lambda_j\le x}e^{-\lambda_j/x}\ge e^{-1}\#\{\lambda_j\le x\}$, and $e^{-\lambda t}\le e^{-\Lambda(t-s)}e^{-\lambda s}$ for $\lambda>\Lambda$.
\end{proof}

So, for $\Orb$ of area at most $A$, systole $\ge\ell$ and $n$ cone points of orders $m_i$, with $n_\Orb(x)\le Ce^x$, $\cZ_\Orb(s)\le\frac A{4\pi s}+\sum_ib_0(m_i)+\Hyp(s)$ for all $s$, with $\Hyp(s)$ bounded by Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp}(ii).

\section{Finitely many eigenvalues determine the signature}\label{sec:E}

Fix $A>0$, $\varepsilon>0$ and $M\ge2$. Put $n_*=\lfloor A/\pi\rfloor+4$, $k_*=\min(n_*,M)$, $L_M=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,M)$, $\delta_1=1/(2\min(L_M,M^{2n_*}))$, $\delta_k=a_{k-2}$ for $2\le k\le k_*$, $\gamma_*=\min_{k\le k_*}\delta_k$,
\[
  Q(K)=\frac A{4\pi}|\alpha_{K+1}|+n_*\,|b_K(M)|,\qquad t_1=\min\Big\{1,\ \min_{1\le k\le k_*}\frac{\delta_k}{4\,Q(k-1)}\Big\},
\]
and $\Theta(t)=\frac12\gamma_*t^{k_*-2}$.

\begin{lemma}[The gap]\label{lem:gap}
For $\sigma\ne\sigma'$ in $\Sig(A,M)$ and $0<t\le t_1$, $|G_\sigma(t)-G_{\sigma'}(t)|\ge\Theta(t)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $k$ be the first index of difference, so $k\le k_*$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:int}). By \eqref{eq:Grem} with $K=k-1$, $n\le n_*$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:elem}) and $|b_K|$ increasing in $m$, $|G_\sigma(t)-\sum_{j\le k}c_j(\sigma)t^{j-2}|\le t^{k-1}Q(k-1)$, likewise for $\sigma'$, and so
\[
  |G_\sigma(t)-G_{\sigma'}(t)|\ge t^{k-2}\big(|d_k|-2tQ(k-1)\big)\ge\tfrac12t^{k-2}|d_k|\ge\tfrac12\delta_kt^{k-2}\ge\Theta(t),
\]
since $|d_k|\ge\delta_k$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:int}; the lcm $L_{\sigma\sigma'}$ divides $L_M$ and, as $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ have at most $2n_*$ orders, each at most $M$, is at most $M^{2n_*}$), $t\le\delta_k/(4Q(k-1))$, $t\le1$ and $k\le k_*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finitely many eigenvalues determine the signature]\label{thm:E}
With $C_*=C_*(A,\varepsilon,M)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}, put
\begin{gather*}
  t_2=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2(1+\varepsilon)},\qquad p=k_*-\tfrac32,\qquad\varpi=\frac{3C_*\,\varepsilon e^{\varepsilon/2}}{(1-e^{-\varepsilon})\sqrt{4\pi}},\\
  y_0=2\Big(\log\frac{16\varpi}{\gamma_*}+p\log\frac4{\varepsilon^2}+p\log\frac{2p}e\Big),\qquad t_3=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4\max(y_0,1)},\qquad t_*=\min(t_1,t_2,t_3),\\
  \Theta_*=\Theta(t_*),\qquad\cZ^\sharp(s)=\frac A{4\pi s}+n_*\frac{M^2-1}{12M}+1,\qquad\Lambda=\frac2{t_*}\log\frac{8\cZ^\sharp(t_*/2)}{\Theta_*},\\
  N=\big\lfloor e\,\cZ^\sharp(1/\Lambda)\big\rfloor+1,\qquad\delta=\frac{\Theta_*}{8eNt_*}.
\end{gather*}
Let $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ have eigenvalues $0=\lambda_0<\lambda_1\le\cdots$, counted with multiplicity and in increasing order, and let $\tilde\lambda_0,\dots,\tilde\lambda_{N-1}\ge0$ satisfy $|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j|\le\delta$, or, more generally, $\sum_{j<N}|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j|e^{-t_*\min(\tilde\lambda_j,\lambda_j)}\le N\delta$. Then $\sigma(\Orb)$ is the unique $\sigma\in\Sig(A,M)$ minimising $|\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt_*}-G_\sigma(t_*)|$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\tilde\cZ=\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt_*}$ and $\sigma_0=\sigma(\Orb)$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:trace},
$\tilde\cZ-G_{\sigma_0}(t_*)=\Hyp(t_*)-\sum_{j\ge N}e^{-\lambda_jt_*}+\sum_{j<N}(e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt_*}-e^{-\lambda_jt_*})$.
\emph{Hyperbolic term.} For $0<t\le t_2$, Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp}(ii) with $\ell=\varepsilon$ and the count of Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}, and $1+\frac{2t}{\varepsilon-t}\le3$, give $\Hyp(t)\le B_*(t):=\varpi\,t^{-1/2}e^{-\varepsilon^2/4t}$, which increases on $(0,t_2]$. With $y=\varepsilon^2/4t$, $B_*(t)\le\Theta(t)/8$ reads $e^{-y}y^p\le\frac{\gamma_*}{16\varpi}(\varepsilon^2/4)^p$, and as $e^{-y/2}y^p\le(2p/e)^p$ it holds for $y\ge y_0$, so at $t=t_*$. Hence $\Hyp(s)\le\Theta_*/8\le1$ for $s\le t_*$.
\emph{Tail.} For $s\le t_*$, $\cZ_\Orb(s)\le\cZ^\sharp(s)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:elem} and the line above. As $t_*\le t_1\le1$ gives $\Theta_*\le\frac18$, $\Lambda t_*=2\log(8\cZ^\sharp(t_*/2)/\Theta_*)\ge2\log64\ge1$, so $t_*/2$ and $1/\Lambda$ are at most $t_*$, so $N>e\,\cZ_\Orb(1/\Lambda)$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:counttail} with $s=t_*/2$ gives $\sum_{j\ge N}e^{-\lambda_jt_*}\le e^{-\Lambda t_*/2}\cZ^\sharp(t_*/2)=\Theta_*/8$.
\emph{Perturbation.} $|e^{-at}-e^{-bt}|\le t|a-b|e^{-t\min(a,b)}\le t|a-b|$ for $a,b\ge0$ gives at most $t_*\sum_{j<N}|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j|e^{-t_*\min(\tilde\lambda_j,\lambda_j)}\le Nt_*\delta\le\Theta_*/(8e)$ under either hypothesis.
So $|\tilde\cZ-G_{\sigma_0}(t_*)|<\frac38\Theta_*$, while for $\sigma\ne\sigma_0$ Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} gives $|\tilde\cZ-G_\sigma(t_*)|>\Theta_*-\frac38\Theta_*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Accuracy and omissions high in the list]\label{rem:omissions}
The weighted hypothesis allows the $j$-th value to over-estimate $\lambda_j$ by up to $N\delta e^{\lambda_jt_*}$ if the other values are exact (an under-estimate is weighted by $e^{-t_*\tilde\lambda_j}$ and allows much less): at the top of the list, where $e^{\lambda_jt_*}$ is of the order of $e^{\Lambda t_*}$, a crude accuracy suffices. The proof also leaves room for a few wrong entries. The three errors in it add up to at most $\frac14\Theta_*+\frac1{8e}\Theta_*$, and the conclusion needs less than $\frac12\Theta_*$, so it persists under a further error of at most $\frac18\Theta_*$ in $\tilde\cZ$. The sum $\tilde\cZ$ does not depend on the order of the list. If the list, as a multiset, arises from one satisfying the hypothesis by replacing $K$ of its entries by arbitrary values (an eigenvalue omitted and the next one taken at the end of the list, a value duplicated, a spurious value), with every replaced entry and every inserted value at least $\Lambda_o$, each replacement changes $\tilde\cZ$ by at most $e^{-\Lambda_ot_*}$; so the conclusion holds when $Ke^{-\Lambda_ot_*}\le\frac18\Theta_*$, for instance for one wrong entry above $\Lambda_o=t_*^{-1}\log(8/\Theta_*)$. Below that level a single omission can change the answer, as the computed examples of Section~\ref{sec:practice} show.
\end{remark}

The proof uses Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} only for the gap at $t_*$, and $t_1$ only through that lemma and through $t_*\le1$, which gives $\Theta_*\le\frac18$ and $\Lambda t_*\ge1$. With $\Theta\le8$ and $\Lambda t\ge1$ assumed instead, which is all that the proof uses of them, the same argument gives the following. (The bound $\Theta\le8$ is what makes $\Hyp(s)\le B_*(s)\le\Theta/8\le1$ for $s\le t$, which is the term $1$ of $\cZ^\sharp$; it is not a misprint for the $\frac18$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}.)

\begin{corollary}[A computed gap]\label{cor:computedgap}
Let $0<t\le t_2$ and $0<\Theta\le8$ be such that $|G_\sigma(t)-G_{\sigma'}(t)|\ge\Theta$ for all $\sigma\ne\sigma'$ in $\Sig(A,M)$ at this one time $t$, and $B_*(t)\le\Theta/8$, where $B_*(t)=\varpi t^{-1/2}e^{-\varepsilon^2/4t}$ is the bound for the closed geodesics in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}, and define $\Lambda$, $N$ and $\delta$ by the formulas of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} with $t,\Theta$ in place of $t_*,\Theta_*$. If $\Lambda t\ge1$, the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} holds with $t$ in place of $t_*$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}, with the hypothesis on the gap in place of Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}; $B_*$ increases on $(0,t_2]$, so $\Hyp(s)\le\Theta/8\le1$ for $s\le t$.
\end{proof}

At every rational time below $t_*$ the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:computedgap} can be reached by a computation that is guaranteed to stop; this is the computable form of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}.

\begin{theorem}[A terminating certified procedure]\label{thm:computable}
Let $A>0$ with $A/2\pi$ rational, as in every example, let $\varepsilon>0$ be a computable real number and $M\ge2$, let $t_*$, $\Theta(t)$, $\cZ^\sharp$ and $B_*(t)=\varpi t^{-1/2}e^{-\varepsilon^2/4t}$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:E} and its proof, and let $t$ be a rational number with $0<t<t_*$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $|G_\sigma(t)-G_{\sigma'}(t)|\ge\Theta(t)$ for all $\sigma\ne\sigma'$ in $\Sig(A,M)$, and $B_*(t)<\Theta(t)/8$.
\item For $k=1,2,\dots$ compute rational numbers $q_\sigma$, $\sigma\in\Sig(A,M)$, with $|q_\sigma-G_\sigma(t)|\le2^{-k}$, and a rational number $b_k$ with $B_*(t)\le b_k\le B_*(t)+2^{-k}$, put
\[
  \Theta_k=\min\Big\{1,\ \max\Big(\Theta(t),\ \min_{\sigma\ne\sigma'}|q_\sigma-q_{\sigma'}|-2^{1-k}\Big)\Big\},
\]
and stop at the first $k$ with $b_k\le\Theta_k/8$. This procedure stops, and the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:computedgap} hold at $t$ with $\Theta=\Theta_k$. Consequently, with $\Lambda=\frac2t\log(8\cZ^\sharp(t/2)/\Theta)$, for every integer $N>e\cZ^\sharp(1/\Lambda)$ and every $\delta$ with $0<\delta\le\Theta/(8eNt)$, the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} holds with $t$ in place of $t_*$.
\end{enumerate}
Every step is a finite computation, and $t_*$ is a positive computable real number, so that a rational $t$ with $0<t<t_*$ can be chosen; so $N$ and $\delta$ are computable from $A$, $\varepsilon$ and $M$ by a procedure that always terminates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) As $t<t_*\le t_1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} gives the bound for the gaps. Put $y=\varepsilon^2/4t$ and $p=k_*-\frac32$, positive as $k_*\ge2$. As $t<t_*\le t_3$, $y>\max(y_0,1)\ge y_0$. As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}, $B_*(t)<\Theta(t)/8$ is equivalent to $e^{-y}y^p<\frac{\gamma_*}{16\varpi}(\frac{\varepsilon^2}4)^p$, and $e^{-y}y^p=e^{-y/2}\,(e^{-y/2}y^p)\le e^{-y/2}(2p/e)^p<e^{-y_0/2}(2p/e)^p$, which equals $\frac{\gamma_*}{16\varpi}(\frac{\varepsilon^2}4)^p$ by the definition of $y_0$.
(ii) As $\gamma_*\le\delta_2=a_0=\frac1{12}$ and $t<1$, $\Theta(t)\le\frac1{24}$, so $\Theta_k\ge\Theta(t)$ for every $k$; as $b_k\to B_*(t)<\Theta(t)/8$ by (i), the procedure stops. When it stops, every gap is at least $\Theta(t)$, by (i), and at least $|q_\sigma-q_{\sigma'}|-2^{1-k}$, so at least $\Theta_k$; further $0<\Theta_k\le1\le8$, $B_*(t)\le b_k\le\Theta_k/8$, $t<t_*\le t_2$, and $\Lambda t=2\log(8\cZ^\sharp(t/2)/\Theta_k)\ge2\log8\ge1$, as $\cZ^\sharp\ge1$. So Corollary~\ref{cor:computedgap} applies. Its proof, that of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}, uses of $N$ and $\delta$ only that $N$ is an integer with $N>e\cZ^\sharp(1/\Lambda)$, so that $N\ge e\cZ_\Orb(1/\Lambda)$, and that $Nt\delta\le\Theta/(8e)$.
Every step is a finite computation. $\Sig(A,M)$ is finite and is enumerated exactly from $n+4g\le A/\pi+4$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:elem}), the condition $s(\sigma)\le A/2\pi$ being decided in rational arithmetic as $A/2\pi$ is rational; $\Theta(t)$ is rational; $B_*(t)$, $\cZ^\sharp$ and $\Lambda$ are elementary expressions in $t$, $\Theta_k$ and the constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}, so a rational $u$ with $e\cZ^\sharp(1/\Lambda)\le u\le e\cZ^\sharp(1/\Lambda)+1$ can be computed, and $N=\lfloor u\rfloor+1$ and a rational $\delta\le\Theta/(8eNt)$ can be taken. For the $q_\sigma$: $G_\sigma(t)=\frac{s(\sigma)}2\mathrm I_1(t)+\sum_i\mathrm E_{m_i}(t)$, with $s(\sigma)=\Area/2\pi$ and $\mathrm I_1(t)=e^{-t/4}/t-\int_0^\infty h$, $h(r)=4re^{-t(1/4+r^2)}/(e^{2\pi r}+1)$, as in the certificate below, and $\mathrm E_m(t)$ is a finite sum of multiples of $\int_\R F_j$, $F_j(r)=e^{-2\theta_jr-t(1/4+r^2)}/(1+e^{-2\pi r})$. These integrals have the explicit tail bounds of the certificate below, $0\le h\le1$ and $|h'(r)|\le2+2r(2\pi+2tr)$ for $r\ge0$, and $0<F_j\le1$ and $|F_j'(r)|\le(2\theta_j+2\pi+2t|r|)F_j(r)$; so on a bounded interval of length $H$ with these Lipschitz constants $L$, the midpoint sum with $n$ points has error at most $LH^2/4n$, and with the exponentials evaluated to a sufficient accuracy this gives each $q_\sigma$.
\end{proof}

Since $\Theta_k\ge\Theta(t)$ and $\Lambda$ decreases as $\Theta$ grows, the $N$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:computable} at $t$ is, up to the rounding in $u$, at most that of the formulas of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} evaluated at $t$ with $\Theta(t)$; in practice the computed gap is far larger than $\Theta(t)$. At a time $t\ge t_*$, where the gaps are larger and $N$ smaller, the procedure need not stop; Proposition~\ref{prop:certgap} below records six runs of the same certification, in ball arithmetic and at such times, that did; in them $\Theta$ is the certified least gap, rounded down, not $\Theta_k$ of the rule in (ii).

Each $G_\sigma(t)$ is a finite sum of explicit integrals (Theorem~\ref{thm:trace}), so the gap is a finite computation, and we certified it. For each class of Table~\ref{tab:certgap}, $\Sig(A,M)$ was enumerated exactly from $n+4g\le A/\pi+4$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:elem}), and a time $t$ near the best time of a floating-point search was rounded to the decimal number printed in the table; this exact rational number enters the computation as a ball that contains it. Every integral was enclosed at that $t$ in ball arithmetic, with Arb's certified integration \cite{arb2017,arbintegration2018} at $200$ bits (python-flint 0.9.0 with FLINT 3.6.0), with Arb's default relative and absolute tolerances $2^{-200}$ and its default limits on the work, which can only abort a computation (every ball returned is an enclosure), on a finite interval, and an explicit bound for each tail was added to the radius of the ball: the identity term was used in the form $\mathrm I(t)=\frac{\Area}{4\pi}\big(e^{-t/4}/t-\int_0^\infty4re^{-t(1/4+r^2)}(e^{2\pi r}+1)^{-1}dr\big)$, from $r\tanh\pi r=|r|-2|r|/(e^{2\pi|r|}+1)$, integrated over $[0,40]$ with the tail bound $\int_{40}^\infty4re^{-2\pi r}dr$, and the $j$-th elliptic integral over $[-R_1,R_2]$, $R_2=\lfloor80/2\theta_j\rfloor+1$, $R_1=\lfloor80/(2\pi-2\theta_j)\rfloor+1$, with the tail bounds $e^{-2\theta_jR_2}/2\theta_j$ and $e^{-(2\pi-2\theta_j)R_1}/(2\pi-2\theta_j)$, all below $2e^{-80}$. Arb's integration requires the integrand to be holomorphic on the complex balls on which it is evaluated. The integrands here are built from the exponential, polynomials and division only, so they are meromorphic, with poles only where $e^{2\pi r}+1$ or $1+e^{-2\pi r}$ vanishes ($r\in i(\Z+\frac12)$), and no branch cut; ball evaluation on a ball that meets a pole divides by a ball containing $0$ and returns a non-finite value, which the integration routine treats as a failure of holomorphy, so the requirement is met without further checks. Every pairwise difference of the enclosures of the $G_\sigma(t)$ was decided to be nonzero, and the least of their lower ends, rounded down, is $\Theta$. Then $C_*$, $B_*(t)$, $t_2$, $\cZ^\sharp$ and $\Lambda$ were evaluated in ball arithmetic, the inequalities $B_*(t)\le\Theta/8$, $t\le t_2$ and $\Lambda t\ge1$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:computedgap} were decided by the balls, $N=\lfloor e\cZ^\sharp(1/\Lambda)\rfloor+1$ was taken only when the ball of $e\cZ^\sharp(1/\Lambda)$ lies strictly between two consecutive integers, and $\delta$ was rounded down. Table~\ref{tab:certdetail} gives, for each class, a pair with the least gap and the certified margins; the smallest margin is in the third row, $B_*(t)\le0.0180$ against $\Theta/8=0.0190$. The least gap is attained by two pairs in the first class, by two in each class with $A=\frac{4\pi}3$ and $M=3$, by three in the class with $A=2\pi$, and by one in the other two classes. These ties are exact: replacing an order common to $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ by another changes both areas equally and leaves $G_\sigma-G_{\sigma'}$ unchanged, so $(0;2,3,8)$, $(0;2,4,5)$ has the same gap as $(0;3^2,8)$, $(0;3,4,5)$, and all pairs that differ by $\{3,3,3\}$ against $\{2,2,2,2\}$ have the same gap. Appendix~\ref{app:certificate} lists the enclosures of $\mathrm I_1(t)=\frac{4\pi}{\Area}\mathrm I(t)$ and of $\mathrm E_m(t)$, $2\le m\le M$, at most twelve numbers per class, from which every $G_\sigma(t)=\frac{s(\sigma)}2\mathrm I_1(t)+\sum_i\mathrm E_{m_i}(t)$, every gap and the inequalities of Table~\ref{tab:certdetail} can be checked by hand or with any interval package. The program \texttt{certified\_gap.py} of the code and data deposit carries out these steps and asserts each of them, including that the decimals of Appendix~\ref{app:certificate} alone give every gap at least $\Theta$; it runs in less than a minute. This proves the following.

\begin{proposition}[Certified instances]\label{prop:certgap}
For each class $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ of Table~\ref{tab:certgap}, the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:computedgap} hold at the stated $t$ with the stated $\Theta$. The decision rule needs $G_\sigma(t_*)$ only to within a fraction of $\Theta_*$, say $\Theta_*/16$, which leaves the margin of the proof intact; by \eqref{eq:Grem} this is achieved by the truncated expansion $\sum_{j\le K+1}c_j(\sigma)t_*^{j-2}$ for any $K$ with $t_*^K\,Q(K)\le\Theta_*/16$. A rational $t\le t_*$ may replace $t_*$ if $\Theta_*$, $\Lambda$, $N$ and $\delta$ are recomputed at $t$, and the rule is then computable to any prescribed accuracy; evaluating it costs a number of digits that grows with $\log(1/\Theta_*)$, while the dominant cost, computing $N$ eigenvalues to accuracy $\delta$, is out of reach for the classes of Table~\ref{tab:E}; $e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}$ is not rational, so it is not a computation in exact rational arithmetic.

Taking $\tilde\lambda_j=\lambda_j(\Orb')$ gives the last statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:intro}: orbifolds of $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ whose first $N$ eigenvalues agree to within $\delta$ have the same signature. The data must be the first $N$ eigenvalues, with multiplicity and none omitted; an approximation that misses one eigenvalue shifts every later index, and the theorem says nothing about it.

\begin{remark}[Sharper constants]\label{rem:sharper}
The constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} are uniform over the class and are not optimised. Their size is due in part to the small times at which the heat invariants control the gap, and in part to the a-priori data $k_*$ and $\gamma_*$, which are worst cases; the following gains are available. (a) The separations $\delta_k$ and the index $k_*$ are worst cases over $\Sig(A,M)$: in every row of Table~\ref{tab:E} the minimum defining $t_1$ is attained at $k=k_*$, while in the classes we enumerated exhaustively ($A\le4\pi$ with $M\le10$, $A\le2\pi$ with $M\le30$, $A\le6\pi$ with $M\le6$; a computer enumeration of the code deposit, which also checks Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} on these classes, an observation and not a theorem) no two signatures first differ beyond $k=3$, which fails at larger area and order: $(0;2,2,2,2,7^7)$ and $(0;4^6,8^4)$, of area $12\pi$, share their first three heat invariants and differ at the fourth; $\delta_1$ is far below the least nonzero first difference in a given finite $\Sig(A,M)$. Both can be computed exactly over $\Sig(A,M)$, in rational arithmetic from \eqref{eq:cj}, and the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} applies with them in place of $k_*$ and $\delta_k$: for $A=\frac\pi2$ and $M=12$ the largest first-difference index is $3$, against $k_*=4$, the least first differences at $k=1,2,3$ are $\frac1{2640}$, $\frac1{12}$ and $\frac{25}{12}$, and $t_1$ rises from $6.8\times10^{-9}$ to $2.4\times10^{-5}$. Further, $\Theta(t)$ may keep the individual terms $\delta_kt^{k-2}$ instead of combining the least $\delta_k$ with the highest power of $t$. Corollary~\ref{cor:computedgap} and Theorem~\ref{thm:computable} go further and use the gap itself; Proposition~\ref{prop:certgap} is this class-exact route. (b) The hyperbolic term and the spectral tail enter with opposite signs, so their sum may be replaced by their maximum, gaining at most a factor $2$. (c) In Theorem~\ref{thm:post}, enumerated closed geodesics of length at most $L$ may be summed exactly and the count used only above $L$, replacing $e^{-\ell^2/4t}$ by $e^{-L^2/4t}$ (for the examples of Section~\ref{sec:practice}, $L=4$). (d) In the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}, $s=t_*/2$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:counttail} may be replaced by the infimum over $s\in(0,t_*)$, and the factor $1+\frac{2t}{\varepsilon-t}$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp} kept instead of bounded by $3$. We have not used these improvements.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Other test functions]\label{rem:testfn}
The heat function is not the only possible test function. If the geodesic-side function $g$ is supported in $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$, the trace formula for a member of $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ has no geodesic term, and the constraint $t_3$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} disappears. The signature terms must still be separated quantitatively, and the spectral side truncated with a count of eigenvalues, which windowed test functions supported below the systole, as in \cite[proof of Lemma~\ref*{A-lem:admissible}]{companionA}, would give in terms of the area, the orders and the systole alone. That route would remove $t_3$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:countK} from the argument, but needs an explicit treatment of the small eigenvalues and of the separation for a compactly supported $g$; we have not pursued it, and keep the heat function because it gives both inputs explicitly.
\end{remark}

\section{An a-posteriori test on computed spectra}\label{sec:practice}

The constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} are worst cases over a class. Run on given data at one moderate time, the same mechanism gives a test. Its applications below are illustrations, not proofs (Table~\ref{tab:status}).

\begin{theorem}[A-posteriori test]\label{thm:post}
Let $\Orb\in\Sig$ have area at most $A$, systole at least $\ell$, $n_\Orb(x)\le Ce^x$ for $x>0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:counting}(i) with a diameter bound $\Delta$, $C=\pi e^{3\Delta}/\Area(\Orb)$, with $\Area(\Orb)$ replaced by a lower bound such as $\frac\pi{21}$ when it is not known, or Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}, $C=C_*(A,\ell,M)$ when the orders of $\Orb$ are at most $M$), and signature in a finite set $\mathcal S$ of hyperbolic signatures of area at most $A$ (for instance those of area $\Area(\Orb)$, when it is known, or all of $\Sig(A,M)$); put $\beta=\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal S}\sum_ib_0(m_i)$ and let $\mathcal H(s)$ be the bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp}(ii) for $\Hyp(s)$ with $\ell$ and $C$: the first bound for $s\le\ell^2/(2(1+\ell))$ and the bound for every $t>0$ otherwise. Let $\lambda_0\le\lambda_1\le\dots$ be the eigenvalues of $\Orb$, counted with multiplicity, and let $\tilde\lambda_j\ge0$ and $\epsilon_j\ge0$ satisfy $|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j|\le\epsilon_j$ for $j\le N$; in particular none of $\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_N$ is omitted. For $t>0$ put
\[
  E_N(t)=\mathcal H(t)+\inf_{0<s<t}e^{-(\tilde\lambda_N-\epsilon_N)(t-s)}\Big(\frac A{4\pi s}+\beta+\mathcal H(s)\Big)+t\sum_{j<N}\epsilon_je^{-t\max(\tilde\lambda_j-\epsilon_j,0)} .
\]
Then $|\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}-G_{\sigma(\Orb)}(t)|\le E_N(t)$ for every $t>0$. Hence $\sigma(\Orb)=\sigma$ if, at some $t$, either
\begin{enumerate}[label=(C\arabic*)]
\item $\sigma$ is the only element of $\mathcal S$ with $|\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}-G_\sigma(t)|\le E_N(t)$, or
\item $\sigma$ satisfies this inequality and $E_N(t)$ is less than half the distance from $G_\sigma(t)$ to the nearest $G_{\sigma'}(t)$, $\sigma'\in\mathcal S\setminus\{\sigma\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}: the hyperbolic term is at most $\mathcal H(t)$; for $0<s<t$ the tail $\sum_{j\ge N}e^{-\lambda_jt}$ is at most $e^{-\lambda_N(t-s)}\cZ_\Orb(s)$, with $\lambda_N\ge\tilde\lambda_N-\epsilon_N$ and $\cZ_\Orb(s)\le\frac A{4\pi s}+\beta+\mathcal H(s)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:elem}); and the perturbation is at most $t\sum_{j<N}\epsilon_je^{-t\max(\tilde\lambda_j-\epsilon_j,0)}$, by $|e^{-at}-e^{-bt}|\le t|a-b|e^{-t\min(a,b)}$ for $a,b\ge0$ and $\min(\lambda_j,\tilde\lambda_j)\ge\max(\tilde\lambda_j-\epsilon_j,0)$. Under (C1) the true signature passes, so it is $\sigma$; (C2) implies (C1).
\end{proof}

Criterion (C2) is the margin used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}; (C1) is weaker. The test has five inputs, and its conclusion is only as good as they are.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The area. The test needs only an upper bound $A$: with $\mathcal S=\Sig(A,M)$ and $C=C_*(A,\ell,M)$ it uses nothing else about the area (the second-to-last column of Table~\ref{tab:practice}); with $\mathcal S$ the signatures of a known exact area it decides among those.
\item A lower bound $\ell$ for the systole. Ours come from a complete enumeration of the short closed geodesics, certified in interval arithmetic (Section~\ref{sec:numerical}).
\item A count of the closed geodesics. The class-level count $C_*(A,\ell,M)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:countK} needs nothing beyond the area, the systole bound and the order bound; an instance-level count comes from a diameter bound, and ours, $2\diam P$ for the double of a convex polygon $P$, are proved bounds.
\item Completeness: the computed list contains every eigenvalue up to $\tilde\lambda_N$, with multiplicity. Deleting or duplicating one of $\tilde\lambda_1,\tilde\lambda_2,\tilde\lambda_3,\tilde\lambda_5,\tilde\lambda_8,\tilde\lambda_{12}$ of $\Orb(2,8,8)$ or $\Orb(3,3,12)$ makes (C1), with the instance's diameter bound and at the first $N$ at which it singles out a signature, conclude a wrong one in $19$ of these $24$ cases, mostly at a smaller $N$ than the true count. Continuing the scan detects all $24$: each corrupted list reaches some $(N,t)$ at which no signature of $\mathcal S$ passes, which Theorem~\ref{thm:post} excludes for a correct, complete list. The protocol that accepts $\sigma$ only if some signature passes at every $(N,t)$ of the trusted range and $\sigma$ is the only one ever singled out rejects all $24$ lists and accepts the ten computed spectra; it is empirical, not a proved criterion.
\item The errors $\epsilon_j$. Ours are a-posteriori estimates, the difference between two discretisations, and not enclosures. The counts are insensitive to them: replacing $\epsilon_j$ by $\max(\epsilon_j,r\tilde\lambda_j)$ leaves the counts $18$ and $25$ of the triangle orbifolds unchanged for $r\le10^{-3}$, and for $r=10^{-2}$ they become $21$ and $43$.
\end{enumerate}
A rigorous decision would need guaranteed eigenvalue enclosures by index, a proof of completeness independent of the signature, a validated evaluation of $G_\sigma(t)$ and outward rounding in $E_N$; Sylvester's law of inertia for the factorisation $K-sM$ counts discrete eigenvalues exactly, and lower bounds of Crouzeix--Raviart type \cite{liuoishi2013,carstensengedicke2014} with the conforming upper bounds would enclose each $\lambda_j$. For the triangle class of Proposition~\ref{prop:certgap} ($N=283$, $t=0.02482$) the uniform hypothesis asks for $\delta=4.27\times10^{-5}$, about $2\times10^{-8}$ relative at $\lambda_{282}\approx2.25\times10^3$, while the weighted hypothesis $\sum_{j<N}|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j|e^{-t\min(\tilde\lambda_j,\lambda_j)}\le N\delta=0.0121$ holds for a relative error $r$ in every eigenvalue up to about $6\times10^{-5}$ by Weyl's law, and up to $5.9\times10^{-5}$ with our computed eigenvalues for both triangle orbifolds, the eigenvalues above $500$ contributing less than $10^{-4}$ of the sum. So a rigorous instance needs index-wise enclosures of relative accuracy about $6\times10^{-5}$ in the low part of the spectrum, three orders of magnitude weaker than the uniform requirement; we expect it to be within reach of the methods cited, but have not carried it out.

\subsection{Numerical methods}\label{sec:numerical}

\emph{Examples.} $\Orb(2,8,8)$ and $\Orb(3,3,12)$ are doubles of hyperbolic triangles; the eight orbifolds $\Orb_\vartheta$ of signature $(0;3,3,3,3)$, $\vartheta=0,0.4,\dots,2.8$, are doubles of geodesic quadrilaterals $P$ with all angles $\frac\pi3$ and two mirror symmetries, with $a,b$ the distances from the centre to two adjacent sides, $\sinh a\sinh b=\frac12$ and $\vartheta=\log(\sinh a/\sinh b)$.

\emph{Discretisation and solver.} The spectrum of the double of $P$ is the union of the Neumann and Dirichlet spectra of $P$; for $\Orb_\vartheta$ the two mirror symmetries split each further into four sectors. Each problem $-\Delta_Eu=\lambda wu$, $w=4/(1-|z|^2)^2$, is solved in the Poincar\'e disc with $H^1$-conforming finite elements of order $p$ on meshes of uniform hyperbolic size $h$, the elements curved to represent the geodesic sides to order $p$ (the area agrees to $2\times10^{-14}$), with NGSolve \cite{ngsolve,schoberl1997}. No grading is needed, as the eigenfunctions extend smoothly by reflection across the corners; the observed convergence is $h^{13}$ to $h^{19}$ at $p=10$ and geometric in $p$. Eigenvalues are computed by shift-invert Lanczos iteration (ARPACK \cite{arpack1998}) in overlapping spectral windows, every eigenvalue covered by two windows; the triangle spectra, first computed with single windows, were recomputed this way, with the same eigenvalue counts below a common cut, largest relative difference $1.4\times10^{-14}$, and the same counts in Table~\ref{tab:practice}.

\emph{Error estimates.} The production level is $(h,p)=(0.05,10)$, and $\epsilon_j=\max(|\lambda_j(0.05,10)-\lambda_j(0.07,12)|,\ 10^{-14}\max(\lambda_j,1))$, the difference between two discretisations ($\epsilon_0=0$), at most $7.4\times10^{-8}$ relative in the complete ranges. These are estimates, not bounds: the domain is approximated, the weight integrated by quadrature and the computation done in floating point, so no $\tilde\lambda_j$ is a proved upper bound, and no lower bounds are computed. For the first $40$ eigenvalues, all that the counts for the triangle orbifolds use, the $\epsilon_j$ are at rounding level (Table~\ref{tab:spectra}), and the conclusions rest on the insensitivity of the counts to the errors (item~(v) above); with the second estimate $|\lambda_j(0.05,10)-\lambda_j(0.07,10)|$, up to $1.2\times10^{-8}$ relative for the first $500$, the counts for the triangle orbifolds are unchanged. As a benchmark, $\Orb(2,8,8)$ is a quotient of the Bolza surface, and its computed $\tilde\lambda_1=3.838887258842$ agrees with the first eigenvalue $3.83888725884220\ldots$ of the Bolza surface \cite{strohmaieruski2013}, \cite{kmp2024} to all twelve decimals; the convergence data are in the supplement of \cite{companionA}.

\emph{Completeness.} For the triangle orbifolds about $2850$ eigenvalues were computed, and their union can be complete only below $\lambda_{\rm top}\approx2.174\times10^4$ (respectively $2.189\times10^4$). At $t=0.0015$ the computed heat trace agrees with $\mathrm I+\mathrm E$ for the true signature to $4.8\times10^{-13}$ (respectively $5.1\times10^{-13}$), within an error budget of $6.5\times10^{-11}$ (respectively $5.4\times10^{-11}$) formed from the $\epsilon_j$ and the tail bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:counttail} above $\lambda_{\rm top}$, while every other signature of area $\frac\pi2$ with orders at most $12$ differs by at least $2.96\times10^{-3}$; with the budget taken with the maximum $1.604\ldots$ of $\sum_ib_0(m_i)$ over the competitors, the check presupposes only the area and the order bound. One missing or one spurious eigenvalue below $1.563\times10^4$ (respectively $1.576\times10^4$) would exceed the budget, but a missing and a spurious one nearby would not; so completeness below $\lambda_c=1.6\times10^4$ is supported by this comparison and by the agreement of the single- and two-window computations, not proved, and we use the about $2000$ eigenvalues below $\lambda_c$ (Table~\ref{tab:inputs}). For $\Orb_\vartheta$ about $5800$ eigenvalues were computed per member; we use those below $0.8$ times the least sector maximum, where the eigenvalue counts of the three most accurate of five discretisations agree exactly in every sector, $4357$ to $4451$ of them; the trace test at $t\ge0.0025$, with a budget of $2.2\times10^{-11}$, covers $\lambda<9.8\times10^3$, which contains every $\tilde\lambda_N$ that the test with the instance inputs uses, and above it the agreement of the counts is the only evidence.

\emph{Systoles and diameters.} Every closed geodesic of length at most $L$ has a representative $\gamma$ with $d(x_0,\gamma x_0)\le L+2r$, where $r=\max_{y\in P}d(x_0,y)$ (move a point of the axis into $P$ by the reflection group $W$, in which $\Gamma$ is normal; the element obtained is conjugate to $\gamma$ in $W$, possibly replacing its class by the mirror class, of the same length). A tile $wP$ that meets the ball of radius $L+2r$ about $x_0$ has $d(x_0,wx_0)\le L+3r$, so enumerating all tiles of the reflection tiling within distance $L+3r$ of $x_0$ by breadth-first search, with $L=4$, gives the systole and the next length, in $40$- and $50$-digit arithmetic (agreeing to $10^{-30}$); $\ell$ in Table~\ref{tab:inputs} is the systole rounded down at the sixth decimal, a margin of at least $5\times10^{-8}$. The same enumeration in interval arithmetic ($60$ digits; every reflection matrix and product enclosing the exact one, and a tile pruned only when the lower end of its interval distance exceeds $L+3r$) certifies all ten values of $\ell$ as lower bounds for the systoles. For the triangle orbifolds the computed systoles agree with the closed forms $2\operatorname{arccosh}(1+\cos\frac\pi4)$ and $2\operatorname{arccosh}(\frac12+\cos\frac\pi{12})$; for the family they equal $4b$ to $10^{-30}$, a numerical identification (Proposition~\ref{prop:N2} proves only that the systole is at most $4b$). A point of the double is within $\diam P$ of a vertex, which lies in both copies, so $\diam\Orb\le2\diam P$, and $\diam P$ is the largest distance between two vertices, in closed form; the computed diameter equals $\diam P$ in all ten cases.

\emph{The test.} $G_\sigma(t)$ is evaluated by Gauss--Legendre quadrature and checked against $30$-digit quadrature (worst difference $2.1\times10^{-15}$), and $\mathcal S$ is the set of all signatures of the same area with orders at most $M$. The time runs over $400$ log-spaced points in $[0.002,1]$ and $300$ in $[10^{-5},0.002)$, refined by $61$ points around each minimiser, and the infimum over $s$ over $240$ log-spaced values of $s/t$ in $[10^{-8},1]$; any $s$ gives a valid $E_N$. Table~\ref{tab:practice} reports for each count the geometric middle of the window of grid times at which it holds. Rounding in $\sum_je^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}$ and the quadrature error of $G_\sigma$ are not included in $E_N$; both are far below every margin. At every grid time and every $N$ in the complete range, neither criterion singles out a wrong signature, and in every case of Table~\ref{tab:practice} both criteria hold within the complete range.

\section{The hypotheses}\label{sec:hyp}

\subsection{The orders: high orders behave like cusps}

We use J{\o}rgensen's inequality \cite{jorgensen1976}, \cite[Ch.~5]{beardon1983} in the case of a hyperbolic element of a group without parabolic elements, and include a short proof of that case by J{\o}rgensen's argument.

\begin{lemma}[J{\o}rgensen's inequality, hyperbolic case]\label{lem:jorgensen}
Let $\Gamma\subset PSL(2,\R)$ be discrete with no parabolic elements, $A\in\Gamma$ hyperbolic and $B\in\Gamma$ an element that fixes neither fixed point of $A$ in $\partial\Hb$ and does not interchange them. Then, for any lifts to $SL(2,\R)$, $|\tr^2A-4|+|\tr[A,B]-2|\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conjugate so that $A=\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})$, $u>1$, with fixed points $0,\infty$, and let $B=\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{psmallmatrix}$. A direct computation gives $\tr[A,B]-2=-bc(u-u^{-1})^2$, so the claim is $\mu:=(u-u^{-1})^2(1+|bc|)\ge1$; suppose $\mu<1$. Let $B_0=B$, $B_{n+1}=B_nAB_n^{-1}=\begin{psmallmatrix}a_{n+1}&b_{n+1}\\c_{n+1}&d_{n+1}\end{psmallmatrix}$ and $x_n=b_nc_n$. Then
\[
  B_{n+1}=\begin{pmatrix}ua_nd_n-u^{-1}b_nc_n&-a_nb_n(u-u^{-1})\\c_nd_n(u-u^{-1})&-ub_nc_n+u^{-1}a_nd_n\end{pmatrix},\qquad x_{n+1}=-x_n(1+x_n)(u-u^{-1})^2 .
\]
So $|x_n|\le|x_0|$ implies $|x_{n+1}|\le\mu|x_n|$, and by induction $|x_n|\le\mu^n|x_0|\to0$. No $x_n$ vanishes: $x_0\ne0$ as $B$ fixes neither $0$ nor $\infty$; if $n\ge1$ is least with $x_n=0$, then $x_{n-1}=-1$, so $a_{n-1}d_{n-1}=0$; if both vanish, $B_{n-1}$ interchanges $0$ and $\infty$, which is excluded for $n-1=0$ and impossible for the hyperbolic $B_{n-1}$, $n-1\ge1$; if exactly one vanishes, $B_n$, a hyperbolic element with fixed points $B_{n-1}(0)$ and $B_{n-1}(\infty)$, shares exactly one fixed point with $A$, and then the commutator $[A,B_n]$ is a nontrivial parabolic element of $\Gamma$ (upper or lower unitriangular with a nonzero entry). Now $B_n$, $n\ge1$, is hyperbolic with repelling fixed point $p_n=B_{n-1}(0)=b_{n-1}/d_{n-1}$ and attracting fixed point $q_n=B_{n-1}(\infty)=a_{n-1}/c_{n-1}$, both finite and nonzero, with $p_n/q_n=x_{n-1}/(1+x_{n-1})\to0$, and with translation length that of $A$. Choose $k_n\in\Z$ with $u^{-2k_n}\in[\rho_n,u^2\rho_n)$, $\rho_n=|p_nq_n|^{-1/2}$, and put $C_n=A^{-k_n}B_nA^{k_n}\in\Gamma$; its fixed points $u^{-2k_n}p_n$ and $u^{-2k_n}q_n$ have moduli at most $u^2|p_n/q_n|^{1/2}\to0$ and at least $|q_n/p_n|^{1/2}\to\infty$. So $C_n\to A$ in $PSL(2,\R)$, while $C_n\ne A$ as $C_n$ does not fix $0$. This contradicts discreteness.
\end{proof}

For a rotation $\beta$ by $\phi$ about a point at distance $r$ from the axis of a hyperbolic $\gamma$ of translation length $L$, $\tr^2\gamma-4=4\sinh^2\frac L2$ and $\tr[\gamma,\beta]-2=4\sinh^2\frac L2\sin^2\frac\phi2\cosh^2r$ for any lifts: with $\gamma=\operatorname{diag}(e^{L/2},e^{-L/2})$ and $\beta=hkh^{-1}$, $k=\begin{psmallmatrix}\cos\frac\phi2&\sin\frac\phi2\\-\sin\frac\phi2&\cos\frac\phi2\end{psmallmatrix}$, $h=\begin{psmallmatrix}\cosh\frac r2&\sinh\frac r2\\\sinh\frac r2&\cosh\frac r2\end{psmallmatrix}$ (which moves $i$ along the unit circle, perpendicular to the axis, by $r$), the off-diagonal entries of $\beta$ are $\pm\sin\frac\phi2\cosh r$, so $bc=-\sin^2\frac\phi2\cosh^2r$, and $\tr[\gamma,\beta]-2=-bc(e^{L/2}-e^{-L/2})^2$.

\begin{proposition}[The family $\Orb(2,3,m)$]\label{prop:233}
For $m\ge7$ put $s_\infty=\operatorname{arccosh}\frac2{\sqrt3}$, $h_m=\operatorname{arccosh}\frac1{2\sin(\pi/m)}$ and $\ell_{2,3}=2\operatorname{arsinh}\big(2\sqrt{1+\frac34\cosh^2(2s_\infty)}\big)^{-1}=0.56206\ldots$. Then $\Area\Orb(2,3,m)=2\pi(\frac16-\frac1m)<\frac\pi3$, $\ell(\Orb(2,3,m))\ge\ell_{2,3}$, $\diam\Orb(2,3,m)\ge h_m\ge\log\frac m{2\pi}$, and the open ball of radius $h_m$ about the cone point of order $m$ is a hyperbolic cone of angle $2\pi/m$. So $\Orb(2,3,m)\in\Cl(\frac\pi3,\ell_{2,3},m)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Orb=\Orb(2,3,m)$ is the double of the triangle $T$ with vertices $v_2,v_3,v_m$ and angles $\frac\pi2,\frac\pi3,\frac\pi m$; its group $\Gamma_m$ is the orientation-preserving subgroup of the group $W$ generated by the reflections in the sides of $T$, of which $T$ is a fundamental domain \cite{beardon1983}, and the folding $f:\Hb\to T$, which sends each point to the unique point of $T$ in its $W$-orbit, is $1$-Lipschitz with $d(z,Wv)=d(f(z),v)$ for each vertex $v$, since $T$ meets each orbit once and each tile is isometric to $T$. By (H3), $\cosh d(v_2,v_3)=2\cos(\pi/m)/\sqrt3$, so $s_m=d(v_2,v_3)<s_\infty$, and $\cosh d(v_2,v_m)=1/(2\sin(\pi/m))$, so $d(v_2,v_m)=h_m$; the folding of $\Orb$ onto $T$ is $1$-Lipschitz and the identity on $T$, so the cone points of orders $2$ and $m$ are at distance $h_m$ in $\Orb$, and $h_m\ge\log\frac1{2\sin(\pi/m)}\ge\log\frac m{2\pi}$.
\emph{The cone ball.} Let $P$ be the union of the $2m$ tiles $gT$, $g\in W$, with vertex $v_m$, the translates under the stabiliser of $v_m$ in $\Gamma_m$ of the fundamental domain $T\cup s(T)$, $s$ the reflection in $[v_m,v_2]$. For $\gamma\in\Gamma_m$ outside that stabiliser, $\gamma P$ and $P$ have disjoint interiors. $P$ is star-shaped about $v_m$, and its boundary consists of images of $[v_2,v_3]$, at distance $h_m$ from $v_m$ because the angle of $T$ at $v_2$ is $\frac\pi2$. So $B(v_m,h_m)\subset\operatorname{int}P$, its translates are equal or disjoint, and its image is the cone of angle $2\pi/m$ and radius $h_m$.
\emph{The systole.} Let $\gamma\in\Gamma_m$ be hyperbolic with axis $\alpha$ and $L=\ell(\gamma)$. As $\alpha$ is connected and unbounded, it is not covered by the disjoint open balls $gB(v_m,h_m)$; take $\tilde x\in\alpha$ outside them and $x=f(\tilde x)$, so $d(x,v_m)\ge h_m$. Write $x\in[v_m,q]$ with $q\in[v_2,v_3]$; as $d(v_m,\cdot)$ is convex on $[v_2,v_3]$ with minimum $h_m$ at $v_2$, $d(x,q)\le d(v_m,v_3)-h_m\le s_m$, so $d(x,v_3)\le2s_m$. So some elliptic point $\tilde w\in Wv_3$, fixed by a rotation $\beta\in\Gamma_m$ by $\frac{2\pi}3$, lies at distance $r\le2s_m<2s_\infty$ from $\alpha$. The rotation $\beta$ fixes no point of $\partial\Hb$ and cannot interchange the endpoints of $\alpha$ (then $\beta^2$, a rotation of order $3$, would fix them), so Lemma~\ref{lem:jorgensen} and the trace identities give $4\sinh^2\frac L2(1+\frac34\cosh^2r)\ge1$, hence $L\ge\ell_{2,3}$.
\end{proof}

The bound $\ell_{2,3}$ is far from sharp. A floating-point search over words of length at most $24$ in the generators finds no closed geodesic shorter than $0.98399$ on $\Orb(2,3,7)$, which is $2\operatorname{arccosh}\frac{1+2\cos(2\pi/7)}2=0.983986\ldots$, and the shortest length found increases with $m$, to $1.9213$ at $m=100$, below the systole $2\operatorname{arccosh}\frac32=1.9248$ of the modular orbifold; this search is used in no proof.

On a region with metric $d\rho^2+w(\rho)^2d\vartheta^2$ and $w''=w$, the function $f=w^{-1/2}u(\rho)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rayleigh}
  f'^2w=u'^2+q\,u^2+\frac d{d\rho}\Big(-\frac{w'}{2w}u^2\Big),\qquad q=\frac12-\frac{w'^2}{4w^2}=\begin{cases}\frac14-\frac1{4\sinh^2\rho},&w=\sinh\rho,\\\frac14+\frac1{4\cosh^2\rho},&w=\cosh\rho,\end{cases}
\end{equation}
as $f'^2w=u'^2-\frac{w'}wuu'+\frac{w'^2}{4w^2}u^2$ and $\frac d{d\rho}(-\frac{w'}{2w}u^2)=-(\frac{w''}{2w}-\frac{w'^2}{2w^2})u^2-\frac{w'}wuu'$.

\begin{proposition}[Unbounded orders]\label{prop:N1}
For $m\ge7$ and $j\ge1$, $\lambda_j(\Orb(2,3,m))\le\frac14+\pi^2(j+1)^2/h_m^2$. Moreover, for every $N\ge1$ and $\delta>0$, among any $J+1$ distinct orders $m\ge7$, where $J=(\lfloor\Lambda_N/\delta\rfloor+1)^{N-1}$ and $\Lambda_N=\frac14+\pi^2N^2/h_7^2$, there are two, $m\ne m'$, with $|\lambda_j(\Orb(2,3,m))-\lambda_j(\Orb(2,3,m'))|<\delta$ for all $j<N$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the cone ball of Proposition~\ref{prop:233}, with metric $d\rho^2+\sinh^2\rho\,d\vartheta^2$, $\vartheta\in\R/\frac{2\pi}m\Z$, cut $[a,h_m]$, $0<a<h_m$, into $j+1$ intervals of length $\ell=(h_m-a)/(j+1)$ and let $f_i=(\sinh\rho)^{-1/2}u_i$ with $u_i$ a sine arch on the $i$-th interval, zero elsewhere. These Lipschitz functions have disjoint supports, and by \eqref{eq:rayleigh}, as $u_i$ vanishes at the ends and $q\le\frac14$, $\int|\nabla f_i|^2/\int f_i^2\le\frac14+\pi^2/\ell^2$. The min--max principle on their span gives the bound; let $a\to0$. As $h_m$ increases with $m$, $\lambda_j\in[0,\Lambda_N]$ for $j<N$, and two of $J+1$ vectors $(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{N-1})$ lie in a common product of the $\lfloor\Lambda_N/\delta\rfloor+1$ intervals $[i\delta,(i+1)\delta)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The order bound cannot be dropped]\label{rem:Mneeded}
Let $A\ge\frac\pi3$ and $\varepsilon\le\ell_{2,3}$. For every $N$ and $\delta$ there is $M$, for instance $M=(\lfloor\Lambda_N/\delta\rfloor+1)^{N-1}+7$, such that $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ contains $\Orb(2,3,m)$ and $\Orb(2,3,m')$, of different signatures, whose first $N$ eigenvalues agree to within $\delta$; whatever rule decides the signature from such data is wrong for one of them. So $N$ and $\delta$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:E} cannot depend on $A$ and $\varepsilon$ alone; an area bound and a systole bound do not bound the orders. For $A<\frac\pi3$ the question does not arise: then $g=0$ and $n=3$ by the case analysis of Lemma~\ref{lem:elem}, and a hyperbolic triad $p\le q\le r$ has $\frac1p+\frac1q\le\frac56$, so $\frac1r\ge1-\frac{\Area}{2\pi}-\frac56\ge\frac16-\frac A{2\pi}>0$ bounds $r$, and only finitely many triples occur.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[A constraint on $N$ and $\delta$]\label{cor:Nlower}
Let $A\ge\frac\pi3$ and $\varepsilon\le\ell_{2,3}$. If a rule decides the signature of every $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ from any $\tilde\lambda_0,\dots,\tilde\lambda_{N-1}$ with $|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j(\Orb)|<\delta/2$, then the pair $(N,\delta)$ satisfies $(\lfloor\Lambda_N/\delta\rfloor+1)^{N-1}\ge M-6$. In particular, if $M\ge7$, then $(N-1)\log(c'N^2/\delta+1)\ge\log(M-6)$ with $c'=\frac14+\pi^2/h_7^2=33.44\ldots$, so for fixed $\delta$, as $M\to\infty$, $N$ grows at least like $\log M/\log\log M$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The $M-6$ orbifolds $\Orb(2,3,m)$, $7\le m\le M$, lie in $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:233}). If $M-6\ge J+1$, Proposition~\ref{prop:N1} gives $m\ne m'$ among them whose first $N$ eigenvalues differ by less than $\delta$, so that one list of $\tilde\lambda_j$ is within $\delta/2$ of both, and the rule is wrong for one of them. So $M-6\le J$. With $\Lambda_N=\frac14+\pi^2N^2/h_7^2\le c'N^2$ for $N\ge1$, $(N-1)\log(c'N^2/\delta+1)\ge\log(M-6)$.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:Nlower} has content only when $\delta$ is not small: at an accuracy $\delta\le M^{-1}$ it yields only $N\ge2$, as $\Lambda_2>133$. Theorem~\ref{thm:E} works in that regime, its $\delta$ decreasing like a power of $M$ (Remark~\ref{rem:Mgrowth}); its mechanism gives no $N$ at an accuracy fixed independently of $M$, since the perturbation term, about $\delta\Area(\Orb)/4\pi$ at small $t$, must stay below the least gap between the signature terms over $\Sig(A,M)$. Whether some $\delta>0$ independent of $M$ admits an $N$, depending on $A$, $\varepsilon$, $M$ and $\delta$, that determines the signature in $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ for every $M$ is open; such an $N$ would grow at least like $\log M/\log\log M$.

\begin{remark}[How $N$ may grow with $M$]\label{rem:Mgrowth}
For fixed $A$ and $\varepsilon$, Theorem~\ref{thm:E} gives an $N$ polynomial in $M$. As $M\to\infty$, $k_*=n_*$; $b_K(M)=\pm p_K(M)/M$ with $p_K$ a polynomial of degree $2K+2$, so $Q(K)\le cM^{2K+1}$; $\delta_1\ge\frac12M^{-2n_*}$, so $\gamma_*\ge\frac12M^{-2n_*}$ for large $M$ and $t_1\ge cM^{-2n_*-1}$; the radii $r_0,\rho_1$ and the area $v_0$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:countK} are bounded below by powers of $1/M$, so $C_*$ and $\varpi$ are bounded by a power of $M$, $y_0=O(\log M)$ and $t_3\ge c/\log M$. Hence $t_*\ge cM^{-2n_*-1}$, $\Theta_*\ge cM^{-2n_*-(2n_*+1)(n_*-2)}$, $\Lambda$ and $N$ are $O(M^{2n_*+1}\log M)$, $Nt_*=O(\log M)$, and $\delta^{-1}=O(M^{2n_*+(2n_*+1)(n_*-2)}\log M)$, the constants depending on $A$ and $\varepsilon$. At $A=\frac\pi2$ ($n_*=4$) and $\varepsilon=1$ the computed exponents per doubling of $M$, for $M=64$, $128$, $256$ and $512$, decrease towards $9$ for $N$ (from $9.21$ to $9.17$) and towards $26$ for $\delta^{-1}$ (from $26.21$ to $26.17$), and $C_*$ grows like $M^9$. Without the bound $M^{2n_*}$ in $\delta_1$ the formulas would give an $N$ growing exponentially in $M$, as $L_M=e^{(1+o(1))M}$. This upper bound is for the pair $(N,\delta)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}, with $\delta^{-1}$ growing like a power of $M$; at such accuracies Corollary~\ref{cor:Nlower} yields only $N\ge2$, so the two bounds do not bracket one growth rate, and at a fixed $\delta$ we have no upper bound for $N$ at all.
\end{remark}

Figure~\ref{fig:E1} shows the computed eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_6$ of $\Orb(2,3,m)$ for $21$ orders from $7$ to $4096$, with the bounds of Proposition~\ref{prop:N1}; the Neumann and Dirichlet spectra of the triangle, computed at two mesh levels, agree to at least $2.3\times10^{-10}$ relative. Every computed eigenvalue lies below its bound and decreases in $m$ (an observation, not proved here), and $\lambda_1$ falls from $44.89$ at $m=7$ to $0.473$ at $m=4096$; the computed values suggest $\lambda_j\to\frac14$.

\begin{remark}[Elliptic degeneration, conditionally]\label{rem:elliptic}
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-cusp} uses only the upper bounds of Proposition~\ref{prop:N1}. Lower bounds would come from elliptic degeneration in the sense of Garbin and Jorgenson \cite[\S2]{garbinjorgenson2018}: their condition (a) holds for $m\ge13$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:233}, since $\frac1{2\sin(\pi/m)}\ge\frac m{2\pi}\ge1+\frac m{4\pi}$, but we have not verified their condition (b) for this family. If it holds, their Corollary~5.5, with the limit $PSL(2,\Z)\backslash\Hb$, which has no eigenvalue in $(0,\frac14)$, would give $\lambda_j(\Orb(2,3,m))\to\frac14$ for every $j\ge1$; at $m=4096$ the quantity $h_m^2(\lambda_j-\frac14)/\pi^2$ is still $6\%$ to $16\%$ above $j^2$, an observation and not a result.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The area: surfaces of large genus}\label{sec:area}

The bound on the area is needed for the same reason as the bound on the orders, and here no orbifold is needed: closed hyperbolic surfaces of large genus have large diameter, so their first eigenvalues are bounded, and those of systole at least $\varepsilon$ occur in infinitely many genera.

\begin{proposition}[The area bound cannot be dropped]\label{prop:area}
Every closed hyperbolic surface $S$ of genus $g$ has $\lambda_j(S)\le\frac14+4\pi^2j^2/\operatorname{arccosh}(2g-1)^2$ for $j\ge1$. For every $\varepsilon>0$, $N\ge1$ and $\delta>0$ there are two closed hyperbolic surfaces of different genus, each of systole greater than $\varepsilon$, whose first $N$ eigenvalues satisfy $|\lambda_j-\lambda'_j|<\delta$ for $j<N$. Both lie in $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ for every $M\ge2$ and every $A$ at least their areas. So in Theorem~\ref{thm:E} $N$ and $\delta$ cannot depend on $\varepsilon$ and $M$ alone, and no finite part of the spectrum, known to a fixed accuracy, bounds the area.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Eigenvalues and diameter.} Let $S$ have genus $g$ and diameter $d$. Cheng's comparison theorem \cite[Thm~2.1]{cheng1975} states that a compact $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature at least $(n-1)k$ and diameter $d$ has $m$-th eigenvalue, $0=\mu_0<\mu_1\le\cdots$, at most the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the ball of radius $d/2m$ in the simply connected $n$-dimensional space form of curvature $k$. Here $n=2$ and $k=-1$, and the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of a disc of radius $R$ in $\Hb$ is at most $\frac14+\pi^2/R^2$: this is the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:N1} with $\vartheta\in\R/2\pi\Z$, one sine arch on $[a,R]$ and $a\to0$, using \eqref{eq:rayleigh} with $w=\sinh\rho$ and $q\le\frac14$. So $\lambda_j(S)\le\frac14+4\pi^2j^2/d^2$. The covering map $\Hb\to S$, restricted to the ball of radius $d$ about a lift of a point of $S$, is onto and a local isometry, so $4\pi(g-1)=\Area(S)\le2\pi(\cosh d-1)$, that is, $d\ge\operatorname{arccosh}(2g-1)$.

\emph{Systole at least $\varepsilon$ in infinitely many genera.} Let $S_0=\Gamma_0\backslash\Hb$ be a closed hyperbolic surface, $F$ the closed Dirichlet domain of $\Gamma_0$ at a point $\tilde x_0$, and $D_0=\diam S_0$, so that $d(\tilde x_0,\tilde y)\le D_0$ for $\tilde y\in F$. The set $\Phi=\{\gamma\in\Gamma_0\setminus\{1\}:d(\tilde x_0,\gamma\tilde x_0)\le\varepsilon+2D_0\}$ is finite, as $\Gamma_0$ is discrete. Fundamental groups of closed surfaces are residually finite \cite{hempel1972}: for each $\gamma\in\Phi$ there is a homomorphism from $\Gamma_0$ to a finite group whose kernel does not contain $\gamma$, and the intersection $\Gamma_1$ of these kernels is a normal subgroup of finite index with $\Gamma_1\cap\Phi=\emptyset$. Every $\gamma\in\Gamma_1\setminus\{1\}$ is hyperbolic, as $\Gamma_0$ is cocompact and torsion-free. Take $\tilde y$ on its axis and $\eta\in\Gamma_0$ with $\eta\tilde y\in F$; then $\gamma'=\eta\gamma\eta^{-1}$ lies in $\Gamma_1$, as $\Gamma_1$ is normal, its axis passes through $\eta\tilde y$, and $d(\tilde x_0,\gamma'\tilde x_0)\le2d(\tilde x_0,\eta\tilde y)+\ell(\gamma)\le2D_0+\ell(\gamma)$. As $\gamma'\notin\Phi$, $\ell(\gamma)>\varepsilon$. So $S_1=\Gamma_1\backslash\Hb$ is a closed hyperbolic surface of systole greater than $\varepsilon$, of some genus $g_1\ge2$. For every integer $q\ge1$ the kernel $\Gamma_{1,q}$ of a surjection $\Gamma_1\to H_1(S_1;\Z)\cong\Z^{2g_1}\to\Z/q\Z$ has index $q$, so $S_{1,q}=\Gamma_{1,q}\backslash\Hb$ is a closed hyperbolic surface of area $q\Area(S_1)$, that is, of genus $1+q(g_1-1)$; its closed geodesics are hyperbolic classes of $\Gamma_{1,q}\subset\Gamma_1$, with the same lengths, so its systole is greater than $\varepsilon$ too.

\emph{Pigeonhole.} For $q\ge1$ and $1\le j<N$, the first step with $g\ge2$ gives $0<\lambda_j(S_{1,q})\le\Lambda'=\frac14+4\pi^2N^2/\operatorname{arccosh}(3)^2$. The vectors $(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{N-1})$ of the $S_{1,q}$, $q\ge1$, lie in the finitely many products of the $\lfloor\Lambda'/\delta\rfloor+1$ intervals $[i\delta,(i+1)\delta)$, so infinitely many of them lie in one product. Any two of the corresponding surfaces have different genera and $|\lambda_j-\lambda'_j|<\delta$ for $1\le j<N$, and $\lambda_0=\lambda'_0=0$; and as their areas are unbounded, eigenvalues known to within $\frac\delta2$ do not bound the area.
\end{proof}

As $g\to\infty$ the bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:area} tends to $\frac14$ for each fixed $j$. The two necessity results rest on the same pigeonhole argument; for the orders the new input is the uniform systole bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:233}, which gives Corollary~\ref{cor:Nlower}, while a quantitative form for the area would need the least genus of a closed hyperbolic surface of systole at least $\varepsilon$.

\subsection{The systole: an open question}\label{sec:systole}

Pinching a closed geodesic produces small eigenvalues, but this does not show that the systole bound is needed. Fix $k\in\{3,4\}$, $b>0$ and $a$ with $\sinh a\sinh b=\cos\frac\pi k$; in $\Hb$ take orthogonal lines $\beta,\beta'$ through $O$, and let $Q_{k,b}$ be the quadrilateral bounded by the two lines perpendicular to $\beta$ at distance $b$ from $O$ and the two perpendicular to $\beta'$ at distance $a$. In the hyperboloid, with $O=(1,0,0)$ and $\beta,\beta'$ in the planes $x_2=0$, $x_1=0$, the unit normals $(\sinh b,\cosh b,0)$ and $(\sinh a,0,\cosh a)$ of adjacent sides have inner product $-\sinh a\sinh b$, of modulus less than $1$, so these sides meet at an angle with cosine $\sinh a\sinh b$ or its negative (Lemma~\ref{lem:H}, proof of (H3)); the quarter of $Q_{k,b}$ cut out by $\beta,\beta'$ has three right angles and angle sum less than $2\pi$, so its fourth angle is acute, equal to $\frac\pi k$. Let $\Orb_{k,b}$ be the double of $Q_{k,b}$, a sphere with four cone points of order $k$. For $k=3$ these are the orbifolds $\Orb_\vartheta$ of Section~\ref{sec:practice}, with $\vartheta=\log(\sinh a/\sinh b)$, so Table~\ref{tab:practice} runs the test along this pinching family.

\begin{proposition}[Pinching]\label{prop:N2}
$\Orb_{k,b}$ contains an embedded collar $\{|\rho|<a\}\times\R/4b\Z$, with metric $d\rho^2+\cosh^2\rho\,ds^2$, about a separating closed geodesic of length $4b$; so $\ell(\Orb_{k,b})\le4b$ (and, by the symmetry $a\leftrightarrow b$, $\ell(\Orb_{k,b})\le4\min(a,b)$; Section~\ref{sec:practice} uses $b\le a$), $\lambda_1(\Orb_{k,b})\le\sinh a/((a^2+2)\sinh a-2a\cosh a)$ and $\lambda_j(\Orb_{k,b})\le\frac14+\frac1{4\cosh^2(a/2)}+\frac{4\pi^2(j+1)^2}{a^2}$. As $b\to0$, $\lambda_1\to0$ and $\limsup\lambda_j\le\frac14$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In Fermi coordinates $(\rho,s)$ about $\beta$ the sides perpendicular to $\beta$ are $s=\pm b$, and the other two sides are at distance at least $a$ from $\beta$, their common perpendiculars with $\beta$ having length $a$; so $\{|\rho|<a,|s|\le b\}$ lies in $Q_{k,b}$, and its double is the collar, whose core, the double of $\beta\cap Q_{k,b}$, separates the two pairs of cone points. The reflection in $\beta$ induces an isometry exchanging the two sides. For $\lambda_1$ take $f=\rho/a$ on the collar and $\pm1$ outside, odd under that isometry, so $\int f=0$, and compute the Rayleigh quotient, keeping in $\int f^2$ only the part over the collar, which is why the bound depends neither on $k$ nor on the area; for $\lambda_j$ use \eqref{eq:rayleigh} with $w=\cosh\rho$ and $j+1$ sine arches on disjoint intervals of length $a/(2(j+1))$ in $[\frac a2,a)$, where $q\le\frac14+\frac1{4\cosh^2(a/2)}$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:N2} is the classical collar argument \cite{buser1992}, included for its explicit constants; by the symmetry $a\leftrightarrow b$, $\lambda_1(\Orb_{k,b})\to0$ also as $b\to\infty$.

\begin{problem}[Is a systole bound needed?]\label{prob:systole}
Fix $A$ and $M$. (a) Is there an $N$, independent of $\varepsilon$, such that orbifolds in $\Cl(A,0^+,M)=\bigcup_{\varepsilon>0}\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ with the same first $N$ eigenvalues have the same signature? (b) Can $N$ and $\delta>0$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:E} both be chosen independently of $\varepsilon$?
\end{problem}

Part (a) is a loose signature-level analogue of the conjecture of Buser and Courtois \cite[pp.~523--524]{busercourtois1990}: the signature is determined by the heat invariants, while their conjecture concerns full isospectrality. With $A$ and $M$ fixed only finitely many signatures occur, so the pigeonhole argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:N1} is not available. A negative answer to (b) would follow from two degenerating families of different signatures and equal area, with the same number of eigenvalues tending to $0$ and cusped limits with the same eigenvalues below $\frac14$. Signatures of the same area degenerate differently: $(0;3,3,3,3)$, $(1;3)$ and $(0;2,2,6,6)$ all have area $\frac{4\pi}3$, but on $(1;3)$ every essential simple closed curve is non-separating, so we expect $\lambda_1$ to stay away from $0$ under pinching (we do not prove it), while $(0;3,3,3,3)$ pinched along a separating curve has $\lambda_1\to0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:N2}). The natural candidate pair is $(0;3,3,3,3)$ and $(0;2,2,6,6)$, pinched along separating curves to two copies of $(0;3,3,\infty)$, respectively of $(0;2,6,\infty)$; it needs the spectra of these cusped limits below $\frac14$ and a lower bound $\lambda_j\ge\frac14-o(1)$ for $2\le j<N$ along both families. For surfaces such bounds are theorems \cite{hjl1997,otalrosas2009,bmm2017}; their orbifold versions we have not carried out. The growth of our $N$ as $\varepsilon\to0$ (Section~\ref{sec:E}) is a property of the formulas, not of the least admissible $N$.

\section{The enclosures of the certificate}\label{app:certificate}

Table~\ref{tab:certencl} lists, for each class of Table~\ref{tab:certgap}, the enclosures computed for Proposition~\ref{prop:certgap}, rounded to ten significant digits. With them the certificate can be checked without rerunning the integration: enumerate $\Sig(A,M)$ from $n+4g\le A/\pi+4$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:elem}); form $G_\sigma(t)=\frac{s(\sigma)}2\mathrm I_1(t)+\sum_i\mathrm E_{m_i}(t)$, with an error of at most $\frac{s(\sigma)}2$ units of the last digit of $\mathrm I_1(t)$ plus one unit of the last digit of each $\mathrm E_{m_i}(t)$; check that every pairwise difference, less the two errors, is at least the $\Theta$ of Table~\ref{tab:certgap}; and check the inequalities of Corollary~\ref{cor:computedgap} from the closed forms of $C_*$, $B_*(t)$, $\cZ^\sharp$ and $\Lambda$, with the margins of Table~\ref{tab:certdetail}. The program \texttt{certified\_gap.py} carries out the first three steps in exact rational arithmetic from the printed decimals, as well as in ball arithmetic.
