\abstract{The Laplace spectrum of a closed orientable hyperbolic $2$-orbifold determines its signature, the genus and the orders of its cone points, but a measurement or a computation yields only finitely many eigenvalues, each to some accuracy. We show that this suffices, effectively: among orbifolds of area at most $A$, systole at least $\varepsilon$ and cone orders at most $M$, the first $N$ eigenvalues, each known to within $\delta$, determine the signature, with $N$ and $\delta$ given by explicit formulas. The closed geodesics are counted from the area, the systole and the orders alone, without a diameter bound. The constants are very large. On ten computed spectra, an a-posteriori version of the argument, using only the exact area, a systole bound and the order bound, is satisfied with $N=24$ to $842$ (the test with $N$ uses the eigenvalues up to $\tilde\lambda_N$), against $4\times10^4$ to $7\times10^{12}$ for the a-priori theorem; here the systole bound is computed in high-precision floating point, and the eigenvalue errors and the completeness of the computed lists, which the test presupposes, are estimated, not proved, so these are consistency checks on orbifolds of known signature, not proved determinations. The bound on the orders cannot be dropped: the orbifolds $\mathcal O(2,3,m)$ acquire arbitrarily many eigenvalues below any level above $\frac14$.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A drum is heard through its tones, and a closed hyperbolic surface or orbifold through the eigenvalues $0=\lambda_0<\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots$ of its Laplacian. For a closed orientable hyperbolic $2$-orbifold $\Orb$ whose singular points are cone points, the whole spectrum determines the \emph{signature} $\sigma(\Orb)=(g;m_1,\dots,m_n)$, the genus of the underlying surface and the multiset of cone orders \cite[Thm~1.1, Prop.~3.3]{drydenstrohmaier2009}; isospectral orbifolds of the same signature need not be isometric \cite[Thm~A]{linowitzvoight2015}. An experiment or a computation, however, delivers finitely many eigenvalues, each with an error. This paper asks whether such data determine the signature, and with how many eigenvalues and what accuracy.

The answer is yes, with explicit numbers, once three quantities are bounded: the area, from below the systole (the least length of a closed geodesic), and from above the cone orders. Write $\Sig$ for the class of closed orientable hyperbolic $2$-orbifolds whose singular points are cone points, and, for $A,\varepsilon>0$ and an integer $M\ge2$,
\[
  \Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)=\{\Orb\in\Sig:\ \Area(\Orb)\le A,\ \ell(\Orb)\ge\varepsilon,\ \text{all cone orders at most }M\},
\]
with $\ell(\Orb)$ the systole; $\Sig(A,M)$ denotes the finite set of signatures of area at most $A$ with orders at most $M$.

\begin{theorem}[Finitely many eigenvalues]\label{thm:intro}
For $A>0$, $\varepsilon>0$ and an integer $M\ge2$, the numbers $N\ge1$ and $\delta>0$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}, explicit in $A$, $\varepsilon$ and $M$, have the following property. If $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ and $\tilde\lambda_0,\dots,\tilde\lambda_{N-1}\ge0$ satisfy $|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j(\Orb)|\le\delta$, where $\lambda_0\le\lambda_1\le\dots$ are all the eigenvalues of $\Orb$, counted with multiplicity, so that none of the first $N$ is omitted, then $\sigma(\Orb)$ is the unique signature $\sigma\in\Sig(A,M)$ that minimises $|\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt_*}-G_\sigma(t_*)|$, where $G_\sigma$ is the signature term of the trace formula (Section~\ref{sec:heat}) and $t_*$ the time of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}. In particular two orbifolds of $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ whose first $N$ eigenvalues agree to within $\delta$ have the same signature.
\end{theorem}

\emph{What effectivity adds.} Without constants the statement follows from compactness. Fix two signatures $\sigma\ne\sigma'$ in $\Sig(A,M)$. The orbifolds of signature $\sigma$ with systole at least $\varepsilon$ form a compact subset of moduli space, by Mumford's theorem \cite[Cor.~3]{mumford1971}, which Bers extended to Fuchsian groups with elliptic elements \cite{bers1972}; each $\lambda_j$ is continuous there (nearby orbifolds are related by bi-Lipschitz orbifold maps, sending cone points to cone points of the same order, with constants near $1$, and the min-max characterisation of $\lambda_j$ changes by at most the corresponding factors), and two orbifolds of different signatures have different spectra \cite{drydenstrohmaier2009}, \cite[Thm~1]{doylerossetti2011}. Covering the compact product of the two sets by the open sets where some $\lambda_j$ differ gives a finite $N$; for this $N$ the function $\max_{j<N}|\lambda_j(\Orb)-\lambda_j(\Orb')|$ is continuous and positive on the compact product, so it has a positive minimum $2\delta$; and $\Sig(A,M)$ is finite. This argument gives no value of $N$ or $\delta$, and no way to check a given spectrum. Theorem~\ref{thm:E} gives both numbers as formulas. They are enormous (Table~\ref{tab:E}), but the same mechanism, run at one moderate time on the data instead of on worst-case bounds, gives a test that a computed spectrum can pass.

\begin{theorem}[A-posteriori test]\label{thm:intro-post}
Let $\Orb\in\Sig$ have area $A$, systole at least $\ell$, at most $Ce^x$ closed geodesics of length at most $x$ (for instance $C=C_*(A,\ell,M)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:countK} when the orders are at most $M$), and signature in a finite set $\mathcal S$ of signatures of area $A$. Given approximations $\tilde\lambda_0,\dots,\tilde\lambda_N$ of its first $N+1$ eigenvalues $\lambda_0\le\dots\le\lambda_N$, counted with multiplicity and none omitted, with $|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j|\le\epsilon_j$, an explicit error budget $E_N(t)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:post}) has the property: if, at some $t>0$, exactly one $\sigma\in\mathcal S$ satisfies $|\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}-G_\sigma(t)|\le E_N(t)$, then $\sigma(\Orb)=\sigma$; a stronger criterion, (C2), uses the gaps between the $G_\sigma$.
\end{theorem}

The test needs the exact area, a lower bound for the systole, a count of the closed geodesics, the completeness of the computed list up to $\tilde\lambda_N$, and error bounds $\epsilon_j$. We ran it on computed spectra of $\Orb(2,8,8)$, $\Orb(3,3,12)$ and eight orbifolds of signature $(0;3,3,3,3)$ (Section~\ref{sec:practice}), with a systole bound from a complete enumeration of short group elements in $40$- and $50$-digit floating point (not interval arithmetic) and eigenvalue errors that are a-posteriori estimates, not enclosures. With the class-level count of Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}, which uses nothing about the instance beyond the area, the systole bound and the order bound, criterion (C1) holds for all ten with $N=24$ to $842$, for order bounds $M=3$ and $M=12$ alike, while Theorem~\ref{thm:E} asks for $4\times10^4$ to $7\times10^{12}$; with a diameter bound for the instance the counts are $18$ to $847$ (Table~\ref{tab:practice}). These are consistency checks on orbifolds whose signature is known, with estimated errors and lists assumed complete, not determinations of unknown signatures.

The proof runs through the heat trace. By the Selberg trace formula the heat trace of $\Orb$ is a term $G_\sigma(t)$ fixed by the signature plus a positive contribution of the closed geodesics (Theorem~\ref{thm:trace}); the small-$t$ expansion of $G_\sigma$ has coefficients, the heat invariants, that are integer combinations of explicit rationals. At the first heat invariant where two signatures differ, the difference is an integer multiple of an explicit rational number (Lemma~\ref{lem:int}), and with orders at most $M$ that invariant is the $M$-th one at the latest (Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded}). Explicit, enveloping remainders for both expansions (Proposition~\ref{prop:remcone}) turn this into a lower bound for $|G_\sigma(t)-G_{\sigma'}(t)|$ at a small explicit time; a count of the closed geodesics in terms of $A$, $\varepsilon$, $M$ alone (Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}), from the separation of the cone points and a packing argument, controls them there, and the counting function controls the eigenvalues beyond the $N$-th.

The three bounds are not all of the same kind. The method uses each of them, the area through the finiteness of $\Sig(A,M)$ and the count of closed geodesics, the systole through the closed geodesics; but the bound on the orders is also needed for the statement itself.

\begin{theorem}[High orders behave like cusps]\label{thm:intro-cusp}
The triangle orbifolds $\Orb(2,3,m)$, $m\ge7$, have area less than $\frac\pi3$ and systole at least $0.5620$, and $\lambda_j(\Orb(2,3,m))\le\frac14+\pi^2(j+1)^2/h_m^2$ with $h_m=\operatorname{arccosh}\frac1{2\sin(\pi/m)}\ge\log\frac m{2\pi}$. Consequently, for $A\ge\frac\pi3$ and $\varepsilon\le0.5620$, there are no $N$ and $\delta$, depending only on $A$ and $\varepsilon$, such that any two orbifolds of area at most $A$ and systole at least $\varepsilon$ whose first $N$ eigenvalues agree to within $\delta$ have the same signature.
\end{theorem}

The cone point of order $m$ sits at the tip of a cone of angle $2\pi/m$ and radius $h_m$, which for large $m$ looks like the end of a cusp, and the eigenvalues below any fixed level above $\frac14$ multiply, as near the bottom $\frac14$ of the continuous spectrum of a cusp; Figure~\ref{fig:E1} shows the computed eigenvalues. Whether the systole bound is needed we do not know (Problem~\ref{prob:systole}).

\emph{Relation to prior work.} The closest result is the theorem of Buser and Courtois \cite[(1.1)]{busercourtois1990}: among closed hyperbolic surfaces of genus $g$ with injectivity radius at least $\varepsilon$ there is an integer $m(g,\varepsilon)$ such that two surfaces with the same first $m$ eigenvalues are isospectral. Its proof uses compactness and gives no value of $m$, and the authors conjecture that $m$ can be chosen depending on $g$ alone \cite[pp.~523--524]{busercourtois1990}. Theorem~\ref{thm:intro} is an effective counterpart at the level of the signature, for orbifolds, with approximate eigenvalues; Problem~\ref{prob:systole} below is the analogue of their conjecture. Effectivity is the point: Theorem~\ref{thm:E} turns the compactness argument into a finite procedure that decides the signature from finitely many approximate eigenvalues with stated accuracy. Compactness and finiteness of isospectral sets are classical: an isospectral set of closed hyperbolic surfaces is finite \cite{mckean1972}, and so is one of hyperbolic orbisurfaces \cite{dryden2004}; isospectral metrics on a surface form a compact set \cite{ops1988}, and isospectral manifolds under geometric bounds fall into finitely many diffeomorphism types \cite{bpp1992}. For orbifolds, the whole spectrum together with a lower curvature bound allows only finitely many isotropy types and bounds the number of singular points \cite[Main Thms~1--2]{stanhope2005}, and an isospectral family of $2$-orbifolds with a lower curvature bound contains only finitely many diffeomorphism types, a consequence of the finiteness under lower bounds on curvature and volume and an upper bound on the diameter \cite{proctorstanhope2010}; Theorem~\ref{thm:diam} supplies such a diameter bound from the area, the systole and the orders, and the orbifolds of Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-cusp} escape it, their diameters at least $\log\frac m{2\pi}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:233}). Isospectral orbifolds can have different maximal isotropy orders \cite{rsw2008}, though not in dimension $2$ at curvature $-1$, where the spectrum determines the number of cone points of each order \cite{drydenstrohmaier2009}, \cite[Thm~1]{doylerossetti2011}; Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-cusp} shows that no number of eigenvalues known to a fixed accuracy, chosen in terms of the area and the systole alone, bounds the orders. Whether the bound on the area can be dropped, with $N$ and $\delta$ depending on $\varepsilon$ and $M$ only, we do not know. Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-cusp} is an instance of elliptic degeneration, in which cone points of growing order become cusps: Garbin and Jorgenson prove that along such sequences the eigenvalues below $\frac14$ converge to those of the cusped limit \cite[Cor.~5.5]{garbinjorgenson2018}, as do the spectral counting functions pointwise \cite[Thm~6.5]{garbinjorgenson2020}, while the number of eigenvalues below a fixed level above $\frac14$ grows without bound \cite[Thm~5.3]{garbinjorgenson2018} (Proposition~\ref{prop:N1} below gives a lower bound of order $\log m$ for the family used here); the accumulation of spectral data for Hecke triangle groups as the order grows goes back to Selberg and Hejhal \cite{hejhal1983}. What Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-cusp} adds to this is a systole bound uniform in $m$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:233}), which keeps the family in one class $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,\cdot)$, and the explicit eigenvalue bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:N1}; the counterexample then follows by pigeonhole. For pinched geodesics, small eigenvalues vary continuously and converge to those of the cusped limit \cite{hejhal1990}, \cite[p.~264]{ji1993}, and infinitely many eigenvalues accumulate at $\frac14$, indeed cluster at every point of $[\frac14,\infty)$ \cite[p.~67]{wolpert1992a}, \cite{wolpert1992b}, \cite{ji1993}; this is the background of Section~\ref{sec:systole}. Computed hyperbolic spectra have been checked against the Selberg trace formula before: Strohmaier and Uski use the heat trace to check the completeness of computed eigenvalue lists of compact surfaces \cite[\S7]{strohmaieruski2013}, and Booker, Str\"ombergsson and Venkatesh verify computed Maass forms rigorously \cite{bsv2006}. The test of Section~\ref{sec:practice} applies the same idea to test a signature, and our completeness check follows the idea of Strohmaier and Uski, without their rigorous control of the tail. Effective rigidity of another kind is known for arithmetic surfaces: the length sets of two incommensurable ones of bounded area differ at a length bounded explicitly in terms of the area \cite{lmpt2018}; and for cofinite Fuchsian groups the constant in the prime geodesic theorem has an effective bound \cite{fjk2011}, which depends on the group, while Lemma~\ref{lem:countK} counts closed geodesics, more crudely, in terms of $A$, $\varepsilon$ and $M$ alone. For the geometric input, Buser's book bounds the number of closed geodesics of length at most $L$ on a closed surface of genus $g$ that are not iterates of very short ones by $(g-1)e^{L+6}$, in terms of the genus alone \cite[Lemma~6.6.4]{buser1992}; our count of closed geodesics (Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}) uses instead the separation of the cone points and a packing argument, and depends on the area, the systole and the orders. The heat expansion of an orbifold is local \cite{donnelly1976}, \cite[Thm~4.8]{dggw2008}, at constant curvature its coefficients are known in closed form \cite[Thm~4.20]{ucar2017}, and the spectrum determines the signature \cite{drydenstrohmaier2009}, \cite[Thm~1]{doylerossetti2011}; for Euclidean triangles, a triangle that shares its first $N$ Dirichlet eigenvalues with a given one, $N$ depending on the first two, is isospectral to it \cite[Thm~1]{changdeturck1989}.

\section{The heat trace and its expansion}\label{sec:heat}

This section and the next state the results shared with the companion paper (Table~\ref{tab:shared}); Lemma~\ref{lem:counting} is proved here, and the others in Appendix~\ref{app:shared}.

\subsection{Signatures and the trace formula}

Let $\Orb\in\Sig$ have signature $\sigma=(g;m_1,\dots,m_n)$, $m_i\ge2$. It is hyperbolic exactly when $s(\sigma)=2g-2+\sum_i(1-\frac1{m_i})>0$, and then $\Area(\Orb)=2\pi s(\sigma)$ \cite[13.3.4--13.3.6]{thurston1980}, \cite[Thm~3.2]{drydenstrohmaier2009}. Write $\Orb=\Gamma\backslash\Hb$ with $\Gamma\subset PSL(2,\R)$ cocompact, so $\Gamma$ has no parabolic elements. Put $\hat g_t(r)=e^{-t(1/4+r^2)}$ and $g_t(u)=e^{-t/4}e^{-u^2/4t}/\sqrt{4\pi t}$, so that $\hat g_t(r)=\int g_t(u)e^{iru}du$. For hyperbolic $\gamma\in\Gamma$ let $\ell(\gamma)$ be its translation length and $\gamma=\gamma_0^k$ with $\gamma_0$ primitive; a \emph{closed geodesic} is a hyperbolic conjugacy class, and the systole $\ell(\Orb)$ is the least $\ell(\gamma)$. $\cZ_\Orb(t)=\sum_je^{-\lambda_jt}$ is the heat trace.

\begin{theorem}[Trace formula for the heat function {\cite[Thm~\ref*{A-thm:IEH}, Lemma~\ref*{A-lem:admissible}]{companionA}}]\label{thm:trace}
For every $t>0$, $\cZ_\Orb(t)=\mathrm I(t)+\sum_i\mathrm E_{m_i}(t)+\Hyp(t)$, all series and integrals converging absolutely, where
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm I(t)&=\frac{\Area(\Orb)}{4\pi}\int_\R r\tanh(\pi r)\,\hat g_t(r)\,dr,\qquad
  \Hyp(t)=\sum_{[\gamma]}\frac{\ell(\gamma_0)\,g_t(\ell(\gamma))}{2\sinh(\ell(\gamma)/2)}>0,\\
  \mathrm E_m(t)&=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac1{2m\sin(\pi j/m)}\int_\R\frac{e^{-2\pi jr/m}\,\hat g_t(r)}{1+e^{-2\pi r}}\,dr .
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

This is the Selberg trace formula for cocompact Fuchsian groups with elliptic elements \cite[eq.~(1)]{drydenstrohmaier2009}, after \cite{hejhal1976,iwaniec2002}, extended to the heat function by the approximation argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:admissibleB}; each nontrivial element of the stabiliser of a cone point is counted once, and $\mathrm E_m(t)\to b_0(m)=\frac{m^2-1}{12m}$ as $t\downarrow0$. Garbin and Jorgenson state the formula with the same normalisation \cite[Rem.~2.7, (2.8)]{garbinjorgenson2020}. For a signature $\sigma$ put $G_\sigma(t)=\mathrm I(t)+\sum_i\mathrm E_{m_i}(t)$, the part of $\cZ_\Orb$ fixed by $\sigma$.

\begin{lemma}[Counting closed geodesics and the hyperbolic term]\label{lem:counting}\label{lem:hyp}
Let $n_\Orb(x)$ be the number of closed geodesics of $\Orb$ (hyperbolic conjugacy classes, so oriented and not necessarily primitive) of length at most $x$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $n_\Orb(x)\le\pi e^{x+3\diam}/\Area(\Orb)$, with $\diam$ the diameter of $\Orb$.
\item Suppose $n_\Orb(x)\le Ce^x$ for all $x>0$, and let $\ell>0$ be at most the systole. For $0<t\le\ell^2/(2(1+\ell))$,
\[
  0<\Hyp(t)\le B_C(\ell,t):=\frac{C\,\ell e^{\ell/2}}{1-e^{-\ell}}\Big(1+\frac{2t}{\ell-t}\Big)\frac{e^{-\ell^2/4t}}{\sqrt{4\pi t}},
\]
and on this range $B_C$ decreases with $\ell$. For every $t>0$, $0<\Hyp(t)\le\frac{2C}{\sqrt\pi(1-e^{-\ell})}\,e^{\frac{17t}4-\frac12-\ell}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Let $F$ be the Dirichlet domain at a point $\tilde x_0$ fixed by no nontrivial element; $F\subset\bar B(\tilde x_0,\diam)$. Every hyperbolic class has a representative $\gamma$ whose axis meets $F$, at $\tilde y$ say, and $d(\tilde x_0,\gamma\tilde x_0)\le2d(\tilde x_0,\tilde y)+d(\tilde y,\gamma\tilde y)\le\ell(\gamma)+2\diam$. The translates $\gamma F$ with $d(\tilde x_0,\gamma\tilde x_0)\le x+2\diam$ have disjoint interiors, area $\Area(\Orb)$ each, and lie in $B(\tilde x_0,x+3\diam)$, of area $2\pi(\cosh(x+3\diam)-1)\le\pi e^{x+3\diam}$.
(ii) With $\ell(\gamma_0)\le\ell(\gamma)$, $e^{-t/4}\le1$ and $2\sinh(x/2)\ge e^{x/2}(1-e^{-\ell})$ for $x\ge\ell$, $\Hyp(t)\le((1-e^{-\ell})\sqrt{4\pi t})^{-1}\int_{[\ell,\infty)}\phi\,dn_\Orb$ with $\phi(x)=xe^{-x/2-x^2/4t}$, and $n_\Orb=0$ below $\ell$. On the range, $(\log\phi)'=\frac1x-\frac12-\frac x{2t}<0$ for $x\ge\ell$, so integrating by parts, $\int\phi\,dn_\Orb=\int_\ell^\infty n_\Orb(-\phi')\,dx\le C\int_\ell^\infty e^x(-\phi')\,dx=C\big(e^\ell\phi(\ell)+\int_\ell^\infty xe^{x/2-x^2/4t}dx\big)$. Since $xe^{x/2-x^2/4t}=-\frac{2tx}{x-t}\frac d{dx}e^{x/2-x^2/4t}$ and $\frac x{x-t}\le\frac\ell{\ell-t}$ for $x\ge\ell$, the integral is at most $\frac{2t\ell}{\ell-t}e^{\ell/2-\ell^2/4t}$, which gives $B_C$. The logarithmic derivative of $B_C$ in $\ell$ is $-\frac12-(\frac\ell{2t}-\frac{1+\ell}\ell)-\frac{e^{-\ell}}{1-e^{-\ell}}-\frac{2t}{\ell^2-t^2}<0$, the bracket being nonnegative exactly on the range. For every $t>0$, $xe^{-x/2-x^2/4t}=(xe^{-x^2/8t})(e^{3x/2-x^2/8t})e^{-2x}\le2\sqrt t\,e^{-1/2}e^{9t/2}e^{-2x}$ for $x\ge0$ (maxima at $x=2\sqrt t$ and $x=6t$), so $\Hyp(t)\le\frac{2\sqrt t\,e^{-1/2}e^{9t/2}e^{-t/4}}{(1-e^{-\ell})\sqrt{4\pi t}}\sum_{[\gamma]}e^{-2\ell(\gamma)}$, and $\sum_{[\gamma]}e^{-2\ell(\gamma)}=2\int_\ell^\infty n_\Orb(x)e^{-2x}dx\le2Ce^{-\ell}$.
\end{proof}

Part (i) with $C=\pi e^{3\diam}/\Area(\Orb)$ is the count used when a diameter bound for the instance is known (Section~\ref{sec:practice}); Lemma~\ref{lem:countK} gives a count in terms of the area, the systole and the orders alone, which is the one Theorem~\ref{thm:E} uses.

\subsection{The cone and area terms}

Write $\theta_j=\pi j/m$ and, for an integer $m\ge1$, $\Phi_m(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(4m\sin\theta_j\sin(\theta_j-u))^{-1}=\sum_k\phi_k(m)u^{2k}$, analytic and even in $|u|<\pi/m$. Every $\phi_k(m)$ is a positive combination of the numbers $m^{2n-1}-m^{-1}$, $1\le n\le k+1$, hence positive and strictly increasing in $m>1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:PhiB}; \cite[Lemma~\ref*{A-lem:Phi}]{companionA}). Put
\[
  b_l(m)=\frac{(-1)^lp_l(m)}m,\quad p_l(m)=\frac1{4^l}\sum_{k=0}^l\frac{(2k)!}{k!\,(l-k)!}\,m\,\phi_k(m),
\]
\[
  \alpha_k=\frac{(-1)^k}{k!\,4^k}\sum_{l=0}^k\binom kl(-4)^lB_{2l}\big(\tfrac12\big),
\]
with $B_{2l}(x)$ the Bernoulli polynomials; $\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_3=1,-\frac13,\frac1{15},-\frac4{315}$.

\begin{proposition}[Enveloping remainders {\cite[Prop.~\ref*{A-prop:envelope}]{companionA}}]\label{prop:remcone}\label{prop:remarea}
Let $K\ge0$ and $t>0$. (a) For every integer $m\ge2$, $\mathrm E_m(t)-\sum_{l<K}b_l(m)t^l$ has the sign $(-1)^K$ or vanishes, and its modulus is at most $|b_K(m)|\,t^K$; and $|b_K(m)|$ is strictly increasing in $m$. (b) $\frac{4\pi}{\Area}\mathrm I(t)-\sum_{k=0}^K\alpha_kt^{k-1}$ has the sign $(-1)^{K+1}$ or vanishes, and its modulus is at most $|\alpha_{K+1}|\,t^K$.
\end{proposition}

So $\cZ_\Orb(t)\sim\sum_{j\ge1}c_j(\Orb)t^{j-2}$ as $t\downarrow0$, with the \emph{heat invariants}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cj}
  c_1=\frac{\Area}{4\pi},\qquad c_j=\alpha_{j-1}\frac{\Area}{4\pi}+\sum_{i=1}^nb_{j-2}(m_i)\quad(j\ge2),
\end{equation}
functions $c_j(\sigma)$ of the signature, and for $K\ge0$ and every $t>0$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Grem}
  \Big|G_\sigma(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{K+1}c_j(\sigma)\,t^{j-2}\Big|\le t^K\Big(\frac{\Area}{4\pi}|\alpha_{K+1}|+\sum_i|b_K(m_i)|\Big).
\end{equation}
The polynomial $p_l$ is even, with rational coefficients, of degree exactly $2l+2$, with $p_l(1)=0$ and leading coefficient $a_l=|B_{2l+2}|/(2(l+1)!\,(2l+1))$; and $p_l(x)/x=\sum_{k=1}^{l+1}a_{l,k}\psi_k(x)$ with $\psi_k(x)=x^{2k-1}-x^{-1}$ and $a_{l,l+1}=a_l$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:conepolyB}; \cite[Lemmas~\ref*{A-lem:conepoly}, \ref*{A-lem:triangular}]{companionA}), which is the mechanism of U\c{c}ar's induction \cite[Thm~3.40]{ucar2017}.

\section{Heat invariants of a signature}\label{sec:sig}

Write $R=\sum_i1/m_i$, $P_k=\sum_im_i^k$, $\Psi_k(m)=\sum_i\psi_k(m_i)$ and $H_L(\sigma)=(c_1,\dots,c_L)$.

\begin{lemma}[Heat data of a signature {\cite[Lemma~\ref*{A-lem:sigdata}]{companionA}}]\label{lem:sigdata}
Let $\sigma=(g;m)$, $\sigma'=(g';m')$ be hyperbolic, with $n$ and $n'$ cone points, and $L\ge1$. Then $H_L(\sigma)=H_L(\sigma')$ if and only if $2g+n-R(m)=2g'+n'-R(m')$ and $\Psi_k(m)=\Psi_k(m')$ for $1\le k\le L-1$. In that case $d=R(m')-R(m)\in\Z$, and the paddings $U=m\uplus\{1\}^{\max(d,0)}$, $V=m'\uplus\{1\}^{\max(-d,0)}$ satisfy $R(U)=R(V)$ and $P_j(U)=P_j(V)$ for odd $j\le2L-3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Separation and bounded orders {\cite[Cor.~\ref*{A-cor:sigarea}, Thm~\ref*{A-thm:bounded}]{companionA}}]\label{thm:sep}\label{thm:bounded}
(i) Two hyperbolic signatures of area $A$ that share $c_1,\dots,c_L$ with $L\ge\lfloor A/\pi\rfloor+4$ are equal. (ii) Two hyperbolic signatures with all cone orders at most $M$ that share $c_1,\dots,c_M$ are equal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Integrality at the first difference]\label{lem:int}
Let $\sigma\ne\sigma'$ be hyperbolic signatures with orders at most $M$, $k$ the least index with $c_k(\sigma)\ne c_k(\sigma')$, and $d_k=c_k(\sigma)-c_k(\sigma')$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If the areas differ, $k=1$ and $|d_1|\ge1/(2L_{\sigma\sigma'})$, where $L_{\sigma\sigma'}$ is the least common multiple of the orders of $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$.
\item If the areas are equal, to $A$ say, then $2\le k\le\min(\lfloor A/\pi\rfloor+4,M)$ and $d_k=(-1)^ka_{k-2}(P_{2k-3}(U)-P_{2k-3}(V))$, with $U,V$ the paddings of Lemma~\ref{lem:sigdata} for $L=k-1$; the difference of power sums is a nonzero integer, so $|d_k|\ge a_{k-2}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) $c_1=\frac12(2g-2+n-R)$ and $R\in\frac1{L_{\sigma\sigma'}}\Z$. (ii) The bound on $k$ is Theorem~\ref{thm:sep}. Put $l=k-2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:sigdata} with $L=k-1$, $R(U)=R(V)$ and $\Psi_j(U)=\Psi_j(V)$ for $j\le l$, padding by $1$ changing no $C_l=\sum_ib_l(m_i)$ as $p_l(1)=0$. The terms $\alpha_{k-1}A/4\pi$ cancel, and with the triangular expansion of $p_l(x)/x$ in the $\psi_k$ (Section~\ref{sec:heat}), $d_k=(-1)^l\sum_{j\le l+1}a_{l,j}(\Psi_j(U)-\Psi_j(V))=(-1)^la_l(P_{2l+1}(U)-P_{2l+1}(V))$, using $R(U)=R(V)$; this is nonzero as $d_k\ne0$.
\end{proof}

For $k=2$ the bound $|d_k|\ge a_{k-2}$ is attained, for instance by $(0;4,4,4)$ and $(0;3,4,6)$, of equal area, with $d_2=-\frac1{12}$.

\section{The diameter}\label{sec:geom}

\subsection{Three facts of plane hyperbolic geometry}

We use the disc model, with $\cosh d(z,w)=1+2|z-w|^2/((1-|z|^2)(1-|w|^2))$, the upper half-plane, with $\cosh d(z,w)=1+|z-w|^2/(2\operatorname{Im}z\operatorname{Im}w)$, and the hyperboloid $\{x\in\R^{1,2}:\langle x,x\rangle=-1,\ x_0>0\}$ with $\langle x,y\rangle=-x_0y_0+x_1y_1+x_2y_2$, in which lines are the intersections with planes $n^\perp$, $n$ a unit spacelike normal \cite{beardon1983}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:H}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(H\arabic*)]
\item A rotation by $\phi$ about $q$ moves a point at distance $r$ from $q$ by $d$ with $\sinh\frac d2=\sinh r\,|\sin\frac\phi2|$. A hyperbolic $\gamma$ moves a point at distance $r$ from its axis by $d$ with $\sinh\frac d2=\sinh\frac{\ell(\gamma)}2\cosh r$; in particular by at least $\ell(\gamma)$.
\item The product of the reflections in two distinct lines is a rotation by twice their angle about their intersection if they meet, a translation by twice their distance along their common perpendicular if they are ultraparallel, and is parabolic, fixing their common ideal point, if they are asymptotic (in the upper half-plane with that point at $\infty$ the two reflections are $x\mapsto2c_i-\bar x$, and their product is a translation).
\item Let $\lambda$ be the line through $p\ne q$, $d=d(p,q)$, $0<\alpha,\beta\le\frac\pi2$, and $L_1\ni p$, $L_3\ni q$ the lines making the angles $\alpha$, $\beta$ with the segment $[p,q]$, on the same side of $\lambda$. Put $X=\sin\alpha\sin\beta\cosh d-\cos\alpha\cos\beta$. If $X<1$, $L_1$ and $L_3$ meet, on that side, in a point $w$, and the triangle $pqw$ has angles $\alpha,\beta,\theta$ with $\cos\theta=X$; if $X>1$ they are ultraparallel at distance $h$ with $\cosh h=X$; if $X=1$ they are asymptotic.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(H1) In the disc, rotate about $0$ a point $z$ with $|z|=\tanh\frac r2$: $|z-e^{i\phi}z|=2\tanh\frac r2|\sin\frac\phi2|$ and $1-|z|^2=\cosh^{-2}\frac r2$, so $\cosh d-1=8\sinh^2\frac r2\cosh^2\frac r2\sin^2\frac\phi2=2\sinh^2r\sin^2\frac\phi2$, and $\cosh d-1=2\sinh^2\frac d2$. In the half-plane let $\gamma(z)=e^Lz$, with axis $i\R_{>0}$; a point $z$ at distance $r$ from the axis has $|z|/\operatorname{Im}z=\cosh r$ (the distance to the axis is attained along the circle $|w|=|z|$), and $\cosh d(z,e^Lz)-1=(e^L-1)^2|z|^2/(2e^L(\operatorname{Im}z)^2)=2\sinh^2\frac L2\cosh^2r$.
(H2) Let $s_1,s_2$ be the reflections. If the lines meet at $p$ at the angle $\theta$, $s_2s_1$ fixes $p$, preserves orientation, and its differential at $p$ is the product of the linear reflections in two lines at the angle $\theta$, a rotation by $2\theta$; an isometry is determined by its value and differential at one point. If they are ultraparallel with common perpendicular $\mu$, meeting them at $q_1,q_2$, both reflections preserve $\mu$ and each side of it, and act on $\mu$ as the reflections in $q_1,q_2$; so $s_2s_1$ preserves the sides of $\mu$ and translates $\mu$ by $2d(q_1,q_2)$, which determines it as that translation. If they are asymptotic, $s_2s_1$ fixes their common ideal point. If $s_2s_1z=z$ for some $z\in\Hb$, then $s_1z=s_2z$; were this point different from $z$, both lines would be the perpendicular bisector of the segment from $z$ to it, so $z$ lies on both lines, which do not meet. So $s_2s_1$ has no fixed point in $\Hb$.
(H3) For two lines with unit normals $n,n'$: if they meet at $w$, then $n,n'\in T_w=w^\perp$, a Euclidean plane, are normal to the lines there, so $|\langle n,n'\rangle|=\cos\theta'<1$ with $\theta'$ the acute angle between them, and if they are oriented outwards from a convex region with a vertex at $w$ of interior angle $\theta$, $\langle n,n'\rangle=\cos(\pi-\theta)=-\cos\theta$. If they are ultraparallel with common perpendicular $\mu(s)=\cosh s\,x+\sinh s\,v$ meeting them at $s=0,h$, then $n,n'$ are $\pm\mu'(0)$, $\pm\mu'(h)$, and $|\langle\mu'(0),\mu'(h)\rangle|=\cosh h>1$. So the asymptotic case is $|\langle n,n'\rangle|=1$. Now take $p=(1,0,0)$, $\lambda=\{x_2=0\}$, $q=(\cosh d,\sinh d,0)$, the triangle on the side $x_2>0$, and $u_q=(\sinh d,\cosh d,0)$ the unit tangent of $\lambda$ at $q$. Outward unit normals (away from the side $x_2>0$ and from $q$, respectively $p$) of $\lambda$, $L_1$, $L_3$ are $-e_2$, $n_1=-\sin\alpha\,e_1+\cos\alpha\,e_2$, $n_3=\sin\beta\,u_q+\cos\beta\,e_2$; indeed $\langle-e_2,n_1\rangle=-\cos\alpha$ and $\langle-e_2,n_3\rangle=-\cos\beta$, as the rule above requires at $p$ and $q$. Then $\langle n_1,n_3\rangle=-\sin\alpha\sin\beta\cosh d+\cos\alpha\cos\beta=-X$, and $X\ge-\cos(\alpha+\beta)\ge-1$, with equality only if $\alpha=\beta=\frac\pi2$ and $d=0$. So if $X<1$ the lines meet; not on the side $x_2<0$, where the triangle would have angles $\pi-\alpha$ and $\pi-\beta$ summing to at least $\pi$; so on the side $x_2>0$, with $-\cos\theta=\langle n_1,n_3\rangle=-X$. The other two cases follow from $|\langle n_1,n_3\rangle|=|X|$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The diameter bound}

A point $\tilde x\in\Hb$ with nontrivial stabiliser $\Gamma_{\tilde x}$ is \emph{elliptic}; $\Gamma_{\tilde x}$ is cyclic of order $m$, generated by the rotation by $2\pi/m$ about $\tilde x$, and its image in $\Orb$ is a cone point of order $m$.

\begin{lemma}[Cone points are separated]\label{lem:sep}
For $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$, distinct elliptic points satisfy $d(\tilde p,\tilde q)\ge d_0(\varepsilon,M):=\min\{\frac\varepsilon2,\operatorname{arccosh}(1+\frac2{\pi^2M^2})\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $a,b\le M$ be the orders of $\Gamma_{\tilde p},\Gamma_{\tilde q}$, $\alpha=\pi/a$, $\beta=\pi/b$, $d=d(\tilde p,\tilde q)$, and $\lambda,L_1,L_3,X$ as in (H3), with reflections $s_\lambda,s_1,s_3$. By (H2) $s_1s_\lambda$ and $s_\lambda s_3$ are rotations about $\tilde p$, $\tilde q$ by $2\alpha$, $2\beta$, which lie in the cyclic stabilisers (both senses do), so $\gamma=s_1s_3=(s_1s_\lambda)(s_\lambda s_3)\in\Gamma$. As $\Gamma$ has no parabolic element and $\gamma\ne1$, $X\ne1$ by (H2). If $X>1$, $\gamma$ is hyperbolic with $\ell(\gamma)=2h$ and $\cosh h=X\le\cosh d$, so $\varepsilon\le2d$. If $X<1$, $\gamma$ is a rotation by $2\theta$ about $w$, so $w$ is elliptic of some order $c\le M$ and $\theta=\pi k/c$, $1\le k<c$. By (H3)
\[
  \cosh d-1=\frac{\cos\theta+\cos(\alpha+\beta)}{\sin\alpha\sin\beta}=\frac{2\cos\frac{\theta+\alpha+\beta}2\cos\frac{\theta-\alpha-\beta}2}{\sin\alpha\sin\beta}.
\]
The defect $\pi-\alpha-\beta-\theta=\pi(abc-bc-ac-kab)/(abc)$ is positive with integer numerator, so at least $\pi/(abc)$, and $\cos\frac{\theta+\alpha+\beta}2\ge\frac1{abc}$ by $\sin x\ge\frac{2x}\pi$. As $\pi/M\le\theta$ and $\pi/M\le\alpha,\beta\le\frac\pi2$, $|\theta-\alpha-\beta|\le\pi-\frac\pi M$, so $\cos\frac{\theta-\alpha-\beta}2\ge\sin\frac\pi{2M}\ge\frac1M$; with $\sin\alpha\sin\beta\le\pi^2/(ab)$, $\cosh d-1\ge\frac2{\pi^2cM}\ge\frac2{\pi^2M^2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Balls have area bounded below]\label{lem:balls}
For $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$, $r_0=\frac12d_0(\varepsilon,M)$ and $\rho_1=\operatorname{arsinh}(\sinh r_0\sin\frac\pi M)$, every ball of radius $2r_0$ in $\Orb$ has area at least $v_0=2\pi\min\{(\cosh r_0-1)/M,\ \cosh\rho_1-1\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $d(\tilde y,\gamma\tilde y)\ge2r$ for every $\gamma\notin\Gamma_{\tilde y}$, the projection maps $\Gamma_{\tilde y}\backslash B(\tilde y,r)$ injectively onto the ball of radius $r$ about the image of $\tilde y$, of area $2\pi(\cosh r-1)/|\Gamma_{\tilde y}|$. Let $x\in\Orb$. If some cone point $p$ has $d(x,p)<r_0$, the ball of radius $2r_0$ about $x$ contains the ball of radius $r_0$ about $p$, and for $\gamma\notin\Gamma_{\tilde p}$, $\gamma\tilde p\ne\tilde p$ is elliptic, so $d(\tilde p,\gamma\tilde p)\ge2r_0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sep}). Otherwise $\Gamma_{\tilde x}=1$; a hyperbolic $\gamma$ moves $\tilde x$ by at least $\varepsilon\ge2\rho_1$ (H1), and an elliptic one is a rotation by $2\pi j/k$, $k\le M$, about a point at distance at least $r_0$, so moves $\tilde x$ by at least $2\operatorname{arsinh}(\sinh r_0\sin\frac\pi M)=2\rho_1$ (H1); and $\rho_1\le r_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Diameter bound]\label{thm:diam}
Every $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ has $\diam\Orb<D(A,\varepsilon,M):=4r_0A/v_0\le\frac A\pi\max(4M,M^2)\max(\frac4\varepsilon,\frac{10M}3)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\diam\Orb=d(x,y)=\Delta$ and lift to $\tilde x,\tilde y$ with $d(\tilde x,\tilde y)=\Delta$. The image $c$ of the segment, by arclength, satisfies $d(c(s),c(s'))=|s-s'|$: otherwise $d(x,y)<\Delta$. The points $c(4r_0i)$, $0\le i\le\lfloor\Delta/4r_0\rfloor$, are $4r_0$ apart, so the balls of radius $2r_0$ about them are disjoint, and Lemma~\ref{lem:balls} gives $(\lfloor\Delta/4r_0\rfloor+1)v_0\le A$. For the closed form, $\cosh r-1\ge r^2/2$, $\cosh\rho_1-1\ge(\cosh r_0-1)\sin^2\frac\pi M$ and $\sin\frac\pi M\ge\frac2M$ give $v_0\ge\pi r_0^2/\max(M,M^2/4)$, and $\operatorname{arccosh}(1+x)\ge\sqrt{2x/(1+x)}$ gives $d_0\ge\min(\frac\varepsilon2,\frac{0.6}M)$.
\end{proof}

Table~\ref{tab:D} compares $D$ with computed diameters. The bound grows like $AM^3$, and like $A/\varepsilon$ when $\varepsilon/2<\operatorname{arccosh}(1+2/(\pi^2M^2))$, that is, when the systole term of $d_0$ binds. By Proposition~\ref{prop:233} below some growth in $M$ is necessary; the factors $1/M$ in $d_0$ and $v_0$ are not intrinsic, and we do not optimise them. Theorem~\ref{thm:diam} gives a count of closed geodesics through Lemma~\ref{lem:counting}(i), with the factor $e^{3D}$. The same lemmas give a count without the diameter.

\begin{lemma}[Counting closed geodesics without the diameter]\label{lem:countK}
Every $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ has $n_\Orb(x)\le C_*\,e^x$ for all $x>0$, with $r_0,\rho_1,v_0$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:balls} and
\[
  C_*=C_*(A,\varepsilon,M)=\frac{A\,M\,e^{10r_0+\rho_1}}{2\,v_0\,(\cosh\rho_1-1)} .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $Y\subset\Orb$ be a maximal set of points at pairwise distance at least $4r_0$. The balls of radius $2r_0$ about the points of $Y$ are disjoint, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:balls} each has area at least $v_0$, so $|Y|\le A/v_0$; by maximality every point of $\Orb$ is within $4r_0$ of $Y$. For $y\in Y$ let $b(y)$ be a cone point at distance less than $r_0$ from $y$ if there is one, and $y$ otherwise; every point of $\Orb$ is within $5r_0$ of some $b(y)$. Fix lifts $\tilde b$. For $\gamma\notin\Gamma_{\tilde b}$, $d(\tilde b,\gamma\tilde b)\ge2\rho_1$: if $b$ is a cone point, $\gamma\tilde b$ is another elliptic point, at distance at least $d_0=2r_0\ge2\rho_1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sep}); otherwise $\Gamma_{\tilde b}=1$, a hyperbolic $\gamma$ moves $\tilde b$ by at least $\varepsilon\ge2d_0\ge2\rho_1$ and an elliptic one by at least $2\rho_1$, as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:balls}. Hence the orbit points $\gamma\tilde b$ of distinct cosets of $\Gamma_{\tilde b}$ are at least $2\rho_1$ apart, the balls of radius $\rho_1$ about them are disjoint, and as $|\Gamma_{\tilde b}|\le M$,
\[
  \#\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\ d(\tilde b,\gamma\tilde b)\le R\}\le M\,\frac{\cosh(R+\rho_1)-1}{\cosh\rho_1-1}.
\]
A closed geodesic of length at most $x$ passes through a point $z$ within $5r_0$ of some $b=b(y)$; a lift $\tilde z$ with $d(\tilde z,\tilde b)\le5r_0$ lies on the axis of some $\gamma$ in its class, and $d(\tilde b,\gamma\tilde b)\le2d(\tilde b,\tilde z)+\ell(\gamma)\le x+10r_0$. Counting such $\gamma$ at every base point, $n_\Orb(x)\le\frac A{v_0}\,M\,\frac{\cosh(x+10r_0+\rho_1)-1}{\cosh\rho_1-1}\le Ke^x$.
\end{proof}

\section{Counting eigenvalues}\label{sec:count}

\begin{lemma}[Elementary bounds]\label{lem:elem}
(i) Every $\Orb\in\Sig$ has $\Area(\Orb)\ge\pi/21$ and $n+4g\le\Area(\Orb)/\pi+4$. (ii) For $t>0$ and $m\ge2$, $0<\mathrm E_m(t)\le b_0(m)=\frac{m^2-1}{12m}$ and $0<\mathrm I(t)\le e^{-t/4}\Area/4\pi t$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) With $s=\Area/2\pi$: $g\ge2$ gives $s\ge2$; $g=1$ needs $n\ge1$, $s\ge\frac12$; $g=0$, $n\ge5$ gives $s\ge\frac12$; $g=0$, $n=4$ has an order at least $3$, $s\ge\frac16$; and for $g=0$, $n=3$, $p\le q\le r$: $p\ge3$ forces $r\ge4$, $s\ge\frac1{12}$; $p=2$, $q=3$ forces $r\ge7$, $s\ge\frac1{42}$; $p=2$, $q=4$ forces $r\ge5$, $s\ge\frac1{20}$; $p=2$, $q\ge5$ gives $s\ge\frac1{10}$. The second statement holds as each cone point adds at least $\frac12$ to $s$. (ii) Proposition~\ref{prop:remcone}(a) with $K=0$ gives $0<\mathrm E_m(t)\le b_0(m)$, and $0<\hat g_t\le e^{-t/4}$ and $0\le r\tanh\pi r\le|r|$ give the bound on $\mathrm I$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Counting and tails]\label{prop:counttail}
For every $\Orb$, $x>0$, $\Lambda>0$ and $0<s<t$: $\#\{j:\lambda_j\le x\}\le e\,\cZ_\Orb(1/x)$ and $\sum_{\lambda_j>\Lambda}e^{-\lambda_jt}\le e^{-\Lambda(t-s)}\cZ_\Orb(s)$. If $N\ge e\,\cZ_\Orb(1/\Lambda)$ is an integer, then $\lambda_j>\Lambda$ for $j\ge N$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\cZ(1/x)\ge\sum_{\lambda_j\le x}e^{-\lambda_j/x}\ge e^{-1}\#\{\lambda_j\le x\}$, and $e^{-\lambda t}\le e^{-\Lambda(t-s)}e^{-\lambda s}$ for $\lambda>\Lambda$.
\end{proof}

So, for $\Orb$ of area $A$, systole $\ge\ell$ and $n$ cone points of orders $m_i$, with $n_\Orb(x)\le Ce^x$, $\cZ_\Orb(s)\le\frac A{4\pi s}+\sum_ib_0(m_i)+\Hyp(s)$ for all $s$, with $\Hyp(s)$ bounded by Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp}(ii).

\section{Finitely many eigenvalues determine the signature}\label{sec:E}

Fix $A>0$, $\varepsilon>0$ and $M\ge2$. Put $n_*=\lfloor A/\pi\rfloor+4$, $k_*=\min(n_*,M)$, $L_M=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,M)$, $\delta_1=1/(2L_M)$, $\delta_k=a_{k-2}$ for $2\le k\le k_*$, $\gamma_*=\min_{k\le k_*}\delta_k$,
\[
  Q(K)=\frac A{4\pi}|\alpha_{K+1}|+n_*\,|b_K(M)|,\qquad t_1=\min\Big\{1,\ \min_{1\le k\le k_*}\frac{\delta_k}{4\,Q(k-1)}\Big\},
\]
and $\Theta(t)=\frac12\gamma_*t^{k_*-2}$.

\begin{lemma}[The gap]\label{lem:gap}
For $\sigma\ne\sigma'$ in $\Sig(A,M)$ and $0<t\le t_1$, $|G_\sigma(t)-G_{\sigma'}(t)|\ge\Theta(t)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $k$ be the first index of difference, so $k\le k_*$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:int}). By \eqref{eq:Grem} with $K=k-1$, $n\le n_*$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:elem}) and $|b_K|$ increasing in $m$, $|G_\sigma(t)-\sum_{j\le k}c_j(\sigma)t^{j-2}|\le t^{k-1}Q(k-1)$, likewise for $\sigma'$, and so
\[
  |G_\sigma(t)-G_{\sigma'}(t)|\ge t^{k-2}\big(|d_k|-2tQ(k-1)\big)\ge\tfrac12t^{k-2}|d_k|\ge\tfrac12\delta_kt^{k-2}\ge\Theta(t),
\]
since $|d_k|\ge\delta_k$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:int}, the lcm dividing $L_M$), $t\le\delta_k/(4Q(k-1))$, $t\le1$ and $k\le k_*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finitely many eigenvalues determine the signature]\label{thm:E}
With $C_*=C_*(A,\varepsilon,M)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}, put
\begin{gather*}
  t_2=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2(1+\varepsilon)},\qquad p=k_*-\tfrac32,\qquad\varpi=\frac{3C_*\,\varepsilon e^{\varepsilon/2}}{(1-e^{-\varepsilon})\sqrt{4\pi}},\\
  y_0=2\Big(\log\frac{16\varpi}{\gamma_*}+p\log\frac4{\varepsilon^2}+p\log\frac{2p}e\Big),\qquad t_3=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4\max(y_0,1)},\qquad t_*=\min(t_1,t_2,t_3),\\
  \Theta_*=\Theta(t_*),\qquad\cZ^\sharp(s)=\frac A{4\pi s}+n_*\frac{M^2-1}{12M}+1,\qquad\Lambda=\frac2{t_*}\log\frac{8\cZ^\sharp(t_*/2)}{\Theta_*},\\
  N=\big\lfloor e\,\cZ^\sharp(1/\Lambda)\big\rfloor+1,\qquad\delta=\frac{\Theta_*}{8eNt_*}.
\end{gather*}
Let $\Orb\in\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ have eigenvalues $0=\lambda_0<\lambda_1\le\cdots$, counted with multiplicity and in increasing order, and let $\tilde\lambda_0,\dots,\tilde\lambda_{N-1}\ge0$ satisfy $|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j|\le\delta$. Then $\sigma(\Orb)$ is the unique $\sigma\in\Sig(A,M)$ minimising $|\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt_*}-G_\sigma(t_*)|$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\tilde\cZ=\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt_*}$ and $\sigma_0=\sigma(\Orb)$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:trace},
$\tilde\cZ-G_{\sigma_0}(t_*)=\Hyp(t_*)-\sum_{j\ge N}e^{-\lambda_jt_*}+\sum_{j<N}(e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt_*}-e^{-\lambda_jt_*})$.
\emph{Hyperbolic term.} For $0<t\le t_2$, Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp}(ii) with $\ell=\varepsilon$ and the count of Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}, and $1+\frac{2t}{\varepsilon-t}\le3$, give $\Hyp(t)\le B_*(t):=\varpi\,t^{-1/2}e^{-\varepsilon^2/4t}$, which increases on $(0,t_2]$. With $y=\varepsilon^2/4t$, $B_*(t)\le\Theta(t)/8$ reads $e^{-y}y^p\le\frac{\gamma_*}{16\varpi}(\varepsilon^2/4)^p$, and as $e^{-y/2}y^p\le(2p/e)^p$ it holds for $y\ge y_0$, so at $t=t_*$. Hence $\Hyp(s)\le\Theta_*/8\le1$ for $s\le t_*$.
\emph{Tail.} For $s\le t_*$, $\cZ_\Orb(s)\le\cZ^\sharp(s)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:elem} and the line above. As $\Lambda t_*\ge1$, $t_*/2$ and $1/\Lambda$ are at most $t_*$, so $N>e\,\cZ_\Orb(1/\Lambda)$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:counttail} with $s=t_*/2$ gives $\sum_{j\ge N}e^{-\lambda_jt_*}\le e^{-\Lambda t_*/2}\cZ^\sharp(t_*/2)=\Theta_*/8$.
\emph{Perturbation.} $|e^{-at}-e^{-bt}|\le t|a-b|e^{-t\min(a,b)}\le t|a-b|$ for $a,b\ge0$ gives at most $Nt_*\delta\le\Theta_*/(8e)$. (Keeping the factor $e^{-t\min(a,b)}$, the hypothesis $|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j|\le\delta$ can be replaced by $\sum_{j<N}|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j|e^{-t_*\min(\tilde\lambda_j,\lambda_j)}\le N\delta$, which allows, for instance, an over-estimate of $\lambda_j$ by up to $\delta e^{\lambda_jt_*}$.)
So $|\tilde\cZ-G_{\sigma_0}(t_*)|<\frac38\Theta_*$, while for $\sigma\ne\sigma_0$ Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} gives $|\tilde\cZ-G_\sigma(t_*)|>\Theta_*-\frac38\Theta_*$.
\end{proof}

The decision rule needs $G_\sigma(t_*)$ only to within a fraction of $\Theta_*$, say $\Theta_*/16$, which leaves the margin of the proof intact; by \eqref{eq:Grem} this is achieved by the truncated expansion $\sum_{j\le K+1}c_j(\sigma)t_*^{j-2}$ for any $K$ with $t_*^K\,Q(K)\le\Theta_*/16$. We do not prove that such a $K$ exists for every class: $t_*^KQ(K)$ decreases only while $K$ is below about $(\pi/M)^2/t_*$, and its minimum is not compared with $\Theta_*/16$ here. The rule does not depend on it, since $G_\sigma(t_*)$ is a finite sum of explicit integrals (Theorem~\ref{thm:trace}) that can be evaluated to within $\Theta_*/16$ by quadrature with a rigorous error bound. A rational $t\le t_*$ may replace $t_*$ if $\Theta_*$, $\Lambda$, $N$ and $\delta$ are recomputed at $t$, and the rule is then computable to any prescribed accuracy, at a cost that grows with $\log(1/\Theta_*)$; $e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}$ is not rational, so it is not a computation in exact rational arithmetic.

Taking $\tilde\lambda_j=\lambda_j(\Orb')$ gives the last statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:intro}: orbifolds of $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ whose first $N$ eigenvalues agree to within $\delta$ have the same signature. The data must be the first $N$ eigenvalues, in order and with multiplicity; an approximation that misses one eigenvalue shifts every later index, and the theorem says nothing about it.

\begin{remark}[Sharper constants]\label{rem:sharper}
The constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} are uniform over the class and are not optimised. (a) The separations $\delta_k$ are worst cases over $\Sig(A,M)$; $\delta_1=1/(2L_M)$ in particular is far below the least nonzero first difference in a given finite $\Sig(A,M)$, which can be computed exactly, and $\Theta(t)$ may keep the individual terms $\delta_kt^{k-2}$. (b) In the proof, the hyperbolic term is nonnegative and the spectral tail enters with the opposite sign, so their sum may be replaced by their maximum, which gains at most a factor $2$ in the margin. (c) In Theorem~\ref{thm:post}, closed geodesics of length at most $L$ that have been enumerated may be summed exactly, and the count of Lemma~\ref{lem:counting} used only for lengths above $L$; for the examples of Section~\ref{sec:practice}, where all closed geodesics of length at most $4$ are enumerated, this replaces $e^{-\ell^2/4t}$ by $e^{-L^2/4t}$ for the remaining classes. We have not used these improvements.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Other test functions]\label{rem:testfn}
The heat function is not the only possible test function. If the geodesic-side function $g$ is supported in $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$, that is, if the spectral function $h=\hat g$ has Fourier transform supported below the systole, the trace formula for a member of $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ has no geodesic term, since every closed geodesic has length at least $\varepsilon$, and the constraint $t_3$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} disappears. Two inputs remain. The identity and cone terms of the signatures in $\Sig(A,M)$ must be separated quantitatively, which for the heat function is Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}, through the heat invariants. And the spectral side $\sum_jh(r_j)$ must be truncated at the $N$th eigenvalue with a bound for the rest; such a bound needs a count of eigenvalues, which we take from the heat trace at an earlier time (Proposition~\ref{prop:counttail}), and that bound again involves the closed geodesics (Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp}). We keep the heat function because it gives both inputs explicitly; we have not worked out the constants for a compactly supported $g$.
\end{remark}

\section{An a-posteriori test}\label{sec:practice}

The constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} are worst cases over a class. Run on given data at one moderate time, the same mechanism gives a test.

\begin{theorem}[A-posteriori test]\label{thm:post}
Let $\Orb\in\Sig$ have area $A$, systole at least $\ell$, $n_\Orb(x)\le Ce^x$ for $x>0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:counting}(i) with a diameter bound $\Delta$, $C=\pi e^{3\Delta}/A$, or Lemma~\ref{lem:countK}, $C=C_*(A,\ell,M)$), and signature in a finite set $\mathcal S$ of hyperbolic signatures of area $A$; put $\beta=\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal S}\sum_ib_0(m_i)$ and let $\mathcal H(s)$ be the bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp}(ii) for $\Hyp(s)$ with $\ell$ and $C$: the first bound for $s\le\ell^2/(2(1+\ell))$ and the bound for every $t>0$ otherwise. Let $\lambda_0\le\lambda_1\le\dots$ be the eigenvalues of $\Orb$, counted with multiplicity, and let $\tilde\lambda_j\ge0$ and $\epsilon_j\ge0$ satisfy $|\tilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j|\le\epsilon_j$ for $j\le N$; in particular none of $\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_N$ is omitted. For $t>0$ put
\[
  E_N(t)=\mathcal H(t)+\inf_{0<s<t}e^{-(\tilde\lambda_N-\epsilon_N)(t-s)}\Big(\frac A{4\pi s}+\beta+\mathcal H(s)\Big)+t\sum_{j<N}\epsilon_j .
\]
Then $|\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}-G_{\sigma(\Orb)}(t)|\le E_N(t)$ for every $t>0$. Hence $\sigma(\Orb)=\sigma$ if, at some $t$, either
\begin{enumerate}[label=(C\arabic*)]
\item $\sigma$ is the only element of $\mathcal S$ with $|\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}-G_\sigma(t)|\le E_N(t)$, or
\item $\sigma$ satisfies this inequality and $E_N(t)$ is less than half the distance from $G_\sigma(t)$ to the nearest $G_{\sigma'}(t)$, $\sigma'\in\mathcal S\setminus\{\sigma\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}: the hyperbolic term is at most $\mathcal H(t)$; for $0<s<t$ the tail $\sum_{j\ge N}e^{-\lambda_jt}$ is at most $e^{-\lambda_N(t-s)}\cZ_\Orb(s)$, with $\lambda_N\ge\tilde\lambda_N-\epsilon_N$ and $\cZ_\Orb(s)\le\frac A{4\pi s}+\beta+\mathcal H(s)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:elem}); and the perturbation is at most $t\sum\epsilon_j$, by $|e^{-at}-e^{-bt}|\le t|a-b|$; the sharper factor $e^{-t\min(a,b)}$ may be kept, which the completeness budget of Section~\ref{sec:numerical} does. Under (C1) the true signature passes, so it is $\sigma$; (C2) implies (C1).
\end{proof}

Criterion (C2) is the margin used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}; (C1) is the weaker criterion; it uses only the inequality $|\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}-G_{\sigma(\Orb)}(t)|\le E_N(t)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:post}. Since $\Hyp$ and the tail are nonnegative, the acceptance region could be taken one-sided, $-(\text{tail}+\text{perturbation})\le\sum_{j<N}e^{-\tilde\lambda_jt}-G_\sigma(t)\le\mathcal H(t)+\text{perturbation}$, which would lower the counts; we keep the symmetric form. The test has five inputs, and its conclusion is only as good as they are.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The exact area $A$. The test as stated decides among signatures of one known area. Within $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ enough eigenvalues determine the signature and hence the area (Theorem~\ref{thm:intro}), but only with the a-priori $N$; an upper bound for the area would suffice in $E_N$, and running the test over all of $\Sig(A,M)$ we have not done. In our examples the signature is known by construction, so they are demonstrations of the test, not determinations of unknown orbifolds.
\item A lower bound $\ell$ for the systole. Ours come from a complete enumeration of the short closed geodesics in high-precision floating point, rounded down (Section~\ref{sec:numerical}).
\item A count of the closed geodesics. The class-level count $C_*(A,\ell,M)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:countK} needs nothing beyond the area, the systole bound and the order bound; an instance-level count comes from a diameter bound, and ours, $2\diam P$ for the double of a convex polygon $P$, are proved bounds.
\item Completeness: the computed list contains every eigenvalue up to $\tilde\lambda_N$, with multiplicity. The test presupposes a complete list. Deleting or duplicating one eigenvalue shifts every later index and can make the test accept a wrong signature: for $\Orb(2,8,8)$ and $\Orb(3,3,12)$, deleting or duplicating one of $\tilde\lambda_1,\tilde\lambda_2,\tilde\lambda_3,\tilde\lambda_5,\tilde\lambda_8,\tilde\lambda_{12}$ makes (C1), at the first $N$ at which it singles out a signature, conclude a wrong one in $19$ of these $24$ cases, mostly at a smaller $N$ than the true count, so a user who raises $N$ until (C1) holds would stop at the wrong answer. Our completeness evidence (Section~\ref{sec:numerical}) is not a proof.
\item The errors $\epsilon_j$. Ours are a-posteriori estimates, the difference between two discretisations, and not enclosures. The counts are insensitive to them: replacing $\epsilon_j$ by $\max(\epsilon_j,r\tilde\lambda_j)$ leaves the counts $18$ and $25$ of the triangle orbifolds unchanged for $r\le10^{-4}$, while for $r=10^{-3}$ the criterion fails in the complete range.
\end{enumerate}
So what the computations below establish is that the criterion of Theorem~\ref{thm:post} holds with the estimated errors, for lists assumed complete. A rigorous decision would need guaranteed upper and lower eigenvalue bounds, a proof of completeness independent of the signature, a validated evaluation of $G_\sigma(t)$, a certified lower bound for the systole, and outward rounding in $E_N$. For a conforming Galerkin discretisation of the exact domain, the inertia of the factorisation $K-sM$ at each window boundary counts the discrete eigenvalues below $s$ exactly (Sylvester's law of inertia), and index-wise lower bounds, for instance of Crouzeix--Raviart type \cite{liuoishi2013,carstensengedicke2014}, together with the conforming upper bounds would enclose each $\lambda_j$ by its index; completeness would then follow from the enclosures. We have not done this.

\subsection{Numerical methods}\label{sec:numerical}

\emph{Examples.} $\Orb(2,8,8)$ and $\Orb(3,3,12)$ are doubles of hyperbolic triangles; the eight orbifolds $\Orb_\vartheta$ of signature $(0;3,3,3,3)$, $\vartheta=0,0.4,\dots,2.8$, are doubles of geodesic quadrilaterals $P$ with all angles $\frac\pi3$ and two mirror symmetries, with $a,b$ the distances from the centre to two adjacent sides, $\sinh a\sinh b=\frac12$ and $\vartheta=\log(\sinh a/\sinh b)$.

\emph{Discretisation and solver.} The spectrum of the double of $P$ is the union of the Neumann and Dirichlet spectra of $P$ (the reflection exchanging the copies splits $H^1$ into even and odd parts); for $\Orb_\vartheta$ the two mirror symmetries split each further into four sectors. Each problem $-\Delta_Eu=\lambda wu$, $w=4/(1-|z|^2)^2$, is solved in the Poincar\'e disc with $H^1$-conforming finite elements of order $p$ on meshes of uniform hyperbolic size $h$, the geodesic sides represented as rational quadratic splines and the elements curved to order $p$, so that the discrete domain is a polynomial approximation of degree $p$ of the true one (its area agrees to $2\times10^{-14}$), with NGSolve \cite{ngsolve,schoberl1997}; the mass quadrature is raised by $2p$. No grading is used: at a corner of angle $\pi/k$ the eigenfunctions extend by reflection to smooth functions, so there is no corner singularity (at the right-angled corners where a symmetry line of $\Orb_\vartheta$ meets a side with the other boundary condition the singular exponents are odd integers, so there is none there either), and the observed convergence is $h^{13}$ to $h^{19}$ at $p=10$ and geometric in $p$. Eigenvalues are computed by shift-invert Lanczos iteration (ARPACK \cite{arpack1998}, run to machine precision) in overlapping spectral windows of $200$, one sparse factorisation per window. For $\Orb_\vartheta$ every eigenvalue is covered by two windows and clusters are resolved at relative $10^{-9}$. The triangle spectra used here were computed with single-window slicing, each eigenvalue taken from one window, which can miss an eigenvalue at a window boundary; we recomputed all four problems ($\Orb(2,8,8)$ and $\Orb(3,3,12)$, Neumann and Dirichlet) with the two-window solver at the same production level, every eigenvalue below $\lambda\approx2.0\times10^4$ covered by two windows and no window miss found, and the two computations agree: the same number of eigenvalues below a common cut in each problem, every value within the error estimate $\epsilon_j$ below, and the largest relative difference $1.4\times10^{-14}$. The test of Theorem~\ref{thm:post} run on the recomputed lists gives the same counts as in Table~\ref{tab:practice}.

\emph{Error estimates.} The production level is $(h,p)=(0.05,10)$, and $\epsilon_j=\max(|\lambda_j(0.05,10)-\lambda_j(0.07,12)|,\ 10^{-14}\max(\lambda_j,1))$, the difference between two independent discretisations ($\epsilon_0=0$); in the complete ranges below it is at most $7.4\times10^{-8}$ relative. These are estimates, not bounds. Conforming elements would bound the eigenvalues from above in exact arithmetic with exact integration on the exact domain; here the domain is approximated, the weight $w$ is integrated by quadrature and everything is in floating point, and none of these effects is controlled, so no $\tilde\lambda_j$ is a proved upper bound, and no lower bounds are computed. For the lowest eigenvalues the $\epsilon_j$ are about ten to twenty times the floor $10^{-14}\lambda_j$, where the difference reflects the solver and rounding rather than the discretisation; the convergence in $h$ and $p$ is tabulated in the supplement of \cite{companionA}. With the second estimate $|\lambda_j(0.05,10)-\lambda_j(0.07,10)|$, up to $1.2\times10^{-8}$ relative for the first $500$ (larger than $\epsilon_j$ except for some of the lowest eigenvalues), the counts of Table~\ref{tab:practice} for the triangle orbifolds are unchanged. Table~\ref{tab:spectra} lists $\tilde\lambda_j$ and $\epsilon_j$ for $j\le40$ for the two triangle orbifolds.

\emph{Completeness.} For the triangle orbifolds about $2850$ eigenvalues were computed (Neumann and Dirichlet together, up to $\lambda\approx2.2\times10^4$); the computed heat trace agrees with $\mathrm I+\mathrm E$ for the true signature to $4.7\times10^{-13}$ at $t=0.0015$, within an error budget of $1.5\times10^{-11}$ formed by $t\sum_j\epsilon_je^{-(\tilde\lambda_j-\epsilon_j)t}$ and a Weyl-law estimate of the tail beyond the computed range; that tail term is an estimate, not a bound, and floating-point summation is not included. Against every other signature of area $\frac\pi2$ with orders at most $12$ the computed trace differs by at least $2.96\times10^{-3}$, about $2\times10^8$ budgets, so the comparison singles out the true signature. One missing or one spurious eigenvalue $\lambda<1.66\times10^4$ would change the trace by more than the budget, but a missing eigenvalue together with a spurious one nearby would not be seen. Completeness below $1.6\times10^4$ is therefore supported by this one comparison and by the agreement of the single- and two-window computations, not proved, and we use the about $2000$ eigenvalues below $1.6\times10^4$ (Table~\ref{tab:inputs}). For $\Orb_\vartheta$ about $5800$ eigenvalues were computed per member; we use those below $0.8$ times the least sector maximum, where the eigenvalue counts of the three most accurate of five discretisations, in every sector, agree exactly, $4357$ to $4451$ of them; the trace test, with the same limitations, at $t\ge0.0025$ with a budget of $2.2\times10^{-11}$, covers $\lambda<9.8\times10^3$, which contains every $\tilde\lambda_N$ that the test with the instance inputs uses, and above it the agreement of the counts is the only evidence.

\emph{Systoles and diameters.} Every closed geodesic of length at most $L$ has a representative $\gamma$ with $d(x_0,\gamma x_0)\le L+2r$, where $r=\max_{y\in P}d(x_0,y)$ (move a point of the axis into $P$ by the reflection group, in which $\Gamma$ is normal). Enumerating all tiles of the reflection tiling within distance $L+3r$ of $x_0$ by breadth-first search, with $L=4$, at $40$ and $50$ digits, gives the systole and the next length, computed in $40$- and $50$-digit floating-point arithmetic (agreeing to $10^{-30}$; not interval arithmetic); $\ell$ in Table~\ref{tab:inputs} is the systole rounded down at the sixth decimal, a margin of at least $5\times10^{-8}$. For the triangle orbifolds the computed systoles agree with the closed forms $2\operatorname{arccosh}(1+\cos\frac\pi4)$ and $2\operatorname{arccosh}(\frac12+\cos\frac\pi{12})$; for the family they equal $4b$ to $10^{-30}$, a numerical identification (Proposition~\ref{prop:N2} proves only that the systole is at most $4b$). A point of the double is within $\diam P$ of a vertex, which lies in both copies, so $\diam\Orb\le2\diam P$, and for a convex geodesic polygon $\diam P$ is the largest distance between two vertices, in closed form; the computed diameter (by a grid and local optimisation over pairs of points in the same and in opposite copies) equals $\diam P$ in all ten cases.

\emph{The test.} $G_\sigma(t)$ is evaluated by Gauss--Legendre quadrature of the integrals of Theorem~\ref{thm:trace} and checked against $30$-digit quadrature (worst difference $2.1\times10^{-15}$, relative for the identity term and absolute for the cone terms), and $\mathcal S$ is the set of all signatures of the same area with orders at most $M$. The time runs over $400$ log-spaced points in $[0.002,1]$ and $300$ in $[10^{-5},0.002)$, refined by $61$ points around each minimiser. The infimum over $s$ is taken on $240$ log-spaced values of $s/t$; any $s$ gives an upper bound for it, so the grid value already gives a valid $E_N$, and Brent's method, applied to the infimum, only confirms that the reported count does not change. Table~\ref{tab:practice} reports for each count the middle of the window of grid times at which it holds. The program also checks that, at every time of the grid and for every $N$ in the complete range, neither criterion singles out a wrong signature. Counts are values of $N$; the test with $N$ uses the $N+1$ eigenvalues $\tilde\lambda_0,\dots,\tilde\lambda_N$, while $N$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:E} is the number of eigenvalues used. In every case of Table~\ref{tab:practice} both criteria hold within the complete range.

\section{The hypotheses}\label{sec:hyp}

\subsection{The orders: high orders behave like cusps}

We use J{\o}rgensen's inequality \cite{jorgensen1976} (see \cite[Ch.~5]{beardon1983} for an exposition) in the case in which one element is hyperbolic and the group has no parabolic elements, and include a short proof of that case, by J{\o}rgensen's argument, for completeness.

\begin{lemma}[J{\o}rgensen's inequality, hyperbolic case]\label{lem:jorgensen}
Let $\Gamma\subset PSL(2,\R)$ be discrete with no parabolic elements, $A\in\Gamma$ hyperbolic and $B\in\Gamma$ an element that fixes neither fixed point of $A$ in $\partial\Hb$ and does not interchange them. Then, for any lifts to $SL(2,\R)$, $|\tr^2A-4|+|\tr[A,B]-2|\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conjugate so that $A=\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})$, $u>1$, with fixed points $0,\infty$, and let $B=\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{psmallmatrix}$. A direct computation gives $\tr[A,B]-2=-bc(u-u^{-1})^2$, so the claim is $\mu:=(u-u^{-1})^2(1+|bc|)\ge1$; suppose $\mu<1$. Let $B_0=B$, $B_{n+1}=B_nAB_n^{-1}=\begin{psmallmatrix}a_{n+1}&b_{n+1}\\c_{n+1}&d_{n+1}\end{psmallmatrix}$ and $x_n=b_nc_n$. Then
\[
  B_{n+1}=\begin{pmatrix}ua_nd_n-u^{-1}b_nc_n&-a_nb_n(u-u^{-1})\\c_nd_n(u-u^{-1})&-ub_nc_n+u^{-1}a_nd_n\end{pmatrix},\qquad x_{n+1}=-x_n(1+x_n)(u-u^{-1})^2 .
\]
So $|x_n|\le|x_0|$ implies $|x_{n+1}|\le\mu|x_n|$, and by induction $|x_n|\le\mu^n|x_0|\to0$. No $x_n$ vanishes: $x_0\ne0$ as $B$ fixes neither $0$ nor $\infty$; if $n\ge1$ is least with $x_n=0$, then $x_{n-1}=-1$, so $a_{n-1}d_{n-1}=0$; if both vanish, $B_{n-1}$ interchanges $0$ and $\infty$, which is excluded for $n-1=0$ and impossible for the hyperbolic $B_{n-1}$, $n-1\ge1$; if exactly one vanishes, $B_n$, a hyperbolic element with fixed points $B_{n-1}(0)$ and $B_{n-1}(\infty)$, shares exactly one fixed point with $A$, and then the commutator $[A,B_n]$ is a nontrivial parabolic element of $\Gamma$ (upper or lower unitriangular with a nonzero entry). Now $B_n$, $n\ge1$, is hyperbolic with repelling fixed point $p_n=B_{n-1}(0)=b_{n-1}/d_{n-1}$ and attracting fixed point $q_n=B_{n-1}(\infty)=a_{n-1}/c_{n-1}$, both finite and nonzero, with $p_n/q_n=x_{n-1}/(1+x_{n-1})\to0$, and with translation length that of $A$. Choose $k_n\in\Z$ with $u^{-2k_n}\in[\rho_n,u^2\rho_n)$, $\rho_n=|p_nq_n|^{-1/2}$, and put $C_n=A^{-k_n}B_nA^{k_n}\in\Gamma$; its fixed points $u^{-2k_n}p_n$ and $u^{-2k_n}q_n$ have moduli at most $u^2|p_n/q_n|^{1/2}\to0$ and at least $|q_n/p_n|^{1/2}\to\infty$. So $C_n\to A$ in $PSL(2,\R)$, while $C_n\ne A$ as $C_n$ does not fix $0$. This contradicts discreteness.
\end{proof}

For a rotation $\beta$ by $\phi$ about a point at distance $r$ from the axis of a hyperbolic $\gamma$ of translation length $L$, $\tr^2\gamma-4=4\sinh^2\frac L2$ and $\tr[\gamma,\beta]-2=4\sinh^2\frac L2\sin^2\frac\phi2\cosh^2r$ for any lifts: with $\gamma=\operatorname{diag}(e^{L/2},e^{-L/2})$ and $\beta=hkh^{-1}$, $k=\begin{psmallmatrix}\cos\frac\phi2&\sin\frac\phi2\\-\sin\frac\phi2&\cos\frac\phi2\end{psmallmatrix}$, $h=\begin{psmallmatrix}\cosh\frac r2&\sinh\frac r2\\\sinh\frac r2&\cosh\frac r2\end{psmallmatrix}$ (which moves $i$ along the unit circle, perpendicular to the axis, by $r$), the off-diagonal entries of $\beta$ are $\pm\sin\frac\phi2\cosh r$, so $bc=-\sin^2\frac\phi2\cosh^2r$, and $\tr[\gamma,\beta]-2=-bc(e^{L/2}-e^{-L/2})^2$.

\begin{proposition}[The family $\Orb(2,3,m)$]\label{prop:233}
For $m\ge7$ put $s_\infty=\operatorname{arccosh}\frac2{\sqrt3}$, $h_m=\operatorname{arccosh}\frac1{2\sin(\pi/m)}$ and $\ell_{2,3}=2\operatorname{arsinh}\big(2\sqrt{1+\frac34\cosh^2(2s_\infty)}\big)^{-1}=0.56206\ldots$. Then $\Area\Orb(2,3,m)=2\pi(\frac16-\frac1m)<\frac\pi3$, $\ell(\Orb(2,3,m))\ge\ell_{2,3}$, $\diam\Orb(2,3,m)\ge h_m\ge\log\frac m{2\pi}$, and the open ball of radius $h_m$ about the cone point of order $m$ is a hyperbolic cone of angle $2\pi/m$. So $\Orb(2,3,m)\in\Cl(\frac\pi3,\ell_{2,3},m)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Orb=\Orb(2,3,m)$ is the double of the triangle $T$ with vertices $v_2,v_3,v_m$ and angles $\frac\pi2,\frac\pi3,\frac\pi m$; its group $\Gamma_m$ is the orientation-preserving subgroup of the group $W$ generated by the reflections in the sides of $T$, of which $T$ is a fundamental domain \cite{beardon1983}, and the folding $f:\Hb\to T$, which sends each point to the unique point of $T$ in its $W$-orbit, is $1$-Lipschitz with $d(z,Wv)=d(f(z),v)$ for each vertex $v$, since $T$ meets each orbit once and each tile is isometric to $T$. By (H3), $\cosh d(v_2,v_3)=2\cos(\pi/m)/\sqrt3$, so $s_m=d(v_2,v_3)<s_\infty$, and $\cosh d(v_2,v_m)=1/(2\sin(\pi/m))$, so $d(v_2,v_m)=h_m$; the folding of $\Orb$ onto $T$ is $1$-Lipschitz and the identity on $T$, so the cone points of orders $2$ and $m$ are at distance $h_m$ in $\Orb$, and $h_m\ge\log\frac1{2\sin(\pi/m)}\ge\log\frac m{2\pi}$.
\emph{The cone ball.} Let $P$ be the union of the $2m$ tiles $gT$, $g\in W$, with vertex $v_m$, the translates under the stabiliser of $v_m$ in $\Gamma_m$ of the fundamental domain $T\cup s(T)$, $s$ the reflection in $[v_m,v_2]$. For $\gamma\in\Gamma_m$ outside that stabiliser, $\gamma P$ and $P$ have disjoint interiors. $P$ is star-shaped about $v_m$, and its boundary consists of images of $[v_2,v_3]$, at distance $h_m$ from $v_m$ because the angle of $T$ at $v_2$ is $\frac\pi2$. So $B(v_m,h_m)\subset\operatorname{int}P$, its translates are equal or disjoint, and its image is the cone of angle $2\pi/m$ and radius $h_m$.
\emph{The systole.} Let $\gamma\in\Gamma_m$ be hyperbolic with axis $\alpha$ and $L=\ell(\gamma)$. As $\alpha$ is connected and unbounded, it is not covered by the disjoint open balls $gB(v_m,h_m)$; take $\tilde x\in\alpha$ outside them and $x=f(\tilde x)$, so $d(x,v_m)\ge h_m$. Write $x\in[v_m,q]$ with $q\in[v_2,v_3]$; as $d(v_m,\cdot)$ is convex on $[v_2,v_3]$ with minimum $h_m$ at $v_2$, $d(x,q)\le d(v_m,v_3)-h_m\le s_m$, so $d(x,v_3)\le2s_m$. So some elliptic point $\tilde w\in Wv_3$, fixed by a rotation $\beta\in\Gamma_m$ by $\frac{2\pi}3$, lies at distance $r\le2s_m<2s_\infty$ from $\alpha$. The rotation $\beta$ fixes no point of $\partial\Hb$ and cannot interchange the endpoints of $\alpha$ (then $\beta^2$, a rotation of order $3$, would fix them), so Lemma~\ref{lem:jorgensen} and the trace identities give $4\sinh^2\frac L2(1+\frac34\cosh^2r)\ge1$, hence $L\ge\ell_{2,3}$.
\end{proof}

The bound $\ell_{2,3}$ is far from sharp; it is what the argument needs uniformly in $m$, and it loses mainly through the crude bound $2s_m$ on the distance from the axis to a point of order $3$. A search over all words of length at most $24$ in the generators finds no closed geodesic shorter than $0.98399$ on $\Orb(2,3,7)$, which is $2\operatorname{arccosh}\frac{1+2\cos(2\pi/7)}2=0.983986\ldots$, and the shortest length found increases with $m$, to $1.9213$ at $m=100$, below the systole $2\operatorname{arccosh}\frac32=1.9248$ of the modular orbifold (floating point, not interval arithmetic; this search is used in no proof).

On a region with metric $d\rho^2+w(\rho)^2d\vartheta^2$ and $w''=w$, the function $f=w^{-1/2}u(\rho)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rayleigh}
  f'^2w=u'^2+q\,u^2+\frac d{d\rho}\Big(-\frac{w'}{2w}u^2\Big),\qquad q=\frac12-\frac{w'^2}{4w^2}=\begin{cases}\frac14-\frac1{4\sinh^2\rho},&w=\sinh\rho,\\\frac14+\frac1{4\cosh^2\rho},&w=\cosh\rho,\end{cases}
\end{equation}
as $f'^2w=u'^2-\frac{w'}wuu'+\frac{w'^2}{4w^2}u^2$ and $\frac d{d\rho}(-\frac{w'}{2w}u^2)=-(\frac{w''}{2w}-\frac{w'^2}{2w^2})u^2-\frac{w'}wuu'$.

\begin{proposition}[Unbounded orders]\label{prop:N1}
For $m\ge7$ and $j\ge1$, $\lambda_j(\Orb(2,3,m))\le\frac14+\pi^2(j+1)^2/h_m^2$. Moreover, for every $N\ge1$ and $\delta>0$, among any $J+1$ distinct orders $m\ge7$, where $J=(\lfloor\Lambda_N/\delta\rfloor+1)^{N-1}$ and $\Lambda_N=\frac14+\pi^2N^2/h_7^2$, there are two, $m\ne m'$, with $|\lambda_j(\Orb(2,3,m))-\lambda_j(\Orb(2,3,m'))|<\delta$ for all $j<N$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the cone ball of Proposition~\ref{prop:233}, with metric $d\rho^2+\sinh^2\rho\,d\vartheta^2$, $\vartheta\in\R/\frac{2\pi}m\Z$, cut $[a,h_m]$, $0<a<h_m$, into $j+1$ intervals of length $\ell=(h_m-a)/(j+1)$ and let $f_i=(\sinh\rho)^{-1/2}u_i$ with $u_i$ a sine arch on the $i$-th interval, zero elsewhere. These Lipschitz functions have disjoint supports, and by \eqref{eq:rayleigh}, as $u_i$ vanishes at the ends and $q\le\frac14$, $\int|\nabla f_i|^2/\int f_i^2\le\frac14+\pi^2/\ell^2$. The min--max principle on their span gives the bound; let $a\to0$. As $h_m$ increases with $m$, $\lambda_j\in[0,\Lambda_N]$ for $j<N$, and two of $J+1$ vectors $(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{N-1})$ lie in a common product of the $\lfloor\Lambda_N/\delta\rfloor+1$ intervals $[i\delta,(i+1)\delta)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The order bound cannot be dropped]\label{rem:Mneeded}
Let $A\ge\frac\pi3$ and $\varepsilon\le\ell_{2,3}$. For every $N$ and $\delta$ there is $M$, for instance $M=(\lfloor\Lambda_N/\delta\rfloor+1)^{N-1}+7$, such that $\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ contains $\Orb(2,3,m)$ and $\Orb(2,3,m')$, of different signatures, whose first $N$ eigenvalues agree to within $\delta$; whatever rule decides the signature from such data is wrong for one of them. So $N$ and $\delta$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:E} cannot depend on $A$ and $\varepsilon$ alone; an area bound and a systole bound do not bound the orders. For $A<\frac\pi3$ the question does not arise: then $g=0$ and $n=3$ by the case analysis of Lemma~\ref{lem:elem}, and a hyperbolic triad $p\le q\le r$ has $\frac1p+\frac1q\le\frac56$, so $\frac1r\ge1-\frac{\Area}{2\pi}-\frac56\ge\frac16-\frac A{2\pi}>0$ bounds $r$, and only finitely many triples occur.
\end{remark}

Figure~\ref{fig:E1} shows the computed eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_6$ of $\Orb(2,3,m)$ for $21$ orders from $7$ to $4096$, on logarithmic axes, with the bounds of Proposition~\ref{prop:N1} (thin dashed, one for each $j$, in the shade of its $\lambda_j$, darkest for $\lambda_1$; as the bound for $\lambda_j$ has the numerator $(j+1)^2$, it runs close to the curve of $\lambda_{j+1}$) and the line $\frac14$; panel~(b) shows $h_m^2(\lambda_j-\frac14)/\pi^2$ with dotted levels at $j^2$. The spectrum of $\Orb(2,3,m)$ is the union of the Neumann and Dirichlet spectra of the triangle; we computed both by high-order finite elements at two mesh levels, which agree to at least $2.3\times10^{-10}$ relative. Every computed eigenvalue lies below its bound and decreases in $m$ (an observation, not proved here), and $\lambda_1$ falls from $44.89$ at $m=7$ to $0.473$ at $m=4096$; the computed values suggest $\lambda_j\to\frac14$, which Proposition~\ref{prop:N1} does not prove (it gives only the upper bounds; the convergence would follow if the convergence of small eigenvalues along elliptic degeneration \cite[Cor.~5.5]{garbinjorgenson2018} applied to this family, whose limit $PSL(2,\Z)\backslash\Hb$ has no eigenvalue in $(0,\frac14)$, which we have not checked), and the plotted quantity $h_m^2(\lambda_j-\frac14)/\pi^2$ in panel (b) is still between $6\%$ and $16\%$ above $j^2$, the value for an interval of length $h_m$, at $m=4096$: an observation and not a result.

\subsection{The systole: an open question}\label{sec:systole}

Pinching a closed geodesic produces small eigenvalues, but this does not show that the systole bound is needed. Fix $k\in\{3,4\}$, $b>0$ and $a$ with $\sinh a\sinh b=\cos\frac\pi k$; in $\Hb$ take orthogonal lines $\beta,\beta'$ through $O$, and let $Q_{k,b}$ be the quadrilateral bounded by the two lines perpendicular to $\beta$ at distance $b$ from $O$ and the two perpendicular to $\beta'$ at distance $a$. In the hyperboloid, with $O=(1,0,0)$ and $\beta,\beta'$ in the planes $x_2=0$, $x_1=0$, the unit normals $(\sinh b,\cosh b,0)$ and $(\sinh a,0,\cosh a)$ of adjacent sides have inner product $-\sinh a\sinh b$, of modulus less than $1$, so these sides meet at an angle with cosine $\sinh a\sinh b$ or its negative (Lemma~\ref{lem:H}, proof of (H3)); the quarter of $Q_{k,b}$ cut out by $\beta,\beta'$ has three right angles and angle sum less than $2\pi$, so its fourth angle is acute, equal to $\frac\pi k$. Let $\Orb_{k,b}$ be the double of $Q_{k,b}$, a sphere with four cone points of order $k$. For $k=3$ these are the orbifolds $\Orb_\vartheta$ of Section~\ref{sec:practice}, with $\vartheta=\log(\sinh a/\sinh b)$, so Table~\ref{tab:practice} runs the test along this pinching family.

\begin{proposition}[Pinching]\label{prop:N2}
$\Orb_{k,b}$ contains an embedded collar $\{|\rho|<a\}\times\R/4b\Z$, with metric $d\rho^2+\cosh^2\rho\,ds^2$, about a separating closed geodesic of length $4b$; so $\ell(\Orb_{k,b})\le4b$, $\lambda_1(\Orb_{k,b})\le\sinh a/((a^2+2)\sinh a-2a\cosh a)$ and $\lambda_j(\Orb_{k,b})\le\frac14+\frac1{4\cosh^2(a/2)}+\frac{4\pi^2(j+1)^2}{a^2}$. As $b\to0$, $\lambda_1\to0$ and $\limsup\lambda_j\le\frac14$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In Fermi coordinates $(\rho,s)$ about $\beta$ the sides perpendicular to $\beta$ are $s=\pm b$, and the other two sides are at distance at least $a$ from $\beta$, their common perpendiculars with $\beta$ having length $a$; so $\{|\rho|<a,|s|\le b\}$ lies in $Q_{k,b}$, and its double is the collar, whose core, the double of $\beta\cap Q_{k,b}$, separates the two pairs of cone points. The reflection in $\beta$ induces an isometry exchanging the two sides. For $\lambda_1$ take $f=\rho/a$ on the collar and $\pm1$ outside, odd under that isometry, so $\int f=0$, and compute the Rayleigh quotient, keeping in $\int f^2$ only the part over the collar, which is why the bound depends neither on $k$ nor on the area; for $\lambda_j$ use \eqref{eq:rayleigh} with $w=\cosh\rho$ and $j+1$ sine arches on disjoint intervals of length $a/(2(j+1))$ in $[\frac a2,a)$, where $q\le\frac14+\frac1{4\cosh^2(a/2)}$.
\end{proof}

Since $Q_{k,b}$ is symmetric under $a\leftrightarrow b$, $\lambda_1(\Orb_{k,b})\to0$ also as $b\to\infty$, and, as $\lambda_1(\Orb_{k,b})$ is continuous in $b$ (the quadrilaterals, and hence the orbifolds, for nearby $b$ are related by bi-Lipschitz maps with constants near $1$, as in Section~\ref{sec:intro}), the intermediate value theorem gives $b,b'$ in a compact interval with $\lambda_1(\Orb_{3,b})=\lambda_1(\Orb_{4,b'})$: two orbifolds of different signatures, with systoles in a compact range, share $\lambda_1$, which only says that $N=2$ is too small.

\begin{problem}[Is a systole bound needed?]\label{prob:systole}
Fix $A$ and $M$. (a) Is there an $N$, independent of $\varepsilon$, such that orbifolds in $\Cl(A,0^+,M)=\bigcup_{\varepsilon>0}\Cl(A,\varepsilon,M)$ with the same first $N$ eigenvalues have the same signature? (b) Can $N$ and $\delta>0$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:E} both be chosen independently of $\varepsilon$?
\end{problem}

Part (a) is a signature-level analogue of the conjecture of Buser and Courtois \cite[pp.~523--524]{busercourtois1990}, in a loose sense: the signature is determined by the heat invariants, the smooth part of the spectrum, while their conjecture concerns full isospectrality, which the closed geodesics carry. With $A$ and $M$ fixed only finitely many signatures occur, so the pigeonhole argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:N1} is not available. Under pinching, as recalled in Section~\ref{sec:intro}, the small eigenvalues converge to those of the cusped limit and infinitely many eigenvalues accumulate at $\frac14$. So a negative answer to (b) would follow from two degenerating families of different signatures whose cusped limits have the same eigenvalues below $\frac14$, while (a) asks for exact equality and is not settled by such limits. In our setting: the upper bounds above give $\limsup\lambda_j\le\frac14$, and the missing input is a matching lower bound $\lambda_j\ge\frac14-o(1)$ for $2\le j<N$ along both families. For surfaces both available routes to such a bound are theorems: under degeneration the spectral counting functions converge below $\frac14$ \cite{hjl1997}, a closed hyperbolic surface of Euler characteristic $\chi$ has $\lambda_{-\chi}>\frac14$ \cite{otalrosas2009}, and Ballmann, Matthiesen and Mondal bound the number of small eigenvalues by $-\chi$ for complete surfaces of finite type \cite{bmm2017}. What is missing here is an orbifold version of either: the first for the lift of the degeneration to a fixed torsion-free cover, whose spectrum contains that of the orbifold, together with the spectrum below $\frac14$ of the cusped limit; or the second for cone metrics with angles $2\pi/k$. We have not carried out either. The growth of our $N$ as $\varepsilon\to0$ (Section~\ref{sec:E}) is a property of the formulas, not of the least admissible $N$.

\subsection{The trace formula for the heat function}

Dryden and Strohmaier state the trace formula of Theorem~\ref{thm:trace} for $h=\hat g$ with $g\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ even \cite[eq.~(1)]{drydenstrohmaier2009}; in it, the elliptic term of a cone point of order $m$ sums over the $m-1$ nontrivial elements of its stabiliser, each once, and $\mathrm E_m(t)\to b_0(m)=\frac{m^2-1}{12m}$ as $t\downarrow0$. The following approximation extends the formula to the heat function.

\begin{lemma}[The heat function is admissible]\label{lem:admissibleB}
Fix an even $\omega\in C_c^\infty(\R)$, $0\le\omega\le1$, $\omega=1$ on $[-1,1]$, and let $g_X=g_t\,\omega(\cdot/X)$. As $X\to\infty$, every term of the trace formula for $\hat g_X$ tends to the corresponding term for $\hat g_t$; hence Theorem~\ref{thm:trace} holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Counting eigenvalues.} Let $\lambda_j=\frac14+r_j^2$ and take $\psi\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ even, nonnegative, $\int\psi=1$, supported in $[-\epsilon,\epsilon]$ with $2\epsilon<\ell(\Orb)$, $\epsilon\le1$; then $|\hat\psi|\le1$ and $\hat\psi(r)\ge\cos(\epsilon r)\ge\frac12$ for $|r|\le1$. The function $\eta_T(r)=\hat\psi(r-T)^2+\hat\psi(r+T)^2=\hat g$ for $g=2\cos(Tu)(\psi*\psi)(u)$, supported in $[-2\epsilon,2\epsilon]$, so the hyperbolic side vanishes for it; its identity term is $O(T+1)$, each elliptic term $O(1)$, and the finitely many imaginary $r_j$ contribute $O(1)$; so, as $\eta_T\ge\frac14$ on $|r-T|\le1$, $\#\{j:|r_j-T|\le1\}=O(T+1)$, hence $\#\{\lambda_j\le x\}=O(x)$.
\emph{Convergence.} On the hyperbolic side $0\le g_X\le g_t$, with equality on $[0,X]$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:counting}(i) gives dominated convergence. With $f_X=g_t(1-\omega(\cdot/X))$, $\hat g_t-\hat g_X=\hat f_X$ and $\|f_X^{(k)}\|_{L^1}\to0$ ($k\le3$), so $|\hat f_X(r)|\le\epsilon_X\min(1,|r|^{-3})$ with $\epsilon_X\to0$, which handles the identity and elliptic terms and, as $\sum_j\min(1,|r_j|^{-3})<\infty$, the real $r_j$; for imaginary $r_j$, $|\hat f_X(r_j)|\le\int|f_X|e^{|u|/2}\to0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The cone and area terms}

\begin{lemma}[Closed form]\label{lem:PhiB}
For $0<|u|<\pi/m$, $\Phi_m(u)=\dfrac{\cot u-m\cot(mu)}{4m\sin u}$. Writing $u/\sin u=\sum_{i\ge0}\sigma_iu^{2i}$, $\sigma_0=1$, $\sigma_i>0$,
\[
  m\,\phi_k(m)=\frac14\sum_{n=1}^{k+1}\sigma_{k+1-n}\,\frac{4^n|B_{2n}|}{(2n)!}\,\big(m^{2n}-1\big),
\]
with $B_{2n}$ the Bernoulli numbers; every $\phi_k(m)$ is positive and strictly increasing in $m>1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$(\sin\theta\sin(\theta-u))^{-1}=(\cot(\theta-u)-\cot\theta)/\sin u$, and the $\cot\theta_j$ cancel in pairs $j,m-j$. The function $\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\cot(x+\pi j/m)-m\cot(mx)$ is $\pi/m$-periodic, has no poles and tends to $0$ as $\operatorname{Im}x\to\pm\infty$, so it vanishes; at $x=-u$ this gives the closed form. From $\frac x2\coth\frac x2=\sum_nB_{2n}x^{2n}/(2n)!$ at $x=2iu$, $\cot u-m\cot(mu)=\sum_{n\ge1}4^n|B_{2n}|(m^{2n}-1)u^{2n-1}/(2n)!$, using $\operatorname{sgn}B_{2n}=(-1)^{n+1}$; multiply by $u/\sin u=\prod_n(1-u^2/n^2\pi^2)^{-1}$, which has positive coefficients, and by $1/(4mu)$. Every term, divided by $m$, is a positive multiple of $m^{2n-1}-m^{-1}$, positive and strictly increasing for $m>1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:remcone}]
For $0<a<2\pi$ let $F_a(r)=e^{-ar}/(1+e^{-2\pi r})$; Euler's beta integral gives $\int F_a(r)e^{sr}dr=[2\sin((a-s)/2)]^{-1}$ for $a-2\pi<s<a$, so the moments $\mathfrak m_n(a)=\int r^nF_a$ are its derivatives at $s=0$, and
\[
  \sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{\mathfrak m_{2k}(2\theta_j)}{2m\sin\theta_j}=\frac{d^{2k}}{ds^{2k}}\Phi_m\Big(\frac s2\Big)\Big|_{s=0}=\frac{(2k)!}{4^k}\phi_k(m).
\]
Put $g_k(m)=(-1)^k\frac{(2k)!}{k!\,4^k}\phi_k(m)$, of sign $(-1)^k$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:PhiB}, so that $b_l(m)=\sum_{k\le l}g_k(m)\frac{(-1/4)^{l-k}}{(l-k)!}$. For $x\ge0$ the Taylor remainder $e^{-x}-\sum_{k<K}(-x)^k/k!$ has the sign $(-1)^K$ and modulus at most $x^K/K!$. (a) With $x=tr^2$, multiplying by the positive weight $F_{2\theta_j}(r)/(2m\sin\theta_j)$, integrating and summing over $j$, $\rho_K(t)=e^{t/4}\mathrm E_m(t)-\sum_{k<K}g_k(m)t^k$ has the sign $(-1)^K$ and $|\rho_K(t)|\le|g_K(m)|t^K$. Since
\[
  \mathrm E_m(t)-\sum_{l<K}b_l(m)t^l=e^{-t/4}\rho_K(t)+\sum_{k<K}g_k(m)\,t^k\Big(e^{-t/4}-\sum_{i<K-k}\frac{(-t/4)^i}{i!}\Big),
\]
where the bracket has the sign $(-1)^{K-k}$ and modulus at most $(t/4)^{K-k}/(K-k)!$, every term has the sign $(-1)^K$, and the modulus of the sum is at most $t^K\sum_{k\le K}|g_k(m)|4^{k-K}/(K-k)!=|b_K(m)|t^K$, which is strictly increasing in $m$ because every $\phi_k$ is. (b) From $r\tanh\pi r=|r|-2|r|/(e^{2\pi|r|}+1)$, $\frac{4\pi}{\Area}\mathrm I(t)=e^{-t/4}(\frac1t-J(t))$ with $J(t)=4\int_0^\infty re^{-tr^2}(e^{2\pi r}+1)^{-1}dr$. With $\mu_k=4\int_0^\infty r^{2k+1}(e^{2\pi r}+1)^{-1}dr>0$, $J(t)-\sum_{k<K}\frac{(-1)^k\mu_k}{k!}t^k$ has the sign $(-1)^K$ and modulus at most $\frac{\mu_K}{K!}t^K$; $\frac1t(e^{-t/4}-\sum_{i\le K}\frac{(-t/4)^i}{i!})$ has the sign $(-1)^{K+1}$ and modulus at most $\frac{t^K}{4^{K+1}(K+1)!}$; and the cross terms $-\frac{(-1)^k\mu_k}{k!}t^k(e^{-t/4}-\sum_{i<K-k}\frac{(-t/4)^i}{i!})$ have the sign $(-1)^{K+1}$ and modulus at most $\frac{\mu_k4^{k-K}}{k!(K-k)!}t^K$. The moments give $\alpha_k=\frac{(-1/4)^k}{k!}-\sum_{j<k}\frac{(-1)^j\mu_j}{j!}\frac{(-1/4)^{k-1-j}}{(k-1-j)!}$, every term of sign $(-1)^k$, the closed form of Section~\ref{sec:heat} following from $\mu_k=(1-2^{-2k-1})(-1)^kB_{2k+2}/(k+1)$ and $B_{2l}(\frac12)=(2^{1-2l}-1)B_{2l}$; so the moduli above sum to $|\alpha_{K+1}|t^K$. Finally \eqref{eq:cj} and \eqref{eq:Grem} follow from (a), (b) and Theorem~\ref{thm:trace}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cone polynomials]\label{lem:conepolyB}
For every $l\ge0$, $p_l$ is an even polynomial with rational coefficients, of degree exactly $2l+2$, with $p_l(1)=0$ and leading coefficient $a_l=|B_{2l+2}|/(2(l+1)!\,(2l+1))$. Writing $p_l(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{l+1}a_{l,k}x^{2k}$, $p_l(x)/x=\sum_{k=1}^{l+1}a_{l,k}\psi_k(x)$ with $a_{l,l+1}=a_l$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:PhiB} and the definition of $p_l$, $p_l$ is a combination with positive rational weights of the even polynomials $m^{2n}-1$, $1\le n\le l+1$; the degree $2l+2$ occurs only for $k=l$, $n=l+1$, with coefficient $\frac1{4^l}\cdot\frac{(2l)!}{l!}\cdot\frac{4^{l}|B_{2l+2}|}{(2l+2)!}=a_l$. Then $\sum_ka_{l,k}=p_l(1)=0$ gives $p_l(x)/x=\sum_k a_{l,k}(x^{2k-1}-x^{-1})$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Heat invariants of a signature}

Write $C_l(m)=\sum_ib_l(m_i)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:conepolyB}, for finite multisets $m,m'$ of positive reals, $C_l(m)=C_l(m')$ for $0\le l\le L-2$ if and only if $\Psi_k(m)=\Psi_k(m')$ for $1\le k\le L-1$: the system is triangular with diagonal $\pm a_{l,l+1}\ne0$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sigdata}]
$c_1$ agrees exactly when the areas do, that is, when $2g+n-R(m)=2g'+n'-R(m')$; then the terms $\alpha_{j-1}\Area/4\pi$ of \eqref{eq:cj} agree, and $c_2,\dots,c_L$ agree exactly when $C_0,\dots,C_{L-2}$ do, that is, when $\Psi_k(m)=\Psi_k(m')$ for $k\le L-1$. An order $1$ changes neither $n-R$ nor any $\Psi_k$, as $\psi_k(1)=0$; so the paddings have $R(U)=R(V)$, by the area equation and $d\in\Z$, and $\Psi_k(U)=\Psi_k(V)$, hence $P_{2k-1}(U)=P_{2k-1}(V)$ for $k\le L-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Parity]\label{lem:parityB}
In $\Q[x_1,\dots,x_N]$ let $s_j=\sum_ix_i^j$ and $e_j$ the elementary symmetric functions. For odd $j$, $e_j$ lies in the ideal generated by $s_1,s_3,\dots,s_j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Newton's identities $e_j$ is a $\Q$-linear combination of products $s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_r}$ with $\sum i_t=j$, and for odd $j$ some $i_t$ is odd.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sep}]
(i) Let the two signatures $(g;m)$, $(g';m')$, of area $A$, share $c_1,\dots,c_L$, with paddings $U,V$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:sigdata}, and let $U^*,V^*$ be $U,V$ with their common elements removed; they are disjoint, still satisfy $R(U^*)=R(V^*)$ and $P_j(U^*)=P_j(V^*)$ for odd $j\le2L-3$, and $|V^*|-|U^*|=|V|-|U|=2(g-g')$, so $\kappa=|U^*|+|V^*|$ is even. If $\kappa=0$, then $m=m'$ up to orders $1$, which signatures do not contain, and $g=g'$. Suppose $2\le\kappa\le2L$, and let $X=U^*\uplus(-V^*)$, $\kappa$ nonzero numbers. For odd $j\le\kappa-3\le2L-3$, $s_j(X)=P_j(U^*)-P_j(V^*)=0$, so $e_j(X)=0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:parityB}; and $e_{\kappa-1}(X)=e_\kappa(X)\sum_{x\in X}x^{-1}=e_\kappa(X)(R(U^*)-R(V^*))=0$. So $\prod_{x\in X}(z-x)$ is even, $X=-X$ with multiplicities, and for $x>0$, $\mult_{U^*}(x)=\mult_{V^*}(x)$; by disjointness $U^*=V^*=\emptyset$, against $\kappa\ge2$. Hence different signatures sharing $c_1,\dots,c_L$ have $\kappa\ge2L+2$. Each cone point adds at least $\frac12$ to $\frac A{2\pi}+2-2g$, so $n+4g\le A/\pi+4$, and likewise for the other signature; and $|U|+|V|=n+n'+|d|$ with $d=2(g'-g)+n'-n$, which is $2\max(n+g-g',n'+g'-g)\le2(\lfloor A/\pi\rfloor+4)$; as $\kappa\le|U|+|V|$, $L\ge\lfloor A/\pi\rfloor+4$ forces equal signatures.
(ii) Let $\nu(a)=\mult_m(a)-\mult_{m'}(a)$ for $2\le a\le M$ and $y_a=a^2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:sigdata} with $L=M$, the areas agree and $0=\Psi_k(m)-\Psi_k(m')=\sum_{a=2}^M\frac{\nu(a)}a(y_a^k-1)$ for $1\le k\le M-1$. Subtracting the column $a=1$ from the others in the Vandermonde matrix $(y_a^k)_{0\le k\le M-1,\,1\le a\le M}$ shows that $\det(y_a^k-1)_{1\le k\le M-1,\,2\le a\le M}=\prod_{1\le a<b\le M}(y_b-y_a)\ne0$. So $\nu=0$, then $n=n'$, $R(m)=R(m')$ and, by the equal areas, $g=g'$.
\end{proof}
