Same capture: "Journal Impact Factor 0.7 (2024)", "Submission to first decision (median) 12 days",
and a journal update "SNAPP – A New Manuscript Submission system": "Snapp (Springer Nature’s Article

10.1007/s10455-025-10024-1). The ethics page states: "Content published in this journal is peer

**Fit of the manuscript.** The paper studies heat invariants and inverse spectral problems for
closed hyperbolic 2-orbifolds (spectral geometry of the Laplacian on singular Riemannian
2-orbifolds, heat-trace asymptotics, Selberg-trace-formula consequences, stability of recovery).
This is global analysis on (orbi)manifolds in the sense of the first two scope sentences, and
squarely in the journal's spectral-geometry line: the bibliography already cites four AGAG papers
(`stanhope2005`, `adfg2008` Abreu–Dryden–Freitas–Godinho, `rsw2008`, `schueth2025`), and the
Schueth paper on heat coefficients of surfaces with conical singularities appeared in AGAG in
December 2025, per the home-page capture. The number-theoretic parts (Prouhet–Tarry–Escott,
elliptic curves, reciprocal sums) are not in the listed areas. The cover letter should present
them as tools for an inverse spectral question, not as results in their own right. Choosing the
handling Co-Editor-in-Chief (Agricola or Bögelein) is asked for at submission; Agricola (differential
geometry) is the closer match. This choice belongs to the authors.

## 5. Reference style

Guidelines, "References", verbatim:

> Reference citations in the text should be identified by numbers in square brackets. Some examples:
> 1. Negotiation research spans many disciplines [3].
> 2. This result was later contradicted by Becker and Seligman [5].
> 3. This effect has been widely studied [1-3, 7].

> The entries in the list should be numbered consecutively.
> If available, please always include DOIs as full DOI links in your reference list (e.g. “https://doi.org/abc”).

Example entry: "Hamburger, C.: Quasimonotonicity, regularity and duality for nonlinear systems of
partial differential equations. Ann. Mat. Pura Appl. 169, 321–354 (1995)". And: "Always use the
standard abbreviation of a journal’s name according to the ISSN List of Title Word Abbreviations
... If you are unsure, please use the full journal title."

So the style is **numbered**, in square brackets, Springer math/physics format. That is what the
repo's `sn-mathphys-num` option and `sn-mathphys-num.bst` produce. In `sn-jnl.cls`, the
`sn-mathphys-num` option is the "Math and Physical Sciences Reference Style" (class comment,
line 71 after CR→LF conversion). The guidelines do not name a `.bst` file.

## 8. AI-use policy

### 8a. AGAG guidelines' own LLM paragraph

Guidelines, "Title Page" (capture 2025-10-01), verbatim. The text is word for word the JGA paragraph
quoted in OUTSTANDING.md §1a:

> Large Language Models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT, do not currently satisfy our authorship criteria. Notably an attribution of authorship carries with it accountability for the work, which cannot be effectively applied to LLMs. Use of an LLM should be properly documented in the Methods section (and if a Methods section is not available, in a suitable alternative part) of the manuscript. The use of an LLM (or other AI-tool) for "AI assisted copy editing" purposes does not need to be declared. In this context, we define the term "AI assisted copy editing" as AI-assisted improvements to human-generated texts for readability and style, and to ensure that the texts are free of errors in grammar, spelling, punctuation and tone. These AI-assisted improvements may include wording and formatting changes to the texts, but do not include generative editorial work and autonomous content creation. In all cases, there must be human accountability for the final version of the text and agreement from the authors that the edits reflect their original work.

Guidelines, "Generative AI Images" (under both Artwork and SI), verbatim:

> Please check Springer’s policy on generative AI images and make sure your work adheres to the principles described therein.

### 8c. Springer Nature, "AI use in manuscript preparation" (live, 2026-10-07)

Verbatim key extracts (same file hash as 2026-10-05; OUTSTANDING.md §1b has longer extracts):

> Springer Nature applies a risk-assessment approach, focusing on how AI is used, rather than whether it is used.

The risk table (cells read row by row from the page):

> Green (low risk) | AI supports clarity and presentation without altering scholarly content | Grammar and language editing; improving readability and flow; formatting; translation; summarising author-written text | Outputs must be checked for accuracy and meaning | Clearly describe use and confirm author accountability
>
> Amber (requires caution and care from authors) | AI contributes to structuring or shaping written content | Generating outlines; drafting sections based on author input; suggesting alternative phrasing or framing; reference management support; creating visual content with verifiable source data or material | Risk of generic or inaccurate content, loss of disciplinary voice, fabricated or incorrect citations, all AI generated or assisted content must be clearly labelled | Clearly describe use and confirm author accountability
>
> Red (not permitted) | AI replaces authorship or generates scholarly content without oversight | Writing results or discussion independently; generating scientific claims or interpretations; producing data or figures as if empirical; listing AI as an author; creating content from text prompts or without verifiable source data or material | Undermines originality, accuracy, and accountability; risk of infringing copyright, licensing, or other intellectual property rights | Not permitted

> AI can assist with expression, but the argument, interpretation, and conclusions must be author-led.

> The use of AI to generate, analyse, visualise or enhance visual content may be permitted where authors meet the following requirements:
> - AI use is disclosed appropriately, including model and purpose of use;
> - the underlying data, source material, methods or computational outputs can be inspected, verified and are clearly attributed;
> - any AI-assisted processing, analysis or code generation has been subject to appropriate human review and validation;

> Where AI-generated or AI-assisted visual content is published, information about the AI system used, the purpose of its use, and the extent of its contribution should be provided in an AI Declaration in your manuscript.
> Specific disclosure should be provided within the caption or legend of the visual content.

> You remain fully accountable for everything submitted in your name.

## 9. Consequences for the manuscript

Checked against `paper/jga/manuscript.tex` as of 2026-10-07 (35 pp. main PDF, 8 pp. supplement).
Changes needed or advisable:

1. **Template and reference style: no change.** `\documentclass[pdflatex,sn-mathphys-num]{sn-jnl}`
   is the recommended template (same December 2024 zip) and gives numbered square-bracket
   citations, as AGAG asks. Submit `references.bib` with `sn-mathphys-num.bst`, or paste the
   `.bbl` into the `.tex` as the LaTeX-support page advises. Include all style files, figures and
   the compiled PDF.
2. **AI-use placeholder.** Its current place (Appendix "Computational methods and
   reproducibility", paragraph "Use of AI tools") still satisfies the AGAG wording, which is the
   same as JGA's. The placeholder's own text is accurate for AGAG and can stay. Because the
   2026 Springer Nature policy asks for disclosure even of Green-level use and speaks of "an AI
   Declaration in your manuscript", the authors may also wish to add a short one-line pointer
   under "Statements and Declarations" (e.g. a "Use of AI tools" item referring to the appendix).
   This is optional, and the content remains the authors' decision. If any figure was made or
   modified with AI help, a disclosure in that figure's caption is also required (8c).
3. **"Statements and Declarations".** The heading matches AGAG's exactly and sits before the
   bibliography (in `\backmatter`, before `\begin{appendices}`). Funding, Competing interests,
   Ethics approval and consent, Data and code availability and Author contributions cover AGAG's
   list. The Author-contributions placeholder must be filled. Author contributions and competing
   interests must also be entered in the submission interface (Snapp), and only that version is
   published.
4. **Acknowledgements.** AGAG says acknowledgments "should be placed in a separate section on the
   title page". The manuscript has `\bmhead{Acknowledgements}` in the back matter (the template
   default). This is a minor point, likely handled in production, but note it for the authors.
5. **Unpublished work in the reference list.** `@unpublished{companion}` ("Companion manuscript,
   in preparation"), cited in §1, conflicts with "Personal communications and unpublished works
   should only be mentioned in the text." Either cite it in the text only (e.g. "(P. Gang et al.,
   in preparation)") or post it to arXiv first and cite the preprint.
6. **arXiv preprints.** `doylerossetti2011`, `dryden2004`, `chen2025survey` and `crootmaoyip2026`
   are preprints. The AGAG text ("published or accepted") is stricter than the publisher policy,
   which explicitly allows preprints ("Preprint at http://arxiv.org/abs/..."). Keeping them is
   defensible under the Springer Nature preprint policy. Check whether any has since appeared in
   a journal and update it. Disclose the paper's own arXiv posting, if any, with DOI and licence at
   submission.
7. **DOIs.** "please always include DOIs as full DOI links". Check that `sn-mathphys-num.bst`
   prints `https://doi.org/...` for every entry with a DOI.
8. **Abstract, keywords, MSC.** The abstract has about 165 words (rough count, math counted as
   one word), within 150–250. It has 6 keywords (limit 4–6) and MSC codes in `\pacs[MSC
   Classification]`. No change.
9. **Supplement.** The guidelines say to call it "Online Resource" (e.g. "Online Resource 1"), to
   name the file `ESM_1.pdf`, to give it a concise caption, and to put the article title, journal
   name, author names and the corresponding author's affiliation and e-mail in the file. The
   manuscript currently says "the supplement" / "the electronic supplement" and uses
   cross-references via `xr`. Wording changes (and a caption) are needed, and the supplement's
   title block should be checked. The SI is "published as received", so the PDF must be final.
10. **Figures.** Check captions against "Fig." bold and no terminal punctuation (production may
    restyle), widths ≤119 mm for a small-format journal (not verifiable: no fetched page gives
    AGAG's format), and the "Patterns ... in addition to colors" accessibility point.
11. **Length.** No limit is stated, so no cut is required by the rules.
12. **Handling editor.** The submitter must choose one of the two Co-Editors-in-Chief (Agricola,
    Bögelein).
13. **Recheck before submitting.** The only guidelines capture is from 2025-10-01. Open the live
