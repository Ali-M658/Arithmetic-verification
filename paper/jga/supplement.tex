\section{Searches and explicit pairs}\label{sec:searches}

\emph{Pencil splittings (Remark~\ref{rem:witnesses}).} A pencil splitting is a pair $(A,B)$ of $4$-multisets of nonzero integers with $e_1(A)=e_1(B)=0$, $e_3(A)=e_3(B)$, $e_4(A)=e_4(B)$, such that $Z=A\uplus(-B)$ has four positive and four negative entries and no pair $\{z,-z\}$; its witness is the pair formed by $Z_{>0}$ and $-Z_{<0}$, two disjoint multisets of orders with equal $R$, $P_1$ and $P_3$. The eight orders of the witness are the absolute values of the entries of $A$ and $B$, so a bound on the entries is a bound on the orders. Counting distinct witnesses (unordered, and primitive when the eight orders have gcd $1$), pencil splittings with entries of absolute value at most $130$ give $17$ witnesses, $14$ of them primitive, and at most $220$ give $49$, $30$ primitive (every witness is rechecked exactly, with $P_5$ different; Section~\ref{sec:methods}). An exhaustive search of all pairs of $4$-multisets of orders in $[2,440]$ with equal $(R,P_1,P_3)$ finds $193$ pairs, $107$ of them primitive; among the primitive ones with largest order at most $130$, $220$, $440$ there are $16$, $33$, $107$, of which $14$, $30$, $101$ have a pencil splitting. The six without one are
\[
\begin{gathered}
(16,16,74,74;\,11,37,44,88),\quad(11,21,99,99;\,9,51,51,119),\\
(17,17,88,136;\,11,52,52,143),\quad(77,88,182,208;\,68,112,154,221),\\
(154,175,308,350;\,143,200,280,364),\quad(22,50,209,418;\,19,85,170,425),
\end{gathered}
\]
each written as the two orbifolds' orders separated by a semicolon. Chen's survey lists the witness of Theorem~\pref{thmC}(3) as an ideal solution of type $(-1,1,3)$ \cite[A.685]{chen2025survey}; for the type $(-1,1,3,5)$ that $n=5$ requires it gives the identity \cite[(3.33)]{chen2025survey} but lists no numerical solution. For $n=5$, all $216{,}071{,}394$ multisets of orders in $[2,120]$, grouped by $P_1$ and keyed on the exact integers $(P_3,P_5,\operatorname{lcm}(2,\dots,120)R)$, give no witness.

\emph{The first open case (Remark~\ref{rem:T3}).} A genus pair of size $8$ sharing three heat invariants has shape $(3,5)$. For each $5$-multiset $V$ of positive integers with gcd $1$ and entries at most $220$, the $3$-element side is the root set of the cubic $z^3-e_1z^2+Re_3z-e_3$ with $e_1=P_1(V)$, $R=R(V)$ and $e_3=(P_3(V)-e_1^3)/(3-3e_1R)$; it must have three positive rational roots. The search rejects $V$ by the modular test of Remark~\ref{rem:T3}, described in Section~\ref{sec:methods}(ii): of the $4{,}325{,}115{,}770$ such $V$, fifteen pass it, and none of their cubics has a rational root. Real solutions of the shape exist: $\{1,1,1,1,7\}$ has exactly one partner triple, $\{0.2664,4.2830,6.4506\}$.

\emph{Explicit pairs.} Table~\ref{tab:pairs} writes out every pair of Example~\ref{ex:ptepairs} and every diamond and square of Figure~\pref{fig:F4}, except the Prouhet squares with $L\ge4$, whose cone lists are in a data file of the public repository. The pairs with $L\ge4$ are built by Lemma~\pref{lem:combine} from two pieces $P$ and $B$, scaled so that the reciprocal sum vanishes, after deleting pairs $\{z,-z\}$ (each recipe is recorded with the pair in the code and data deposit). Here $X\pm c$ is the shift of Proposition~\pref{prop:shift}, $\pm\{a,b\}=\{\pm a,\pm b\}$, and $[x]=[y]$ is an equal sum of odd powers, entering as $x\uplus(-y)$. \emph{Genus pairs.} $L=4$: $P$ the shift by $c=-\frac{11}2$ of $\pm\{7,11,18\}$ against $\pm\{3,14,17\}$, $B$ from $[1,13,17,23]=[3,9,21,21]$. $L=5$: $P$ the shift by $-\frac92$ of $\pm\{2,16,21,25\}$ against $\pm\{5,14,23,24\}$, $B$ from $[3,19,37,51,53]=[9,11,43,45,55]$. $L=6$: $P$ the shift by $-43$ of $\pm\{18,245,331,471,508\}$ against $\pm\{103,189,366,452,515\}$, $B$ from $[7,91,173,269,289,323]=[29,59,193,247,311,313]$. $L=7$: $P$ and $B$ the shifts by $-27$ and $-\frac{27}2$ of $\pm\{22,61,86,127,140,151\}$ against $\pm\{35,47,94,121,146,148\}$. \emph{Equal-count pairs.} $L=4$: the $7$-sets $\{-51,-33,-24,7,13,38,50\}$ and $\{-120,-110,-23,-13,38,105,123\}$. $L=5$: Letac's $9$-sets $\{-98,-82,-58,-34,13,16,69,75,99\}$ and $\{-169,-161,-119,-63,8,50,132,148,174\}$. $L=6$: $[23,163,181,341,347,407]=[37,119,221,311,371,403]$ and $[43,161,217,335,391,463]=[85,91,283,287,403,461]$. $L=7$: the shifts by $-\frac{129}2$ and $-\frac{27}2$ of the size-$12$ solution above.

\section{Stability of the recovery}\label{sec:stability}\label{sec:certificates}

This section is about the stability of the algebraic recovery map, from $(c_1,\dots,c_n)$ to the positive real orders $m$ of a sphere with $n$ cone points, $\mu=\max_im_i$; for real $m$ the $c_j$ are the rational functions of Proposition~\pref{prop:heatinput} evaluated at $m$, an algebraic device, and heat invariants of an orbifold only for integer orders. Its statements about orbifolds are the thresholds below which integer orders are recovered exactly (Theorem~\ref{thm:S4}, Proposition~\ref{prop:S5}, Corollary~\ref{cor:aposteriori}). How far these are from a heat trace is shown, end to end, by the minimal pair (Remark~\ref{rem:fromtrace}): for $\Orb(2,8,8)$ the certified threshold is $\delta_{\rm cert}=2.341\times10^{-3}$ (Table~\ref{tab:thresholdsfull}); Lemma~\pref{lem:fromtrace} reaches it from exact values of the heat trace at eight times $\tau,\frac\tau2,\dots,\frac\tau{128}$ only for $\tau\le1.5\times10^{-2}$, whose smallest time is then about $1.2\times10^{-4}$, and from values with an error $\eta$, at half that $\tau$, with smallest time $6\times10^{-5}$, only for $\eta\le1.3\times10^{-8}$; in our scans no set of at most $16$ times in an interval $[a,T]$ with $a\ge10^{-3}$ reaches it, while the computed spectra fix the heat trace only for $t\ge1.5\times10^{-3}$, to about $3\times10^{-11}$, with estimated rather than proved errors. So the proved route from a computed spectrum to $c_3$ is not open, and the recovery of Section~\ref{sec:blind} of the supplement is validated, not proved. Genus $0$ and a known $n$ are assumed, and the data are heat invariants. Lemma~\pref{lem:fromtrace} in Appendix~\pref{app:derivation} obtains them from finitely many values of a heat trace with a proved error, under a-priori bounds on the largest order, the systole, the diameter and the area; the error is strongly amplified at small times (Remark~\ref{rem:fromtrace}). The genus and $n$ are not supplied by the heat trace in this section; Theorem~\pref{thm:intro-algebra}(i) needs $n$, and against all of $\Sig$ the counts of Section~\pref{sec:signature} apply. The \emph{recovery map} has three layers: (1) $\tilde\cI=\mathbf F^{-1}(\tilde c-h_0)$, a triangular linear map (Proposition~\ref{prop:frontend}); (2) $\tilde e=\mathbf M(\tilde\cI)^{-1}\mathbf b(\tilde\cI)$, the system of Theorem~\pref{thmB}; (3) the roots of $\tilde q(z)=\sum_j(-1)^j\tilde e_jz^{n-j}$, $\tilde e_0=1$; and, for integer orders, (4) rounding their real parts. Distances between multisets are $\dist(m,m')=\min_\pi\max_i|m_i-m'_{\pi(i)}|$.

\begin{proposition}[Front end]\label{prop:frontend}
With $p_\nu(x)=\sum_ka_{\nu,k}x^{2k}$, $(c_1,\dots,c_n)^\top=\mathbf F\,\cI_n+h_0(n)$, where $\mathbf F$ is lower triangular, indexed from $0$, with $\mathbf F_{00}=-\frac12$, $\mathbf F_{\nu+1,0}=-\frac12\alpha_{\nu+1}+(-1)^\nu a_{\nu,0}$, $\mathbf F_{\nu+1,k}=(-1)^\nu a_{\nu,k}$ ($1\le k\le\nu+1$), and $h_0(n)=\frac{n-2}2(1,\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_{n-1})$. $\mathbf F$ does not depend on $n$, and its diagonal entries, $\mathbf F_{00}=-\frac12$ and $\mathbf F_{\nu+1,\nu+1}=(-1)^\nu|B_{2\nu+2}|/(2(\nu+1)!(2\nu+1))$, are nonzero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By (\pref{eq:cj}), $c_1=(n-2-R)/2$ and $c_{\nu+2}=\alpha_{\nu+1}(n-2-R)/2+(-1)^\nu\sum_i\sum_ka_{\nu,k}m_i^{2k-1}$; the diagonal is Lemma~\pref{lem:conepoly}.
\end{proof}

If every $|\delta c_j|\le\delta$, the $r$-th entry of $\cI_n$ has error at most $\amp_r\delta$, the absolute row sum of $\mathbf F^{-1}$: $\amp_0,\dots,\amp_4=2,14,498,4062,\frac{56230}3$ (for example $P_3=-18\tilde c_1-120\tilde c_2-360\tilde c_3$, $\tilde c=c-h_0$).

\begin{theorem}[Stability of the recovery]\label{thm:stab}
Let $m$ be $n\ge2$ positive real orders, with distinct values $a$ of multiplicities $k_a$; for non-integral orders the $c_j(m)$ are the algebraic device of Proposition~\pref{prop:heatinput}, not heat invariants of an orbifold, and for $n=2$ the statement is algebraic only, since a hyperbolic sphere has at least three cone points. There are $\delta_0(m)>0$ and $C_a(m)$, computable from $m$ (Section~\ref{app:certificates}, Theorem~\ref{thm:S3}; they are closed formulas except for one matrix norm, $\cond$, which Theorem~\ref{thm:S2}(a) bounds explicitly), such that if $|\tilde c_j-c_j(m)|\le\delta\le\delta_0(m)$ for $1\le j\le n$, then $\mathbf M(\tilde\cI)$ is invertible and, for every $a$, exactly $k_a$ of the recovered roots lie within $C_a(m)\,\delta^{1/k_a}$ of $a$. If $m$ is integral, there is an explicit $\delta_{\rm thm}(m)>0$ (Section~\ref{app:certificates}, Theorem~\ref{thm:S4}) such that $|\tilde c_j-c_j(m)|\le\delta_{\rm thm}(m)$ for $j\le n$ implies that rounding the real parts of the recovered roots returns $m$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The three layers are controlled one at a time (Section~\ref{app:certificates}, which gives every constant). By Proposition~\ref{prop:frontend} the scaled errors in $\cI_n$ are at most $\Xi_\mu\delta$ for an explicit $\Xi_\mu$. The matrix of Theorem~\pref{thmB} factors as $\mathbf M=\mathbf Y^{-1}\mathbf B$ with $\det\mathbf B=\pm\prod_{i<j}(m_i+m_j)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:hurwitz}), so the system is singular exactly where $\Hur_{n-1}(\chi_m)=0$, and $\mathbf M^{-1}=\mathbf B^{-1}\mathbf Y$; Theorem~\ref{thm:S2} bounds the error in the scaled $e_k$ linearly in $\delta$. Finally Rouch\'e's theorem on circles about each order $a$ puts exactly $k_a$ roots within a radius proportional to $\delta^{1/k_a}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:S3}); for integer orders $\delta_{\rm thm}$ makes every radius at most $\frac12$, so the disjoint discs $|z-a|<\frac12$ contain all $n$ roots and rounding returns $m$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:S4}).
\end{proof}

So recovery is Lipschitz at simple orders and H\"older of exponent $1/k$ at a $k$-fold coincidence. The exponent $1/k$ is sharp for arbitrary data (Proposition~\ref{prop:sharpexp}(ii)); for data coming from real orders it is $\frac12$ at a double order (Proposition~\ref{prop:sharpexp}(i)) and when all the orders are equal (Proposition~\ref{rem:triple}); other configurations are not settled.

\begin{proposition}[Sharpness of the exponents]\label{prop:sharpexp}
(i) \emph{Double order, realisable data.} For $m(s)=(a+s,a-s,m_3,\dots,m_n)$ the heat invariants are smooth and even in $s$, so they move by $O(s^2)$ while the orders move by $s$; for $(2,8,8)$, with $a=8$, $\delta R=s^2/(4(64-s^2))$, $\delta P_1=0$, $\delta P_3=48s^2$. (ii) \emph{$k$-fold order, general data.} Let $q_s(z)=((z-a)^k-s^k)g(z)$, $g$ real and monic of degree $n-k$, $a\ne0$, $g(0)\ne0$, $\prod_{i<j}(z_i+z_j)\ne0$ for the roots of $q_0$. For small $s>0$ the data of $q_s$ are within $O(s^k)$ of those of $q_0$, the system of Theorem~\pref{thmB} is invertible at them and returns $q_s$, and its roots $a+se^{2\pi ij/k}$ lie at distance $s$ from $a$. For $k\ge3$ these roots are not all real, so these data are not the heat invariants of any real multiset.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Exchanging $a\pm s$ fixes the multiset. (ii) The data are smooth in the coefficients near $q_0$, as $e_n(q_0)\ne0$, and $\mathbf M$ is invertible at the data of $q_0$ (Theorem~\pref{thmB}), hence at those of $q_s$ for small $s$; a real multiset with these data would have the elementary symmetric functions of $q_s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Realisable data when all orders are equal]\label{rem:triple}
Let $n\ge3$, $a>0$, and let $a+d=(a+d_1,\dots,a+d_n)$ be real orders, $|d_i|<a$, whose heat invariants are within $\delta$ of those of $(a,\dots,a)$. Then $\max_i|d_i|\le((498+42a^2)\delta/(2a))^{1/2}$, and the family $(a+s,a-s,a,\dots,a)$ shows that the exponent $\frac12$ is attained.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As $P_1(a+d)-P_1(a)=\sum_id_i$, $(P_3-3a^2P_1)(a+d)-(P_3-3a^2P_1)(a)=\sum_id_i^2(3a+d_i)\ge2a\sum_id_i^2$. Under $|\delta c_j|\le\delta$, $|\delta P_1|\le\amp_1\delta=14\delta$ and $|\delta P_3|\le\amp_2\delta=498\delta$, so the left side is at most $(498+42a^2)\delta$. The family has $\delta P_1=0$, $\delta P_3=6as^2$ and all other changes $O(s^2)$. The argument uses only $c_1,c_2,c_3$, which is why $n\ge3$ is assumed, and it needs no smallness of $\delta$. (For $n=1$, an algebraic case with no hyperbolic orbifold, recovery is Lipschitz; mixed clusters are not settled.)
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A-posteriori use]\label{cor:aposteriori}
Let $m$ be the integer orders of a sphere with $n$ cone points, and let $\tilde c$ be data with $|\tilde c_j-c_j(m)|\le\delta_1$ for $j\le n$, where $\delta_1$ is a proved bound on the error of the data; an estimated $\delta_1$ gives no conclusion, and the proved $\delta_1$ of Lemma~\pref{lem:fromtrace} needs values of the heat trace at times shorter than computed spectra resolve (Remark~\ref{rem:fromtrace}). Let $m^*$ be integer orders, for instance those returned by rounding the recovery map at $\tilde c$, and $\delta_2=\max_{j\le n}|\tilde c_j-c_j(m^*)|$. If $\delta_1+\delta_2\le\delta_{\rm thm}(m^*)$, then $m=m^*$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The exact invariants $c(m)$ satisfy $|c_j(m)-c_j(m^*)|\le\delta_1+\delta_2$, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:stab} at $m^*$ rounding the recovery from the data $c(m)$ returns $m^*$. But from exact data the system of Theorem~\pref{thmB} returns $e(m)$, as $\prod_{i<j}(m_i+m_j)\ne0$, so the recovered roots are $m$ itself, and rounding returns $m$. Hence $m=m^*$.
\end{proof}

The same argument gives $m=m^*$ when, instead of $\delta_1+\delta_2\le\delta_{\rm thm}(m^*)$, the sharper certificate of Proposition~\ref{prop:S5} in Section~\ref{app:certificates}, evaluated in exact arithmetic, holds at $m^*$ with radii $\delta_1+\delta_2$; the thresholds $\delta_{\rm cert}$ quoted in this section rest on it, and their values are in Table~\ref{tab:thresholdsfull} of the supplement.

Here $\delta_2$ is computable, while $\delta_1$ must come with the data; for data read off a heat trace, Lemma~\pref{lem:fromtrace} supplies it under a-priori bounds, but only from values of the trace at times shorter than computed spectra resolve (Remark~\ref{rem:fromtrace}). The blind recovery in the supplement (Section~\ref{sec:blind}) applies the criterion with heuristic error bars for $\delta_1$, so its conclusions are validated, not proved.
\subsection{Constants of the stability theorem}\label{app:certificates}

This appendix gives the constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:stab} with their proofs and the explicit threshold $\delta_{\rm thm}$, and a sharper certificate, evaluated in exact arithmetic (Proposition~\ref{prop:S5}). We use the notation of Section~\ref{sec:stability}: the front end $c=\mathbf F\cI_n+h_0$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:frontend}, the system $\mathbf M(\cI)e=\mathbf b(\cI)$ of Theorem~\pref{thmB}, the recovery map, $\mu=\max_im_i$, and the scale-free quantities $\hat m=m/\mu$, $\hat e_k=e_k/\mu^k$, $\hat\cI=(\mu R,P_1/\mu,P_3/\mu^3,\dots)$; $\hat e$ solves $\mathbf M(\hat\cI)\hat e=\mathbf b(\hat\cI)$ because the system is weighted homogeneous. For a distinct order $a$ of multiplicity $k_a$ put $\widehat{\sepn}_a=\min_{b\ne a}|\hat a-\hat b|$ ($+\infty$ if $a$ is the only distinct order) and $\hat\Pi_a=\prod_{b\ne a}|\hat a-\hat b|^{k_b}$, the products over the distinct orders $b\ne a$.

\begin{lemma}[Hurwitz factorisation]\label{lem:hurwitz}
Let $\mathbf B,\mathbf Y$ be the $n\times n$ matrices with rows $0\le k\le n-1$, the columns of $\mathbf B$ indexed by $1\le j\le n$, as are those of $\mathbf M$ by the unknowns $e_j$, and those of $\mathbf Y$ by $0\le i\le n-1$, $\mathbf B_{k,j}=(-1)^{j+1}e_{2k+1-j}$, $\mathbf Y_{k,i}=e_{2(k-i)}$ for $i\le k\le n-2$, $\mathbf Y_{n-1,n-1}=(-1)^ne_n$, other entries of $\mathbf Y$ zero ($e_i=0$ outside $[0,n]$). Then $\mathbf B=\mathbf Y\mathbf M$, $\det\mathbf B=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{i<j}(m_i+m_j)$ and $\mathbf M^{-1}=\mathbf B^{-1}\mathbf Y$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $f=\prod_i(1+m_iz)=E_f(z^2)+zO_f(z^2)$ and $d(z)=\sum_kd_kz^k=E_d(z^2)+zO_d(z^2)$, $\mathbf Bd$ is the coefficient vector of $E_fO_d-E_dO_f$, and $(\mathbf Md)_j=[z^{2j+1}](zO_d-\mathcal TE_d)$ for $j\le n-2$; since $\mathcal T\equiv zO_f/E_f$ modulo $z^{2n-1}$, comparing coefficients of $E_f(zO_d-\mathcal TE_d)$ gives the rows $k\le n-2$, and the top row uses $R=e_{n-1}/e_n$. Then $\det\mathbf Y=(-1)^ne_n$ and Theorem~\pref{thmB} give the determinant.
\end{proof}

So $\|\mathbf M^{-1}\|\le\|\mathbf B^{-1}\|\,\|\mathbf Y\|$, a one-sided bound, with $\det\mathbf B=\pm\Hur_{n-1}(\chi_m)$. Let $\cond=\|\mathbf M(\hat\cI)^{-1}\|_\infty$, $\mathfrak t_k(n)=[w^k]\operatorname{artanh}(w)\sec^2((n+1)\operatorname{artanh}w)$,
\[
  \zeta_n=\max\Big(1,\ \max_{1\le j\le n-2}\ \sum_{0\le i<j,\ 2j-2i\le n}\mathfrak t_{2i+1}(n)\Big),
\]
\[
  \mathfrak r_n(\hat m)=\max\Big(\hat e_n,\ \max_{0\le j\le n-2}\sum_{i\le j}\mathfrak t_{2i+1}(n)\,\hat e_{2j-2i}\Big),
\]
the indices $j$ running over the rows of $\mathbf M$ (so $\zeta_3=1$, $\zeta_4=\frac{79}3$, $\zeta_5=\frac{14048}{15}$).

\begin{theorem}[Lipschitz dependence of $e$ on $\cI_n$]\label{thm:S2}
Let $\eta=\max\big(\mu|\tilde R-R|,\max_{1\le l\le n-1}|\tilde P_{2l-1}-P_{2l-1}|/\mu^{2l-1}\big)$ and assume $\eta\le1$. (a) $\cond\le n\binom{2n}n^{(n-1)/2}2^{n-1}/\prod_{i<j}(\hat m_i+\hat m_j)$. (b) If $\cond\,\zeta_n\eta\le\frac12$, then $\mathbf M(\tilde\cI)$ is invertible and $\max_k|\tilde e_k-e_k|/\mu^k\le2\cond\,\mathfrak r_n(\hat m)\,\eta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) By Lemma~\ref{lem:hurwitz}, $\mathbf M^{-1}=\mathbf B^{-1}\mathbf Y$ at $\hat\cI$. An entry of $\hat{\mathbf B}^{-1}$ is an $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ cofactor of $\hat{\mathbf B}$ divided by $|\det\hat{\mathbf B}|=\prod_{i<j}(\hat m_i+\hat m_j)$; by Hadamard's inequality the cofactor is at most the product of the Euclidean norms of $n-1$ columns, and each column has squared norm at most $\sum_l\hat e_l^2\le\sum_l\binom nl^2=\binom{2n}n$, as $\hat e_l\le\binom nl$. So $\|\hat{\mathbf B}^{-1}\|_\infty\le n\binom{2n}n^{(n-1)/2}/\prod(\hat m_i+\hat m_j)$, and every row of $\hat{\mathbf Y}$ has absolute sum at most $\sum_{l\ \mathrm{even}}\binom nl=2^{n-1}$. (b) All $P_k>0$, so coefficientwise $\hat U\le n\operatorname{artanh}w$ and $|\delta\hat U|\le\eta\operatorname{artanh}w$; as the coefficients of $\tanh$ are those of $\tan$ up to sign, $|\tilde{\mathcal T}_k-\mathcal T_k|\le\eta\,\mathfrak t_k(n)$. Hence, in the scaled variables, the residual $r=\mathbf b(\tilde\cI)-\mathbf M(\tilde\cI)e$ has $\|r\|_\infty\le\mathfrak r_n\eta$, $\|\mathbf M(\tilde\cI)-\mathbf M(\cI)\|_\infty\le\zeta_n\eta$, and $\mathbf M(\tilde\cI)(\tilde e-e)=r$. Since $\cond\,\zeta_n\eta\le\frac12$, $\mathbf M(\tilde\cI)=\mathbf M(\cI)(I+X)$ with $\|X\|_\infty\le\frac12$, so by the Neumann series $\mathbf M(\tilde\cI)$ is invertible with $\|\mathbf M(\tilde\cI)^{-1}\|_\infty\le2\cond$, and $\|\tilde e-e\|_\infty\le2\cond\,\mathfrak r_n\eta$.
\end{proof}

On every test multiset the exact $\cond$ is at most $3.511$, so (a) is lossy. Ostrowski's root bound \cite[Th\'eor\`eme~XXX, (71,1)]{ostrowski1940}, in the second part of the memoir that begins in \cite{ostrowski1940a}, has exponent $1/n$ everywhere; Rouch\'e's theorem gives the local exponent.

\begin{theorem}[Heat invariants to cone orders]\label{thm:S3}
Let $\delta=\max_{1\le j\le n}|\tilde c_j-c_j(m)|$, $\Xi_\mu=\max(\mu\amp_0,\max_{1\le r\le n-1}\amp_r\mu^{1-2r})$, and suppose $\Xi_\mu\delta\le1$ and $\cond\,\zeta_n\Xi_\mu\delta\le\frac12$. For a distinct order $a$, if
\[
  r_a=\mu\Big(\frac{2^{2-k_a}3^n\cond\,\mathfrak r_n\,\Xi_\mu\,\delta}{\hat\Pi_a}\Big)^{1/k_a}\le\mu\,\frac{\min(\widehat{\sepn}_a,1)}2,
\]
then the recovered polynomial, rescaled, has exactly $k_a$ roots within $r_a$ of $a$. So $\dist(\text{roots},m)\le\max_aC_a\delta^{1/k_a}$ with $C_a=r_a\delta^{-1/k_a}$ when this holds for every $a$; this is Theorem~\ref{thm:stab} with $\delta_0(m)$ the largest $\delta$ for which the three hypotheses hold.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:frontend} gives $\eta\le\Xi_\mu\delta$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:S2}(b) gives $|\tilde e_k-\hat e_k|\le\varepsilon=2\cond\,\mathfrak r_n\Xi_\mu\delta$. On $|z-\hat a|=r\le\min(\widehat{\sepn}_a,1)/2$, $|q(z)|\ge r^{k_a}\hat\Pi_a2^{-(n-k_a)}$, while $|z|\le\frac32$ gives $|\tilde q-q|<2^{1-n}3^n\varepsilon$; apply Rouch\'e's theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit threshold]\label{thm:S4}
For integer orders $m$ let
\[
  \delta_{\rm thm}(m)=\min\Big(\frac1{\Xi_\mu},\ \frac1{2\cond\,\zeta_n\Xi_\mu},\ \min_a\frac{\hat\Pi_a\,2^{k_a-1}}{3^n(2\mu)^{k_a}\cdot2\cond\,\mathfrak r_n\Xi_\mu}\Big).
\]
If $|\tilde c_j-c_j(m)|\le\delta_{\rm thm}(m)$ for $1\le j\le n$, rounding the real parts of the recovered roots returns $m$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The third term makes $r_a\le\frac12$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:S3}; the discs $|z-a|<\frac12$ are disjoint and contain $k_a$ roots each, hence all of them.
\end{proof}

Here $\cond$ is the exact matrix norm; the values of $\delta_{\rm thm}$ in Table~\ref{tab:thresholdsfull} of the supplement are computed with it in exact arithmetic, and with the closed-form bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:S2}(a) in its place the threshold becomes explicit but smaller, by large factors. The certificate actually used for the examples is the sharper Proposition~\ref{prop:S5}.

The certificate below is sharper than Theorem~\ref{thm:S4} and equally rigorous for errors in the heat invariants; it is evaluated in rational arithmetic, and the thresholds $\delta_{\rm cert}$ of Section~\ref{sec:stability} rest on it. Index $\cI_0=R$, $\cI_k=P_{2k-1}$, let $U(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}P_{2k-1}z^{2k-1}/(2k-1)$, $\mathcal T=\tanh U$ and $\operatorname{sech}^2U=\sum_ks_kz^k$, all series truncated after $z^{2n-3}$, and write $|\cdot|$ coefficientwise or entrywise and $*$ for the Cauchy product.

\begin{proposition}[Certificates]\label{prop:S5}
Let $m$ be integer orders, $e=e(m)$, $\mathbf M=\mathbf M(\cI)$ and $\delta_1,\dots,\delta_n\ge0$ data radii. Put (1) $\Delta=|\mathbf F^{-1}|\delta$ and $|\delta U|(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Delta_kz^{2k-1}/(2k-1)$; (2) $\tau^{\rm lin}=|\operatorname{sech}^2U|*|\delta U|$, $\tau^{\rm rem}=\tan(U+|\delta U|)-\tan U-\sec^2U\cdot|\delta U|$, $\tau=\tau^{\rm lin}+\tau^{\rm rem}$; (3) $\rho_j=\sum_{i\le j}\tau_{2i+1}e_{2j-2i}$ and $\rho^{\rm rem}_j=\sum_{i\le j}\tau^{\rm rem}_{2i+1}e_{2j-2i}$ for $j\le n-2$, $\rho_{n-1}=\Delta_0e_n$, $\rho^{\rm rem}_{n-1}=0$, $\Delta_M$ the matrix with entry $\tau_{2i+1}$ in row $j$, column $e_{2j-2i}$ ($0\le i<j\le n-2$, $2j-2i\le n$), entry $\Delta_0$ in row $n-1$, column $e_n$, and zeros elsewhere, and $A=|\mathbf M^{-1}|\Delta_M$; (4) if $v=(I-A)^{-1}\mathbf 1$ exists and is positive, $E=(I-A)^{-1}|\mathbf M^{-1}|\rho$ and $\xi=|\mathbf M^{-1}|\rho^{\rm rem}+AE$. Then every data vector with $|\tilde c_j-c_j(m)|\le\delta_j$ is recovered exactly after rounding if, for every distinct order $a$ and some $r_0\in\{\frac{20}{40},\dots,\frac1{40},\frac1{100},\frac1{1000}\}$, either (i) $r_0^{k_a}\prod_{b\ne a}(|a-b|-r_0)^{k_b}>\sum_jE_j(a+r_0)^{n-j}$, or (ii) $r_0^{k_a}\prod_{b\ne a}(|a-b|-r_0)^{k_b}>\sum_c\delta_c\sum_l|p_c^{(l)}(a)/l!|\,r_0^l+\sum_j\xi_j(a+r_0)^{n-j}$, where $p_c(z)=\sum_j(-1)^jG_{jc}z^{n-j}$, $G=-\mathbf M^{-1}J\mathbf F^{-1}$, $J_{j,k}=-\frac1{2k-1}\sum_{k-1\le i\le j,\,2j-2i\le n}e_{2j-2i}s_{2i+2-2k}$ ($j\le n-2$, $k\ge1$), $J_{n-1,0}=-e_n$ and the other entries of $J$ zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As formal series $\tilde{\mathcal T}-\mathcal T=\sum_jt_j((U+\delta U)^{2j+1}-U^{2j+1})$, with $|t_j|$ the coefficients of $\tan$; its linear part is $\operatorname{sech}^2(U)\delta U$, and since $U\ge0$ coefficientwise the rest is bounded by $\tau^{\rm rem}$. As the exact $e$ solves the exact system, $r_j=\sum_{i\le j}(\tilde{\mathcal T}_{2i+1}-\mathcal T_{2i+1})e_{2j-2i}$ and $r_{n-1}=(\tilde R-R)e_n$, so $|r|\le\rho$, the nonlinear part is at most $\rho^{\rm rem}$, $|\tilde{\mathbf M}-\mathbf M|\le\Delta_M$, and $\partial\mathcal T_{2i+1}/\partial P_{2k-1}=s_{2i+2-2k}/(2k-1)$ gives $J$. If $v>0$, $\operatorname{diag}(v)^{-1}A\operatorname{diag}(v)$ has row sums $1-1/v_i<1$, so the spectral radius of $A$, and of $X=\mathbf M^{-1}(\tilde{\mathbf M}-\mathbf M)$ since $|X|\le A$, is less than $1$, as $\rho(X)\le\rho(|X|)\le\rho(A)$; then $\tilde{\mathbf M}=\mathbf M(I+X)$ is invertible and $|\tilde e-e|\le|\mathbf M^{-1}||r|+A|\tilde e-e|$ gives $|\tilde e-e|\le E$, since $(I-A)^{-1}=\sum_kA^k\ge0$. Test (i) is Rouch\'e's theorem on $|z-a|=r_0$, whose lower bound uses $r_0<|a-b|$ for the other orders $b$, which holds as $r_0\le\frac12$ and distinct integer orders differ by at least $1$; for (ii), $\tilde e-e=G(\tilde c-c)+\xi'$ with $|\xi'|\le\xi$, and Taylor expansion of $p_c$ at $a$ bounds the linear part.
\end{proof}

For $n\le5$ the odd series $U$, $|\delta U|$, $\mathcal T$ have at most four nonzero coefficients ($z,z^3,z^5,z^7$) and the even series $\operatorname{sech}^2U$, $\sec^2U$ at most four ($1,\dots,z^6$); for $(2,2,2,2,3)$, $\operatorname{sech}^2U=1-121z^2+9328z^4-601472z^6$. So every entry of Table~\ref{tab:thresholdsfull} of the supplement can be re-derived in exact arithmetic.

\section{Computed spectra of the minimal pair}\label{sec:listening}

Figure~\pref{fig:F1} of the paper shows the two orbifolds of Theorem~\pref{thm:minimal}, which share their first two heat invariants, coloured by the diagonal of the heat kernel computed from their spectra.

\emph{The cone timescale.} The computed spectra of the minimal pair, about $2850$ eigenvalues each, show the two timescales on which the heat traces of different orbifolds differ; the method, the error budget and the fits are given below. The eigenvalue errors there are a-posteriori agreements between two discretisations, not enclosures, so what follows is a numerical illustration, used in no proof: the elliptic terms and the coefficients $d_j$ below are exact, while every number computed from the eigenvalues ($3.4\times10^{-14}$, the times $0.341$ and $0.360$, the factors of Section~\ref{sec:moduli}, $\ell^2/4t\approx24.5$) inherits estimated, not enclosed, errors. The difference $D(t)=\cZ_{\Orb(2,8,8)}(t)-\cZ_{\Orb(3,3,12)}(t)$ has the expansion $D(t)\sim\sum_{j\ge3}d_jt^{j-2}$, $d_3=\frac{25}{12}$, $d_4=-\frac{1775}{24}$, $d_5=\frac{153025}{48}$, starting with the third coefficient because the two orbifolds share $c_1,c_2$. A fit of the computed $D$ (below) recovers $d_3$ and $d_4$; for $t\le0.025$ the computed $D$ agrees with the exact difference of the elliptic terms of Theorem~\pref{thm:IEH} to $3.4\times10^{-14}$ (at $t=0.0293$, the last sampled time below $0.03$, the difference is $4\times10^{-13}$, the size there of the closed-geodesic term of $\Orb(3,3,12)$, which is not subtracted), which tests the computed spectra against the trace formula; the fit of $d_3$, $d_4$ is a test of the computation, not of these exactly known coefficients. The usable window is short because the cone coefficients grow rapidly: $|b_l(12)|\approx0.993$, $5.13$, $101$, $3.62\times10^3$, $1.84\times10^5$, $1.21\times10^7$, $9.67\times10^8$ for $l=0,\dots,6$. By Lemma~\pref{lem:growth}, $|b_l(m)|\sim(2\pi\sin\frac\pi m)^{-1}(\pi l)^{-1/2}\,l!\,(m/\pi)^{2l}$, so the terms $|b_l(m)|t^l$ decrease only while $l\lesssim\pi^2/(m^2t)$. Truncated at $K=\lfloor\pi^2/(\mu^2t)\rfloor$, with $\mu$ the largest cone order, the cone series leaves an error $O(t^{-1/2}e^{-\pi^2/(\mu^2t)})$ (Proposition~\pref{prop:opttrunc}); at every fixed order the bound is (\pref{eq:Grem}). For $\mu=12$ the expansion is informative only for $t$ well below $\pi^2/144\approx0.07$. In Figure~\ref{fig:F5}(a) the computed $D$ (heavy) follows the cone-point term of the trace formula (dashed), and the grey curves are the truncations $d_3t$, $d_3t+d_4t^2$ and $d_3t+d_4t^2+d_5t^3$ of its expansion, each useful only below the time at which it leaves the heavy curve; beyond $t\approx0.1$ the closed geodesics become visible, and near $t\approx0.36$ they reverse the sign of $D$ (between the sampled times $0.341$ and $0.360$).


\emph{Method.} The triangle orbifold $\Orb(p,q,r)$ is the double of a hyperbolic triangle $T$ with angles $\pi/p,\pi/q,\pi/r$. The reflection exchanging the two copies splits $H^1(\Orb)$ into even and odd parts, orthogonal for the $L^2$ product and the Dirichlet form, which restrict to $H^1(T)$ and $H^1_0(T)$; so the spectrum of $\Orb$ is the union, with multiplicity, of the Neumann and Dirichlet spectra of $T$, and $\cZ_\Orb=\cZ_N+\cZ_D$. The problems $-\Delta_Eu=\lambda wu$, $w=4/(1-|z|^2)^2$, were solved in the Poincar\'e disc with $H^1$ finite elements of order $p$ on NETGEN meshes \cite{schoberl1997} of uniform hyperbolic size $h$, with NGSolve \cite{ngsolve}; the geodesic side is an exact rational quadratic spline, curved to order $p$, and the finite-element area is exact to $2\times10^{-14}$. The production level is $(h,p)=(0.05,10)$, with $h$-sweeps at $p=10$ ($h=0.1,0.07$) and $p$-sweeps at $h=0.07$ ($p=8,10,12$). Eigenvalues were computed by shift-invert Lanczos iteration (ARPACK \cite{arpack1998}) in overlapping spectral windows of $200$ eigenvalues, one sparse factorisation per window, each eigenvalue taken from one window in the production run, all of them recomputed later with two windows (Limitations below; software versions are pinned in the code and data deposit): $1420$--$1440$ per triangle and boundary condition, up to $\lambda\approx21{,}700$ (Neumann) and $23{,}800$ (Dirichlet), about $2850$ per orbifold. Of these, $2002$ and $2001$ lie below $1.6\times10^4$, the range in which the trace test of (ii) below would detect a single missing or spurious eigenvalue; the companion paper \cite{companionB} uses only these, and for the family of Section~\ref{sec:moduli} only the eigenvalues below $0.8$ times the least sector maximum.

\emph{Convergence and error per eigenvalue.} The error estimate $\mathrm{err}_j$ of each eigenvalue is $|\lambda_j(0.05,10)-\lambda_j(0.07,12)|$, two independent discretisations, and $|\lambda_j(0.05,10)-\lambda_j(0.07,10)|$ is carried as a second estimate, larger than $\mathrm{err}_j$ for most eigenvalues but not for the lowest ones (Table~\ref{tab:conv}); neither is a bound. For the first $500$ eigenvalues the estimate is at most $2.9\times10^{-11}$ relative and the second estimate at most $1.2\times10^{-8}$. The empirical $h$-convergence at $p=10$ is $h^{13}$ to $h^{19}$ (medians over mid-spectrum modes; the meshes are not nested) and the $p$-convergence is geometric, a factor $150$ to $350$ per two orders; all modes converge from above. A corner singularity would cap the rate at an $h$-independent algebraic order; none occurs, as reflection predicts: at a corner of angle $\pi/k$ the reflections in the two sides generate a dihedral group of order $2k$, and the Neumann (Dirichlet) eigenfunctions extend to smooth functions invariant (respectively sign-twisted) under it. Table~\ref{tab:conv} shows representative eigenvalues.

\emph{Error budget.} By the mean value theorem $|e^{-\tilde\lambda t}-e^{-\lambda t}|\le t|\tilde\lambda-\lambda|e^{-\min(\lambda,\tilde\lambda)t}$, so the eigenvalue errors contribute at most $t\sum_j\mathrm{err}_je^{-(\lambda_j-\mathrm{err}_j)t}$ to a computed trace, with no cancellation assumed. The truncation tail satisfies $\sum_{\lambda>\Lambda}e^{-\lambda t}\le-n_\Lambda e^{-\Lambda t}+t\int_\Lambda^\infty e^{-\lambda t}N_{\rm up}(\lambda)\,d\lambda$, where $N_{\rm up}(\lambda)=A\lambda/4\pi\pm L\lambda^{1/2}/4\pi+C_{\rm up}$ is the Weyl law of the triangle (area $A$, perimeter $L$, sign $+$ for Neumann) with $C_{\rm up}$ twice the largest observed excess of the counting function over the two-term law plus $10$, and the integral is in closed form. On $0.0015\le t\le0.012$ the total for $D(t)=\cZ_{\Orb(2,8,8)}(t)-\cZ_{\Orb(3,3,12)}(t)$ is at most $2.9\times10^{-11}$, and $3.9\times10^{-10}$ with the second estimates; the tail is at most $7.6\times10^{-13}$, against $d_3t=3.1\times10^{-3}$ at $t=0.0015$.

\emph{Validation.} (i) \emph{Weyl law.} With area, boundary and constant terms, $N(\lambda)\approx A\lambda/4\pi\pm L\lambda^{1/2}/4\pi+c_2/2$, the residuals have mean $|{\cdot}|\le0.003$ and root mean square $0.66$--$0.70$ over $1420$ or more eigenvalues; without the boundary term they drift by about $\pm65$. (ii) \emph{Traces.} At the sampled times $0.0015\le t\le0.0293$, each computed trace agrees with the identity and elliptic terms of Theorem~\pref{thm:IEH}, the closed-geodesic term not being subtracted, to $7.1\times10^{-13}$, against an error bound of $1.5\times10^{-11}$; closed geodesics contribute up to about $4\times10^{-13}$ there, at $t=0.0293$, by $\Orb(3,3,12)$ (about $7.8\times10^{-13}$ at $t=0.03$), and less than $3\times10^{-15}$ for $t\le0.025$; $\cZ_N-\cZ_D$ agrees with the boundary term to $1.8\times10^{-13}$. A missing eigenvalue $\lambda$ would add $e^{-\lambda t}$, which exceeds the error bound at $t=0.0015$ when $\lambda\lesssim1.6\times10^4$, so a single missing eigenvalue below about $1.6\times10^4$ would be detected, while a missing eigenvalue paired with a spurious one nearby would not, and completeness is supported, not proved (Section~\ref{sec:listening}, Limitations); above that, about $1.6$ to $2.4\times10^4$, the test is blind. (iii) \emph{Bolza benchmark.} The Bolza surface covers $\Orb(2,3,8)$, so every eigenvalue of the $(2,3,8)$ triangle with a sign character of its reflection group occurs in Bolza's spectrum. The relation at the corner of angle $\pi/3$ leaves four characters: Neumann, Dirichlet, and two mixed conditions. The list of Strohmaier and Uski \cite[\S7.1 and the ancillary file of arXiv:1110.2150v4]{strohmaieruski2013}, \cite{strohmaieruski2018} has $42$ multiplicity-one eigenvalues below $998$; the four triangle problems give exactly $42$ eigenvalues in that range ($15$, $6$, $10$, $11$), matched one-to-one to $5.7\times10^{-11}$ relative, the precision of the published digits. Only the $21$ Neumann and Dirichlet ones belong to $\Orb(2,3,8)$. The Bolza surface also covers $\Orb(2,8,8)$, as its quotient by the rotations of order $8$ about the centre of the regular octagon (the centre and the vertex class become the cone points of order $8$, the four edge-midpoint classes one cone point of order $2$, and $\chi=-2/8$), so the spectrum of $\Orb(2,8,8)$ is contained in Bolza's; its first nonzero eigenvalue, $3.838887258842$, agrees with Bolza's $\lambda_1=3.83888725884220\ldots$ \cite{strohmaieruski2013} to all twelve decimals printed. (iv) \emph{Second machine.} The $(3,3,12)$ Neumann spectrum, $1434$ eigenvalues, was recomputed on a second machine; the largest relative difference is $1.0\times10^{-12}$, at most $0.15$ times the error estimate.

\emph{Prediction.} Both orbifolds have area $\pi/2$ and $c_2=\frac{67}{48}$, and $D(t)\sim\sum_{j\ge3}d_jt^{j-2}$ with $d_3=\frac{25}{12}$, $d_4=-\frac{1775}{24}$, $d_5=\frac{153025}{48}$ (Section~\pref{sec:locality}); its coefficients grow rapidly (Section~\pref{sec:locality}). For small $t$ the orbifold with the smaller $P_3$ has the larger heat trace ($D(t)$ changes sign near $t=0.36$, between the sampled times $0.341$ and $0.360$, below).

\emph{Result.} The model $D(t)/t=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n_c}\hat d_\nu t^{\nu-1}$ was fitted by unweighted least squares with a column-scaled Vandermonde matrix, over nine windows inside $[0.0015,0.012]$ and $n_c=2,\dots,11$. For each configuration the \emph{data error} is the worst-case change of a coefficient under any perturbation of the data within the error budget, $|\mathrm{pinv}|\cdot\mathrm{err}$, and the \emph{model error} the change from $n_c-1$ to $n_c$; the reported configuration minimizes the larger of the two and was chosen without reference to the prediction: window $[0.0015,0.012]$, $n_c=10$. It gives $\hat d_1=2.0833320\pm0.0000031$ and $\hat d_2=-73.9551\pm0.0062$, against $d_3=2.0833333$ and $d_4=-73.9583$. The error bars are $\max(\text{data error},\text{model error})$, heuristic and not standard errors; the deviations are $0.43$ and $0.52$ of them, and with the second eigenvalue estimates the bars are $1.4\times10^{-5}$ and $0.028$. Applied to the exact difference of the elliptic terms, the same fit recovers $d_3$ to about $10^{-6}$ and $d_4$ to about $0.005$, so the method is not the limiting factor. The fitted $t^{-1}$ and $t^0$ terms of the two traces agree to within the fit error. The computed $D(t)$ differs pointwise from the exact difference of the elliptic terms, with no geodesic term subtracted, by at most $3.4\times10^{-14}$ for $0.0015\le t\le0.025$ and by at most $4.0\times10^{-13}$ at the sampled times up to $t=0.0293$, the last below $0.03$; the larger value occurs at $t=0.0293$, where the shortest closed geodesic of $\Orb(3,3,12)$ contributes about that much (about $7.8\times10^{-13}$ at $t=0.03$ and $2.6\times10^{-15}$ at $t=0.025$). The coefficient $\hat d_3=3184.5\pm5.2$ (data error only) against $\frac{153025}{48}=3188.0$ is only a by-product; across stable configurations it ranges over $3180$--$3188$.

\emph{Closed geodesics.} The shortest lengths, from enumerating triangle-group words of length at most $10$ (the first two confirmed by the complete enumeration of closed geodesics of the companion paper, which also certifies the systole in interval arithmetic), are $2.2568$, $2.8816$, $3.0571$ for $(2,8,8)$ and $1.8626$, $2.9807$, $3.4027$ for $(3,3,12)$. With a generous multiplicity per length, the hyperbolic term is below $10^{-30}$ on the fit window. Fitting $\cZ-\mathrm I-\mathrm E$ on $[0.06,0.5]$ by the hyperbolic term gives leading multiplicities $1.997$ and $1.9998$: one geodesic, two orientations. $D(t)$ changes sign near $t=0.36$ (between the sampled times $0.341$ and $0.360$), solely because of the geodesics.

\emph{Limitations.} The eigenvalue errors are a-posteriori agreements between two discretisations, not enclosures, so nothing in Sections~\ref{sec:listening}--\ref{sec:moduli} is certified: the recovery of Section~\ref{sec:blind} is validated, not proved. The tail bound assumes that the counting function beyond the computed range stays below the Weyl law plus twice its largest observed excess. The spectra were computed with single-window spectral slicing, which the experiment of Section~\ref{sec:moduli} showed can miss an eigenvalue at a window boundary. All four were recomputed with the double-window solver of Section~\ref{sec:moduli} at the same production level, every eigenvalue below $\lambda\approx2.0\times10^4$ being covered by two windows and no window miss being found: in each problem the two computations have the same number of eigenvalues below a common cut, every value agrees within the second estimate, and the largest relative difference is $1.4\times10^{-14}$. A missing eigenvalue paired with a spurious one nearby would escape the trace test, and the tail of its budget is a Weyl-law estimate, not a bound; completeness is supported, not proved.

\section{Blind recovery}\label{sec:blind}

The recovery chain of Section~\ref{sec:stability} was run on the computed spectra of Section~\ref{sec:listening} under a protocol committed to the repository before the pipeline and before any result (dated 1 October 2026; the protocol file and its version history are part of the deposit named in the data statement of the paper). \emph{What was assumed:} each specimen is the double of a hyperbolic triangle, so it has genus $0$ and exactly $n=3$ cone points, and $K=-1$; the universal front-end constants for $n=3$ were used. \emph{What was blinded:} only the labels; the specimens were called A and B, the pipeline read the four eigenvalue files without parsing their names, and the label-to-truth map was used once, after every estimate and verdict had been written. No area, perimeter, $c_2$, geodesic length or Weyl constant derived from the orders was used; the truncation tail was bounded from a fit of the counting function of the data alone. The test is easy by design: it separates two candidates whose third coefficients differ by about $10^4$ error bars, so it exercises the pipeline rather than a hard inference.

The traces $\cZ(t)$ were fitted as $t\cZ(t)=\sum_{j\le d}\gamma_jt^j$ over the windows and degrees of the protocol, with data error and model error as in Section~\ref{sec:listening}; the error bar of each coefficient is the larger of the best configuration's $\max(\text{data},\text{model})$ and half the range over the eight best configurations, again heuristic. The candidate was obtained by solving the system of Theorem~\pref{thmB} in exact arithmetic and rounding, and then checked by Corollary~\ref{cor:aposteriori}, with the certificate of Proposition~\ref{prop:S5} at the candidate and radii equal to the distance from the estimates to the candidate's exact invariants ($\delta_2$) plus the error bars ($\delta_1$). The check is rigorous only given the error bars; since these are heuristic, a-posteriori discretisation agreements and not enclosures, it validates the recovery but does not certify it.

\section{Changing the shape}\label{sec:moduli}

\emph{The family.} The signature $(0;3,3,3,3)$ is hyperbolic, with area $4\pi/3$ and a $2$-dimensional Teichm\"uller space. Each member of the family is the double of a geodesic quadrilateral with all angles $\pi/3$, symmetric under $z\mapsto\bar z$ and $z\mapsto-\bar z$; the two mirror axes cut it into four congruent Lambert quadrilaterals, and with $a,b$ the distances from the centre to two adjacent sides, $\sinh a\sinh b=\cos\frac\pi3$, leaving one parameter $\vartheta=\log(\sinh a/\sinh b)$. Eight members, $\vartheta\in\{0,0.4,\dots,2.8\}$, were computed, about $5800$ eigenvalues each, of which $5451$ to $5572$ lie below the least of the sector maxima, about $1.6\times10^4$ (the companion paper uses only the $4357$ to $4451$ below $0.8$ times that maximum, where the eigenvalue counts of its three most accurate discretisations agree exactly in every sector), with every eigenvalue covered by two independent shift-invert windows (misses counted and repaired). Their systoles, computed by the enumeration of closed geodesics and equal to $4b(\vartheta)$ to $10^{-30}$ (a numerical identification), fall strictly from $2.634$ to $0.694$, so the members are pairwise non-isometric, a numerical statement, and every pair has different systoles. The worst eigenvalue error is $1.3\times10^{-10}$ relative ($8.3\times10^{-9}$ by the second estimate). The family is a real one-parameter slice, the orbifolds with an orientation-reversing isometry fixing the four cone points: such an isometry is an involution (its square preserves orientation, has finite order and fixes four points, so it is the identity) and fixes a single circle through the four cone points (two fixed circles would make the quotient an annulus, whose double is a torus), so the orbifold is the double of a quadrilateral with all angles $\frac\pi3$, and the four points on a circle can be moved by a M\"obius map to a configuration symmetric under two reflections with the same cross-ratio, which by uniqueness of the hyperbolic metric in its conformal class are isometries; Proposition~\pref{thm:sharp} does not need genericity.

\emph{Same expansion, different spectra.} Every member's heat trace equals $\mathrm I+4\mathrm E_3$ to at most $1.2\times10^{-12}$ wherever closed geodesics are negligible, against an error budget of $2.2\times10^{-11}$, and on $t\in[0.0025,0.0037]$ the eight traces are the same function to $1.3\times10^{-13}$: signature locality (Section~\pref{sec:locality}) in the data. The eigenvalues move with the shape: $\lambda_1$ falls from $4.1224$ to $0.4348$, and the first $200$ eigenvalues move by up to $89\%$ (Figure~\pref{fig:F6} of the paper).

\emph{The difference is the closed geodesics.} For all $28$ pairs, $\cZ_i-\cZ_j$ equals $\Hyp_i-\Hyp_j$, computed from the closed geodesics enumerated in floating point up to length $6.5$ (checked by the integrality of the weights $w/\ell$ and by agreement with the computed traces) with nothing fitted, to at most $1.1\times10^{-13}$ for $t\le0.33$, where the traces differ by up to $0.78$. The difference leaves the noise at a time $t^*$ set by the shorter systole alone, with $\ell^2/4t^*\approx24.5$ in every case. Once it is $10^3$ times the noise, its ratio to the leading term of Proposition~\pref{thm:sharp} is between $0.9997$ and $1.0000$. The bound of Proposition~\pref{thm:quantlocality}(a), with the diameter bound twice the diameter of the quadrilateral, holds for every pair, but it is loose because of its factor $e^{3\diam}$: at the first time at which a difference is $10^3$ times its error budget it exceeds the difference by factors between $4.4\times10^6$ (the pair $\vartheta=0,0.4$) and $1.0\times10^{10}$ (pairs with $\vartheta=2.8$), as computed from the traces in the code and data deposit. With the computed diameter of each member (a computed value, not a bound) the factors are $4.0\times10^3$ to $8.8\times10^4$, and with the diameter-free count of the companion paper (Remark~\pref{rem:nodiam}) $4.1\times10^5$ to $1.9\times10^6$.

\section{Cone terms in variable curvature: a derivation in outline}\label{sec:varconeoutline}

This section gives the derivation in outline of Conjecture~\pref{conj:varcone} of the paper, in the notation of Section~\pref{sec:varcurv}. It is not a proof for general germs; for rotationally symmetric germs the conjecture is Proposition~\pref{prop:varconerot} of the paper, whose proof makes the second step below unnecessary: at a fixed $\vartheta$ the $t^l$ coefficient is a finite combination of Taylor coefficients, so no uniformity in $\vartheta$ is needed. The dependence of the fixed-point coefficient on $d\Phi_p$ only through $(I-d\Phi_p)^{-1}=\frac12I+\frac12\cot\frac\vartheta2\,J$ is Donnelly's theorem as stated in \cite[4.2]{dggw2008}; two steps are not carried out in full, the identification of the radial part of the jet with the jet of $dr^2+f(r)^2d\theta^2$ up to terms at least quadratic in the non-radial modes, and the uniformity in $\vartheta$ of the inversion $s\mapsto r$ and of Laplace's method. Nothing in the paper depends on this section.

Let $\Phi$ be a local isometry fixing $p$, with $d\Phi_p$ the rotation $R_\vartheta$ by $\vartheta\in(0,2\pi)$, so that $\Phi(\exp_pv)=\exp_p(R_\vartheta v)$.

\emph{Rotation invariance.} Donnelly's form of the fixed-point term \cite[4.2]{dggw2008} gives $\mathcal B_l(\Phi)=C^{-2}P(\cot\frac\vartheta2)$, $P$ a polynomial whose coefficients are polynomials in the jet, obtained from the jet and $d\Phi_p$ by an expression that does not change when the orthonormal frame at $p$ is rotated. Rotating the frame by $\rho\in SO(2)$ replaces the jet $j$ by $\rho\cdot j$ and $d\Phi_p$ by $\rho\,d\Phi_p\,\rho^{-1}=d\Phi_p$; so the coefficients of $P$ are $SO(2)$-invariant polynomials in the jet, each of weight $2l$.

\emph{Pole order.} We show that $\mathcal B_l(\Phi)=C^{-2l-2}\bigl(X_l+O(\vartheta)\bigr)$ as $\vartheta\to0$, with $X_l$ given below, so that $\deg P\le2l$. Fix $\vartheta$ and write $h_t(x,y)\sim(4\pi t)^{-1}e^{-d(x,y)^2/4t}\sum_{k\ge0}t^ku_k(x,y)$ for the Minakshisundaram--Pleijel expansion near the diagonal, $u_0(x,x)=1$. The contribution of $\Phi$ is the expansion as $t\downarrow0$ of $\int\zeta(x)h_t(x,\Phi x)\,dA(x)$, with $\zeta$ a cut-off equal to $1$ near $p$ and supported in a small disc; $\zeta$ changes it by $O(t^\infty)$, as $d(x,\Phi x)$ is bounded below where $\zeta\ne1$. In the chart, $x=\exp_p(ru)$ with $|u|=1$, so $\Phi x=\exp_p(rR_\vartheta u)$, and in normal coordinates $\Phi x-x=r(R_\vartheta-I)u$, a vector of length $Cr$ whose components divided by $C$ are $\mp\sin\frac\vartheta2$ and $\cos\frac\vartheta2$ (after rotating $u$ to $e_1$). Hence every smooth function of $(x,\Phi x)$ that vanishes to second order on the diagonal is $C^2r^2$ times a series in $r$ whose coefficients are trigonometric polynomials in $\vartheta$; in particular, as in Schueth's expansion of the displacement \cite[Cor.~2.4, Lemma~3.4, (3.2)]{schueth2019} and her substitution \cite[(3.4), (3.5)]{schueth2019},
\[
d(x,\Phi x)^2=C^2r^2\,W_u(r),\qquad W_u(r)=1+O(r),
\]
with coefficients trigonometric polynomials in $\vartheta$, bounded uniformly, and polynomial in the jet. Put $s=d(x,\Phi x)=CrW_u(r)^{1/2}$ and invert: $r=\frac sC\,V_u(\frac sC)$, $V_u(v)=1+O(v)$, with coefficients of the same kind. The area element is $r\,J(r,u)\,dr\,du$ with $J=1+O(r^2)$, so
\begin{gather*}
r\,J\,\frac{dr}{ds}=\frac{s}{C^2}\Bigl(1+\sum_{n\ge1}e_n\bigl(\tfrac sC\bigr)^n\Bigr),\\
u_0(x,\Phi x)-1=s^2\sum_{n\ge0}g_n\bigl(\tfrac sC\bigr)^n,\qquad u_k(x,\Phi x)=\sum_{n\ge0}g_{k,n}\bigl(\tfrac sC\bigr)^n,
\end{gather*}
the second because $u_0-1$ vanishes to second order on the diagonal \cite[(2.3)]{schueth2019} and $C^2r^2=s^2/W_u$; all coefficients are trigonometric polynomials in $\vartheta$, bounded as $\vartheta\to0$, depending on $u$. Since $\int_0^\infty(4\pi t)^{-1}e^{-s^2/4t}s^{1+N}ds$ is a constant times $t^{N/2}$, the coefficient of $t^l$ collects the terms of total degree $1+2l$ in $s$: from $1$ in $u_0$ and the term $e_{2l}$, with the factor $C^{-2-2l}$; from $u_0-1$, with $n+n'=2l-2$ and the factor $C^{-2l}$; and from $t^ku_k$, $k\ge1$, with total degree $1+2(l-k)$ and the factor $C^{-2-2(l-k)}$, at most $C^{-2l}$. So $\mathcal B_l(\Phi)=C^{-2l-2}T(\vartheta)+O(C^{-2l})$ with $T$ a trigonometric polynomial, and $\deg P\le2l$. As $\vartheta\to0$, $d(x,\Phi x)/C\to|J_u(r)|$, the length of the Jacobi field along $r\mapsto\exp_p(ru)$ with $J_u(0)=0$, $|J_u'(0)|=1$, and $r\,J\,dr\,du=|J_u(r)|\,dr\,du$; with $v=|J_u(r)|$ and $r=\rho_u(v)$, the leading term is $(4\pi t)^{-1}\int_{|u|=1}\int_0^\infty e^{-C^2v^2/4t}v\,\rho_u'(v)\,dv\,du$, and $\int_0^\infty e^{-C^2v^2/4t}v^{2l+1}dv=\frac{l!}2(4t/C^2)^{l+1}$ gives $X_l=\frac{4^l\,l!}{2\pi}\int_{|u|=1}[v^{2l}]\rho_u'(v)\,du$.

\emph{Radial reduction.} The jet at a cone point is invariant under rotations of order $m$, so its non-radial Fourier modes in the angle have frequencies that are nonzero multiples of $m$; a mode of frequency $km$ occurs first in $\nabla^{km}K$, at weight $km+2\ge m+2$. In an $SO(2)$-invariant polynomial of weight $2l$, the part linear in the non-radial modes vanishes (averaging over $SO(2)$ annihilates a linear form on modes of nonzero frequency), and the part of degree at least $2$ in them has weight at least $2m+4>2l$ when $m\ge l-1$. So for $m\ge l-1$ every such polynomial takes its value on the radial part of the jet. This is the jet of the rotationally symmetric metric $dr^2+f(r)^2d\theta^2$ up to terms at least quadratic in the non-radial modes (from the passage between covariant derivatives and Taylor coefficients), which the same weight count excludes.

(i) For a rotationally symmetric germ the reflections are isometries and conjugate $\Phi$ to $\Phi^{-1}$, so $\mathcal B_l$ is even in $\vartheta$; with $\deg P\le2l$ and $\cot^2\frac\vartheta2=4C^{-2}-1$ it is $\sum_{i=1}^{l+1}\beta_{l,i}C^{-2i}$, and $\beta_{l,l+1}=X_l$. By the rotation invariance and the radial reduction $\mathcal B_l(\gamma^j)$ is this expression at $\vartheta=2\pi j/m$ for the radial part of the jet; averaging over $j$ gives (i). Differentiating $\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\cot(x+\pi j/m)=m\cot(mx)$ (Lemma~\pref{lem:Phi}) shows that $\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(\sin\frac{\pi j}m)^{-2i}$ is a polynomial in $m^2$ of degree $i$ vanishing at $m=1$, with leading coefficient $2\zeta(2i)/\pi^{2i}=4^i|B_{2i}|/(2i)!$; so $m\Pi_i(m)$ has leading coefficient $|B_{2i}|/(2i)!$. (ii) The coefficient of $m^{2l+1}$ comes from $\Pi_{l+1}$ alone, and $\beta_{l,l+1}$ is the $C^{-2l-2}$ coefficient computed above with $|J_u|=f$. At constant curvature $\kappa$, $(f^{-1})'(v)=(1-\kappa v^2)^{-1/2}$, whose $v^{2l}$ coefficient is $\binom{2l}l(\kappa/4)^l$. To first order in $\bar K=\sum_jk_jr^{2j}$, $f=r-\int_0^r(r-s)\bar K(s)s\,ds$ has the term $-k_{l-1}r^{2l+1}/(2l(2l+1))$, so $(f^{-1})'$ has $k_{l-1}v^{2l}/(2l)$, while the linear part of $(-\Delta)^{l-1}K(p)$ is $4^{l-1}((l-1)!)^2k_{l-1}$; this gives the linear part. At weight $2$ and $4$ the only monomials of degree at least $2$ are $K^2$ ($\nabla K(p)=0$ at a cone point), which the constant-curvature value fixes.

\begin{remark}[The constants $\omega_n$ and the leading terms of Branson, Gilkey and \O rsted]\label{rem:bgo}
The constants $\omega_n$ are the coefficients of the part quadratic in the curvature, with the most derivatives, of the integrated heat invariants. Branson, Gilkey and \O rsted computed the heat invariants of every order modulo cubic terms, for operators of Laplace type in every dimension, by deriving their quadratic terms from their linear ones \cite{bgo1990}; in physics the same quantities are the form factors of covariant perturbation theory, built from the moments $\int_0^1(s(1-s))^n\,ds$ \cite[\S8.2]{vassilevich2003}. The proof above re-derives the two-dimensional, conformally flat case in the form we need, and the mechanism of \cite{bgo1990} gives the same constants. With $\partial_\theta\Delta_\theta=-2\psi\Delta_\theta$ one has $\partial_\theta\cZ(t)=2t\operatorname{tr}(\psi\Delta_\theta e^{-t\Delta_\theta})=-2t\,\partial_t\operatorname{tr}(\psi e^{-t\Delta_\theta})$ (formally on $\R^2$, where these operators are not of trace class; the identity holds on a closed surface, or in the localised form of Remark~\pref{rem:param}), so $\partial_\theta\Omega_l=-\frac{2l}{4\pi}\int\psi\,u_{l+1}\,dA_\theta$; the part of $u_k$ linear in the curvature is $\frac{k\,k!}{(2k+1)!}(-\Delta)^{k-1}\mathrm{scal}$ \cite[(8.3)--(8.4)]{vassilevich2003}, with $\mathrm{scal}=2K_\theta=2\theta\Delta_0\psi+O(\theta^2)$ (in \cite{vassilevich2003} the Laplacian has the opposite sign). Hence $\partial_\theta\Omega_l=-\theta\,\frac{2l}{4\pi}\,\frac{2(l+1)\,(l+1)!}{(2l+3)!}(-1)^l\int\psi\,\Delta_0^{l+1}\psi\,dx+O(\theta^2)$, and integrating in $\theta$ gives $G_l(\theta)=\theta^2\frac{\omega_{l+1}}{2\pi}\int\psi\,\Delta_0^{l+1}\psi\,dx+O(\theta^3)$ with $\omega_{l+1}=(-1)^{l+1}\frac{l\,(l+1)\,(l+1)!}{(2l+3)!}$, which is the closed form above at $n=l+1$. Proposition~\pref{prop:varcurv}(ii) needs only $\omega_n\ne0$ for $n\ge2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Integer witnesses]\label{rem:witnesses}
A \emph{witness} for $n$ is a pair of distinct $n$-multisets of integers at least $2$ with equal $\cI_{n-1}$; it is disjoint by Theorem~\pref{thmA}, and rational witnesses scale to integer ones. For $n=4$ an exhaustive exact search of all $4$-multisets with orders at most $440$ finds $193$ witnesses, $107$ of them primitive (entries with greatest common divisor $1$), and no three multisets with a common $(R,P_1,P_3)$; the eight primitive ones with all orders at most $84$ are those of \cite[(A.685)--(A.692)]{chen2025survey}, and $101$ of the $107$ admit a pencil splitting, a construction described in the supplement (Section~\ref{sec:searches}). For $n=5$ no witness has all orders at most $120$, which is weak evidence either way. As the equalities of Problem~\pref{prob:rivals} are invariant under scaling, a solution of it multiplied by $k\ge2$ is a witness for $n=4$ one side of which contains the greatest common divisor $k$ of its eight entries, and conversely; so this search, with orders at most $440$, covers only solutions with entries at most $220$, fewer than the searches after Proposition~\pref{prop:rivals}. The supplement (Section~\ref{sec:searches}) gives the details.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The wave trace]\label{rem:wave}
The two timescales on which the computed heat traces differ (end of this section) have a simple meaning for the wave trace. Since $\hat g_t$ is even, the elliptic term of Theorem~\pref{thm:IEH} is $\mathrm E_m(t)=\int_\R g_t(u)\,k_m(u)\,du$ with
\[
  k_m(u)=\frac1{2m}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{\cosh(u/2)}{\cosh u-\cos(2\pi j/m)},
\]
by $\int_\R e^{iru}\frac{\cosh(ar)}{\cosh(\pi r)}dr=\frac{2\cos(a/2)\cosh(u/2)}{\cos a+\cosh u}$ for $|a|<\pi$, applied with $a=\pi-2\pi j/m$; this form of the elliptic heat trace, as an integral of the heat kernel along the real line against $\cosh(u/2)/(\sinh^2(u/2)+\sin^2(\pi j/m))$, is Garbin and Jorgenson's \cite[Thm~2.5, Rem.~2.6]{garbinjorgenson2020}, who integrate over the half-line, with the corresponding constant. So a cone point contributes to the even wave trace a function $k_m$ that is real-analytic on $\R$, with no singularity at any real time, and whose singularities nearest to the real axis are simple poles at $u=\pm2\pi i/m$. Its Taylor coefficients at $u=0$, integrated against the Gaussian $g_t$, give the cone coefficients $b_l(m)$ as the finite combinations (\pref{eq:bl}) (the factor $e^{-t/4}$ of $g_t$ being expanded), and the radius $2\pi/m$ of the Taylor series is the origin of the growth $l!\,(m/\pi)^{2l}$ and of the scale $(\pi/m)^2$. The closed geodesics, in contrast, contribute through $g_t(\ell(\gamma))$, that is, at the real times $u=\ell(\gamma)$, which are the singularities of the wave trace in the sense of Duistermaat and Guillemin \cite{duistermaatguillemin1975} (at constant curvature, of Selberg's formula). Cone points of order $m$ are not diffractive; on surfaces with cone points of other angles diffracted geodesics also produce real-time singularities \cite{hillairet2005,fordwunsch2017}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[How short the times must be]\label{rem:fromtrace}
The bound is proved but demanding, because the Vandermonde matrix is ill conditioned at small times and the coefficient $Q_N$ grows with $N$ like the cone coefficients (Lemma~\pref{lem:growth}). The systole bound $\ell_0$ and the diameter bound $D$ are geometric inputs, not spectral ones, so $\delta_1$ is proved relative to them. No theorem of this paper uses this remark. For $\Orb(2,8,8)$ take $\mu_0=12$. Lemma~\pref{lem:fromtrace} needs a-priori bounds valid for whichever orbifold's trace is read, here $\Orb(2,8,8)$ or its competitor $\Orb(3,3,12)$: $12$ bounds the orders of both, $A_0=\frac\pi2$ is the area of both, $D=4.897$ is twice the longest side $\operatorname{arccosh}(\cot^2\frac\pi8)=2.4485$ of the $(2,8,8)$ triangle (the distance from a vertex is a convex function on the triangle, so it is at most the longest side there; hence every point of the double is within that distance of a fixed vertex, which lies in both copies) and bounds the diameter of $\Orb(3,3,12)$ as well, by the same argument with its longest side $2.158$, and $\ell_0=1.8626$ bounds both systoles from below, the systole of $\Orb(3,3,12)$ being the smaller. These systole bounds are taken from the companion manuscript, which certifies them in interval arithmetic by a complete enumeration of closed geodesics \cite[Section~7.1]{companionB}; they are not proved here. The numbers below do not depend on them in any essential way: those for three times are the same for every $\ell_0\ge0.01$, and those for eight times are unchanged at the printed precision for every $\ell_0\ge1.1$, because the term $B_0$ is small at these times; with the bound $\ell_0=2.2567$ for $\Orb(2,8,8)$ alone they are the same. The target is $\delta_{\rm thm}(2,8,8)=3.80\times10^{-7}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:S4}, or the sharper $\delta_{\rm cert}(2,8,8)=2.341\times10^{-3}$ certified by Proposition~\ref{prop:S5} (values in Table~\ref{tab:thresholdsfull}), which the argument of Corollary~\ref{cor:aposteriori} equally admits (see the paragraph after it). With $N=n=3$ times $\tau,\frac\tau2,\frac\tau4$ and the uniform bound, $\delta_1\approx4.9\times10^3\,\tau$ for exact values ($\eta=0$); so $\delta_1\le\delta_{\rm thm}$ needs $\tau$ below about $7.8\times10^{-11}$, and at $\tau=10^{-11}$ it holds exactly when $\eta\le2.07\times10^{-19}$ (to three digits); with the row bounds $\delta_{1,j}$, $j\le3$, in place of the uniform bound, the same three times give $\delta_1\approx1.14\times10^3\,\tau$, so $\tau$ below about $3.3\times10^{-10}$, and at $\tau=10^{-11}$ it needs $\eta\le4.6\times10^{-19}$. This reflects three times, not the problem. With $N=8$ times $\tau,\frac\tau2,\dots,\frac\tau{128}$ and the row bounds $\delta_{1,j}$, $j\le3$, $\delta_{\rm thm}$ is met for $\tau$ up to about $3.6\times10^{-3}$ with exact values, and at $\frac\tau2$ of that for $\eta$ up to about $5.0\times10^{-13}$; $\delta_{\rm cert}$ is met for $\tau$ up to about $1.5\times10^{-2}$, and at $\frac\tau2$ of that for $\eta$ up to about $1.3\times10^{-8}$, the smallest time then being below $10^{-4}$. What no choice avoids in our scans is short times: among $N\le16$ times, geometric or of Chebyshev type, in an interval $[a,T]$, none meets $\delta_{\rm cert}$, even for exact values, when $a\ge10^{-3}$, and none meets $\delta_{\rm thm}$ when $a\ge3\times10^{-4}$, while both are met for some smaller $a$. The computed spectra of the supplement fix the heat trace only for $t\ge1.5\times10^{-3}$, to about $3\times10^{-11}$ and with error estimates rather than bounds, because shorter times need eigenvalues beyond the computed range; so Corollary~\ref{cor:aposteriori} does not apply to them, and what limits a proved reading of $c_3$ is the short times that a finite list of eigenvalues cannot resolve. (The script of Section~\ref{sec:methods} of the supplement checks these numbers.)
\end{remark}

\section{Computational methods}\label{sec:methods}

Results are exact (integer or rational arithmetic) unless stated, and each is produced by a program of the code and data deposit named in the data statement of the paper, whose manifest gives every file's place and checksum.

\emph{Statements that rest on computer search alone.} These are exhaustive searches within the stated bounds, used in no proof: the counts of witnesses for $n=4$ with orders at most $440$, and the absence of witnesses for $n=5$ with orders at most $120$ (Remark~\ref{rem:witnesses}); the exclusion of genus pairs of size $8$ whose five-element side has entries at most $220$ (Remark~\ref{rem:T3}); the $38$ collision-free sums among $18\le S\le4800$ (Section~\pref{sec:rigid} and Computation~\ref{prop:collisionfree}); the exact values of $\Kmult$ on the $525$ complete area classes of Figure~\pref{fig:F4}; the minimality of the areas $12\pi$ and $36\pi$ of the pairs of Theorem~\pref{thm:bounded}(ii) for $M=3,4$, by enumeration of all signatures with orders at most $M$ and area at most that of the pair; the absence, with entries at most $200$, of $4$-configurations of size $10$ built from two five-element sets with $e_1=e_3=0$ (Section~\pref{sec:pte}); and the absence of solutions of the system of Proposition~\pref{prop:rivals} with $\{a,b,c,d\}=\{2,2,c,d\}$, $d\le1000$, or with $b\ge3$ and $d\le20000$, each candidate $\{a,b,c,d\}$ determining the elementary symmetric functions of $\{p,q,r\}$ exactly. The bounds on $T(L)$ rest on explicit pairs, printed in Section~\ref{sec:searches}.

\emph{The computations.} (i)~Remark~\ref{rem:witnesses}, $n=4$: all pairs of $4$-multisets of orders in $[2,440]$ with equal $(R,P_1,P_3)$, found by sorting every multiset on these exact keys; $n=5$: all $216{,}071{,}394$ multisets of orders in $[2,120]$, grouped by $P_1$ and keyed on the exact integers $(P_3,P_5,\operatorname{lcm}(2,\dots,120)\,R)$. (ii)~Remark~\ref{rem:T3}: for each five-multiset $V$ of positive integers with gcd $1$ and entries at most $220$ (all $4{,}325{,}115{,}770$ of them, a count checked against its M\"obius formula), the other side is the root set of the cubic $q_V$ of that remark. For each of the primes $p=223,227,\dots,277$ the program reduces $e_1$, $R(V)$ and $P_3(V)$ modulo $p$ in integer arithmetic (every prime used exceeds every entry of $V$, so it divides no denominator of $R(V)$, and $e_1$, $P_3(V)$ are integers), skips $p$ if $3(1-e_1R(V))\equiv0$, and otherwise computes the reduction of $q_V$ and looks it up in a table of all monic cubics over $\mathbb F_p$ that split into linear factors; $V$ is rejected as soon as one used prime gives a cubic that does not split. The rejection is sound: if $q_V$ had three rational roots, then, as $p>\max V$ and $p\nmid3(1-e_1R(V))$, $q_V$ is monic with $p$-integral coefficients, so by Gauss's lemma its roots are $p$-integral and its reduction modulo $p$ splits into linear factors (Remark~\ref{rem:T3}); and as a control every $3$-multiset with entries at most $220$, whose own cubic splits, is accepted by the same code. Fifteen $V$ are accepted for all twelve primes; each of their cubics is factored exactly over $\Q$ and is irreducible. The search takes about three minutes on four cores. (iii)~Figures~\pref{fig:F4} and~\pref{fig:F7}, Computation~\ref{prop:collisionfree}, the rank of $C_{27/2}$ (PARI/GP's \texttt{ellrank}) and the comparison with U\c{c}ar's coefficients for $l\le40$: exact, the enumerations recomputed independently. The repository's single run-all script reruns all of these except the $n=4$ search of (i) and the search of (ii), whose committed outputs it rechecks (for (ii), every survivor is re-decided exactly); the finite-element spectra of Sections~\ref{sec:listening}--\ref{sec:moduli} are recomputed only on request (about an hour on a laptop), and otherwise their committed values are validated. The software is Python with NumPy, SciPy~1.18.1 (whose \texttt{eigsh} calls ARPACK), NGSolve and NETGEN~6.2.2607 and PARI/GP~2.17.2; every version is pinned in the deposit.

\emph{Variable curvature.} Remark~\ref{rem:varcomp} and the machine checks of Conjecture~\pref{conj:varcone} and Proposition~\pref{prop:varcone} are exact computer algebra in rational arithmetic, every step asserted. (a) The top coefficient $\beta_{l,l+1}=4^l\,l!\,[v^{2l}](f^{-1})'(v)$ for $l\le7$: $f$ is expanded from $f''=-\bar Kf$ with a general radial curvature $\bar K=\sum_jk_jr^{2j}$, inverted as a power series, and the coefficients of $k_0^l$ and of the linear part are checked against Conjecture~\pref{conj:varcone}(ii). (b) The $t^3$ coefficient: for $K=k_0+k_1r^2+k_2r^4$ the Hamilton--Jacobi equation for half the squared distance and the transport equations for the Minakshisundaram--Pleijel coefficients are solved exactly as power series in normal coordinates (residuals asserted zero), the fixed-point integral is expanded term by term by Laplace's method, and the result is averaged over the rotations of order $m$; the program reproduces the local coefficients $u_1=K/3$, $u_2=(K^2-\Delta K)/15$, Donnelly's $\mathcal B_0,\mathcal B_1$, Schueth's $\mathcal B_2$ and $b_0,b_1,b_2$ \cite{schueth2019}, and U\c{c}ar's $p_3(m)/m$ at constant curvature. (c) The linear part: the first-order Duhamel integral of the proof of Proposition~\pref{prop:varcone} is evaluated exactly for every monomial $\psi=x^ay^b$ with $a+b\le14$ and $l\le7$ ($833$ cases), radial and non-radial. (d) Proposition~\pref{prop:varconerot}: \texttt{theory/varcurv/rotsym\_check.py} asserts the algebra of its proof (an even $P$ gives a polynomial in $C^{-2}$ without constant term), the limit $W_u\to f^2/r^2$ numerically by geodesic shooting for a metric of non-constant curvature, the Gaussian moments, the leading coefficients of the cosecant power sums to $10^{-40}$, and the constant-curvature value and the linear part of $\beta_{l,l+1}$ for $l\le8$. (e) The second-variation constants $\omega_n$ of the proof of Proposition~\pref{prop:varcurv}(ii), and the examples of Proposition~\pref{prop:varcurv}(ii)--(iii).
