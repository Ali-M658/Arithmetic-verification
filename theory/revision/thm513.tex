\begin{theorem}[The threshold]\label{thm:threshold}
If $p+q+r\le17$, the first two heat invariants determine the hyperbolic triangle orbifold
$\Orb(p,q,r)$ among all hyperbolic triangle orbifolds: $\Kiso(\Orb(p,q,r);\Pill_3)\le2$. The bound
$17$ is sharp: the first failure occurs at cone-order sum $18$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A collision has equal sums and equal $R$, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:separation} none has sum at most
$17$; a triad of sum at most $17$ cannot share $\keytwo$ with one of a different sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Collision-free sums; computational]\label{prop:collisionfree}
Among the sums $18\le S\le4800$, exactly the $38$ sums
\[
\begin{gathered}
  19,\,21\text{--}25,\,27\text{--}30,\,33,\,41,\,44,\,46\text{--}51,\,59,\,65,\,67,\,81,\,99,\\
  115,\,119,\,123,\,125,\,173,\,199,\,203,\,223,\,235,\,243,\,251,\,307,\,329,\,557
\end{gathered}
\]
carry no collision, that is, no two hyperbolic triads of sum $S$ have the same reciprocal sum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Method]
The statement is a finite computation, in two parts, both in exact rational arithmetic.
(a) For each of the $38$ listed sums, all hyperbolic triads of sum $S$ are enumerated
($p\le q\le r$, $p+q+r=S$, $qr+rp+pq<pqr$) and their reciprocal sums are compared; they are pairwise
distinct. The largest case, $S=557$, has $25\,575$ triads.
(b) For each of the other $4745$ sums a collision is exhibited: two distinct hyperbolic triads of
sum $S$ with the same $R$. If $d\mid S$ and $d$ carries a collision, so does $S$, by scaling both
triads by $S/d$; this covers $3962$ sums, and the remaining $783$ have explicit pairs, the first
being $(2,8,8),(3,3,12)$ at $18$, $(4,8,8),(5,5,10)$ at $20$ and $(4,10,12),(5,6,15)$ at $26$. The
pairs are listed in the supplementary data, and each was checked directly.
Part (a) is independent of the enumeration of Section~\ref{sec:degeneracies}, which reports the
same list.
\end{proof}
