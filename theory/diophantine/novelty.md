## 1. The curve reformulation (Proposition 1)

*Claim:* a degeneracy class (equal $S$, equal $R$) is a set of positive
rational points on one cubic $C_\lambda:(x+y+z)(xy+yz+zx)=\lambda xyz$, with
$\lambda=SR$. Conversely, any such points rescaled to the lcm of their sums
form a class.

**Already in the sources:**

- [BGN] study exactly this cubic, eq. (2) on p. 117, for integer $\lambda=n$.
  They ask which integers $n$ are represented.
- [BGN] p. 117, §1 note scale invariance: "So is $(kx,ky,kz)$ for any $k$."
- [BGN] p. 118, §2 give the Weierstrass model (6), the maps (7), and the
  positivity criterion (the egg, $\sigma<0$).
- [Bea] p. 658, Théorème and Tableau, row $\Gamma^0_0(6)$: the pencil
  $(X+Y)(Y+Z)(Z+X)+tXYZ=0$ with fibre components 6, 3, 2, 1. Ours is the
  member $t=1-\lambda$.

**New:**

- The observation that equal $(S,R)$ means a common $\lambda=S\cdot R$, so
  degeneracies are pairs of points on one member of this pencil. Neither
  source considers two solutions with a common sum, or non-integral
  $\lambda$, which our problem needs: $\lambda=27/2$ for the base pair.
- The explicit $\mathbf Q$-birational map to [BGN]'s model with its
  inverse (`variety_checks.py` §3c). [BGN]'s printed map (7)–(8) is not
  re-used.

**Verdict:** an elementary but new link. It must cite [BGN] and [Bea] as
the source of the curve.

## 2. The dual family

*Claim:* for every non-geometric $t=(a,b,c)$,
$\{e_2(t)\,t,\ e_1(t)\,(bc,ca,ab)\}$ is a degeneracy with $S=e_1e_2$ and
$R=1/e_3$. Reciprocation is translation by the 2-torsion point.

**Already in the sources:**

- [BGN] p. 117, §1: "If $(x,y,z)$ is a solution, so is $(1/x,1/y,1/z)$
  … solutions occur in reciprocal pairs."
- [BGN] p. 120, §4, table: the reciprocal of a solution is
  "$(\sigma_0,\tau_0)+(0,0)$", i.e. translation by the 2-torsion point in
  their model.

**New:**

- The reading of reciprocal pairs as equal-$(S,R)$ degeneracies after
  rescaling, with the closed forms $S=e_1e_2$ and $R=1/e_3$.
- The symbolic proof in the plane model, with base point $(1{:}{-1}{:}0)$
  and $T_2=(0{:}0{:}1)$, that $P+T_2=\iota(P)$ exactly.
- The isosceles subfamily, with its count $c_{\rm iso}=3\log2/(2\pi^2)$.
- The conic-bundle parametrisation and the del Pezzo description of the
  dual locus.

**Verdict:** the involution is [BGN]'s and must be credited. Its use as a
degeneracy generator, and everything quantitative, is new.

## 3. The isolation theorem

*Claim:* rank $C_{27/2}(\mathbf Q)=0$ and $C_{27/2}(\mathbf Q)$ is the
12-point torsion group. So the class of $\{(2k,8k,8k),(3k,3k,12k)\}$ has
exactly two members for every $k$.

**Already in the sources:**

- [BGN] p. 120, §4 prove the torsion is $\mathbf Z/6$ for integer
  $n\ne10$, and show that $n=10$ has $\mathbf Z/2\times\mathbf Z/6$ and
  rank 0. That is the analogous statement for the integer curve through
  $(1,2,2)$.
- [Sch] and [Bea] contain nothing on it.

**New:**

- The curve $\lambda=27/2$ is not integral, so it is outside [BGN].
- Its rank-0 certificate (PARI `ellrank` upper bound 0, unconditional) and
  the explicit torsion list.
- The consequence for the spectral problem.

**Verdict:** new, a computation-backed theorem. The appendix should quote
the PARI call (`ranks.py`).

## 4. The $S\log S$ lower bound (Theorem 4) and $S(\log S)^2$ (Theorem 5)

**Already in the sources:**

- [BGN] contain no counting function. Their §8 tabulates representable
  integers $n$ with $|n|<1000$.
- [Sch] proves existence only.
- [Bea] is not arithmetic.

**New:** entirely new, including the explicit constant and the conic-bundle
argument.

## 5. The large-fibre theorem (Theorem 3)

*Claim:* for every $k$ there are infinitely many primitive classes of $k$
hyperbolic triples with a common $S$ and a common $R$.

**Already in the sources:**

- [Sch] p. 587 (Lemma and proof) and p. 588 (proof of the theorem) supply
  the method, for equal sum and product: a point failing Nagell–Lutz, so of
  infinite order; Poincaré–Hurwitz density in a region giving positive
  triples; then clearing a common denominator of $k$ rational solutions.
- [BGN] p. 119: "If $P$ is on the egg, then just the odd multiples of $P$
  give positive solutions."

**New:**

- The statement for equal $(S,R)$.
- The normalisation step: a common sum is reached by rescaling to the lcm
  of the sums, because $\lambda$ is scale-invariant. This replaces [Sch]'s
  fixed sum = product = 6.
- The explicit generator: $(4,9,18)$ on $C_{155/12}$, of infinite order by
  Mazur and of rank exactly 2 by PARI.

**Verdict:** the method is Schinzel's, and the positivity statement is
[BGN]'s. The paper should present Theorem 3 as an adaptation of [Sch], not
as an independent idea. What is new is the statement for this problem and
the instance.
