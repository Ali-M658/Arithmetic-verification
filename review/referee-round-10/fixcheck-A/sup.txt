                                    Abstract
This supplement contains the material behind five sections of the paper and its
computational methods (Section S8): the computer searches, the explicit pairs
and the complete area classes of Section 3 (Section S1); the triads of small sum of
Section 5 (Section S2); the threshold table for the stability theorem of Section 6,
whose constants, proofs and exact-arithmetic certificate are in Appendix C of the
paper (Section S3); a derivation in outline of Conjecture 2.13 (Section S7); and
the numerical experiments of Section 4: the computed spectra of O(2, 8, 8) and
O(3, 3, 12), a blind recovery of their cone orders, and a family of orbifolds of
signature (0; 3, 3, 3, 3) that share every heat invariant while their spectra move
(Sections S4–S6). The hand descent for the rank of C27/2 in Theorem 5.11 is
Appendix F of the paper, and the proof of Proposition 2.8 is its Appendix B.
The eigenvalue errors of Sections S4–S6 are a-posteriori estimates, not enclosures.
Numbers such as “Proposition 4.2” refer to the paper.

S1 Searches and explicit pairs
Pencil splittings (Remark 3.5). A pencil splitting is a pair (A, B ) of 4-multisets of
nonzero integers with e1 (A) = e1 (B ) = 0, e3 (A) = e3 (B ), e4 (A) = e4 (B ), such that
Z = A ⊎ (−B ) has four positive and four negative entries and no pair {z, −z}; its
witness is the pair formed by Z>0 and −Z<0 , two disjoint multisets of orders with
equal R, P1 and P3 . The eight orders of the witness are the absolute values of the
entries of A and B , so a bound on the entries is a bound on the orders. Counting
distinct witnesses (unordered, and primitive when the eight orders have gcd 1), pencil
splittings with entries of absolute value at most 130 give 17 witnesses, 14 of them
primitive, and at most 220 give 49, 30 primitive (every witness is rechecked exactly,
with P5 different; Section S8). An exhaustive search of all pairs of 4-multisets of orders
in [2, 440] with equal (R, P1 , P3 ) finds 193 pairs, 107 of them primitive; among the
primitive ones with largest order at most 130, 220, 440 there are 16, 33, 107, of which
14, 30, 101 have a pencil splitting. The six without one are

    The first open case (Remark 3.22). A genus pair of size 8 sharing three heat invari-
ants has shape (3, 5). For each 5-multiset V of positive integers with gcd 1 and entries
at most 220, the 3-element side is the root set of the cubic z 3 − e1 z 2 + Re3 z − e3 with
e1 = P1 (V ), R = R(V ) and e3 = (P3 (V ) − e31 )/(3 − 3e1 R); it must have three positive
rational roots. The search rejects V by the modular test of Remark 3.22, described in
Section S8(ii): of the 4,325,115,770 such V , fifteen pass it, and none of their cubics has
a rational root. Real solutions of the shape exist: {1, 1, 1, 1, 7} has exactly one partner
triple, {0.2664, 4.2830, 6.4506}.

S3 Stability: thresholds and certificates
The constants of Theorem 6.2 of the paper, with their proofs, and the explicit thresh-
old δthm are in Appendix C of the paper (Lemma C.1 and Theorems C.2–C.4), whose
notation we use. The sharper certificate, evaluated in rational arithmetic, is Propo-
sition C.5 in Appendix C of the paper, where it is stated and proved; Table S4 lists
its thresholds δcert . For n ≤ 5 the series that it uses have at most four nonzero coef-
ficients; for (2, 2, 2, 2, 3), sech2 U = 1 − 121z 2 + 9328z 4 − 601472z 6 . So every entry of
Table S4 can be re-derived in exact arithmetic.

S5 Blind recovery
The recovery chain of Section 6 was run on the computed spectra of Section S4 under a
protocol committed to the repository before the pipeline and before any result (dated
1 October 2026; the protocol file and its version history are part of the deposit named
in the data statement of the paper). What was assumed: each specimen is the double

two candidates whose third coefficients differ by about 104 error bars, so it exercises

The family. The signature (0; 3, 3, 3, 3) is hyperbolic, with area 4π/3 and a 2-

S8 Computational methods
Results are exact (integer or rational arithmetic) unless stated, and each is produced
by a program of the code and data deposit named in the data statement of the paper,
whose manifest gives every file’s place and checksum.
     Statements that rest on computer search alone. These are exhaustive searches
within the stated bounds, used in no proof: the counts of witnesses for n = 4 with
orders at most 440, and the absence of witnesses for n = 5 with orders at most 120
(Remark 3.5); the exclusion of genus pairs of size 8 whose five-element side has entries
at most 220 (Remark 3.22); the 38 collision-free sums among 18 ≤ S ≤ 4800 (Section 5
and Computation S2.1); the exact values of Kmult on the 525 complete area classes
of Figure 3; the minimality of the areas 12π and 36π of the pairs of Theorem 3.11(ii)
for M = 3, 4, by enumeration of all signatures with orders at most M and area at
most that of the pair; the absence, with entries at most 200, of 4-configurations of size
10 built from two five-element sets with e1 = e3 = 0 (Section 3.4); and the absence
of solutions of the system of Proposition 5.2 with {a, b, c, d} = {2, 2, c, d}, d ≤ 1000,
or with b ≥ 3 and d ≤ 20000, each candidate {a, b, c, d} determining the elementary
symmetric functions of {p, q, r} exactly. The bounds on T (L) rest on explicit pairs,
printed in Section S1.
     The computations. (i) Remark 3.5, n = 4: all pairs of 4-multisets of orders in
[2, 440] with equal (R, P1 , P3 ), found by sorting every multiset on these exact keys;
n = 5: all 216,071,394 multisets of orders in [2, 120], grouped by P1 and keyed on the
exact integers (P3 , P5 , lcm(2, . . . , 120) R). (ii) Remark 3.22: for each five-multiset V
of positive integers with gcd 1 and entries at most 220 (all 4,325,115,770 of them, a
count checked against its Möbius formula), the other side is the root set of the cubic
qV of that remark. For each of the primes p = 223, 227, . . . , 277 the program reduces
e1 , R(V ) and P3 (V ) modulo p in integer arithmetic (every prime used exceeds every
entry of V , so it divides no denominator of R(V ), and e1 , P3 (V ) are integers), skips p
if 3(1 − e1 R(V )) ≡ 0, and otherwise computes the reduction of qV and looks it up in a
table of all monic cubics over Fp that split into linear factors; V is rejected as soon as
one used prime gives a cubic that does not split. The rejection is sound: if qV had three
rational roots, then, as p > max V and p ∤ 3(1 − e1 R(V )), qV is monic with p-integral
coefficients, so by Gauss’s lemma its roots are p-integral and its reduction modulo p
splits into linear factors (Remark 3.22); and as a control every 3-multiset with entries
at most 220, whose own cubic splits, is accepted by the same code. Fifteen V are
