[S1]                                     Abstract
[S1] This supplement contains the material behind five sections of the paper and its
[S1] computational methods (Section S8): the computer searches, the explicit pairs
[S1] and the complete area classes of Section 3 (Section S1); the triads of small sum of
[S1] Section 5 (Section S2); the threshold table for the stability theorem of Section 6,
[S1] whose constants, proofs and exact-arithmetic certificate are in Appendix C of the
[S1] paper (Section S3); a derivation in outline of Conjecture 2.13 (Section S7); and
[S1] the numerical experiments of Section 4: the computed spectra of O(2, 8, 8) and
[S1] O(3, 3, 12), a blind recovery of their cone orders, and a family of orbifolds of
[S1] signature (0; 3, 3, 3, 3) that share every heat invariant while their spectra move
[S1] (Sections S4–S6). The hand descent for the rank of C27/2 in Theorem 5.11 is
[S1] Appendix F of the paper, and the proof of Proposition 2.8 is its Appendix B.
[S1] The eigenvalue errors of Sections S4–S6 are a-posteriori estimates, not enclosures.
[S1] Numbers such as “Proposition 4.2” refer to the paper.

[S2] S1 Searches and explicit pairs
[S2] Pencil splittings (Remark 3.5). A pencil splitting is a pair (A, B ) of 4-multisets of
[S2] nonzero integers with e1 (A) = e1 (B ) = 0, e3 (A) = e3 (B ), e4 (A) = e4 (B ), such that
[S2] Z = A ⊎ (−B ) has four positive and four negative entries and no pair {z, −z}; its
[S2] witness is the pair formed by Z>0 and −Z<0 , two disjoint multisets of orders with
[S2] equal R, P1 and P3 . The eight orders of the witness are the absolute values of the
[S2] entries of A and B , so a bound on the entries is a bound on the orders. Counting
[S2] distinct witnesses (unordered, and primitive when the eight orders have gcd 1), pencil
[S2] splittings with entries of absolute value at most 130 give 17 witnesses, 14 of them
[S2] primitive, and at most 220 give 49, 30 primitive (every witness is rechecked exactly,
[S2] with P5 different; Section S8). An exhaustive search of all pairs of 4-multisets of orders
[S2] in [2, 440] with equal (R, P1 , P3 ) finds 193 pairs, 107 of them primitive; among the
[S2] primitive ones with largest order at most 130, 220, 440 there are 16, 33, 107, of which
[S2] 14, 30, 101 have a pencil splitting. The six without one are
[S2]              (16, 16, 74, 74; 11, 37, 44, 88),       (11, 21, 99, 99; 9, 51, 51, 119),
[S2]          (17, 17, 88, 136; 11, 52, 52, 143),     (77, 88, 182, 208; 68, 112, 154, 221),
[S2]       (154, 175, 308, 350; 143, 200, 280, 364),        (22, 50, 209, 418; 19, 85, 170, 425),
[S2] each written as the two orbifolds’ orders separated by a semicolon. Chen’s survey lists
[S2] the witness of Theorem 3.4(3) as an ideal solution of type (−1, 1, 3) [1, A.685]; for
[S2] the type (−1, 1, 3, 5) that n = 5 requires it gives the identity [1, (3.33)] but lists no
[S2] numerical solution. For n = 5, all 216,071,394 multisets of orders in [2, 120], grouped
[S2] by P1 and keyed on the exact integers (P3 , P5 , lcm(2, . . . , 120)R), give no witness.
[S2]     The first open case (Remark 3.22). A genus pair of size 8 sharing three heat invari-
[S2] ants has shape (3, 5). For each 5-multiset V of positive integers with gcd 1 and entries
[S2] at most 220, the 3-element side is the root set of the cubic z 3 − e1 z 2 + Re3 z − e3 with
[S2] e1 = P1 (V ), R = R(V ) and e3 = (P3 (V ) − e31 )/(3 − 3e1 R); it must have three positive
[S2] rational roots. The search rejects V by the modular test of Remark 3.22, described in
[S2] Section S8(ii): of the 4,325,115,770 such V , fifteen pass it, and none of their cubics has
[S2] a rational root. Real solutions of the shape exist: {1, 1, 1, 1, 7} has exactly one partner
[S2] triple, {0.2664, 4.2830, 6.4506}.

[S13] The family. The signature (0; 3, 3, 3, 3) is hyperbolic, with area 4π/3 and a 2-

[S13] from the centre to two adjacent sides, sinh a sinh b = cos π3 , leaving one parameter
[S13] ϑ = log(sinh a/ sinh b). Eight members, ϑ ∈ {0, 0.4, . . . , 2.8}, were computed, about
[S13] 5800 eigenvalues each, of which 5451 to 5572 lie below the least of the sector maxima,

[S13] maximum, where the eigenvalue counts of its three most accurate discretisations agree

[S13] from 2.634 to 0.694, so the members are pairwise non-isometric, a numerical state-
[S13] ment, and every pair has different systoles. The worst eigenvalue error is 1.3 × 10−10

[S13] the orbifolds with an orientation-reversing isometry fixing the four cone points: such
