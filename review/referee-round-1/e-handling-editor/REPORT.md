# Handling-editor report: "How much of a hyperbolic orbifold does heat hear?" (submission to J. Geom. Anal.)

Materials read: manuscript.pdf (35 pp.), supplement.pdf, note.pdf (companion, "Triples with equal sum and equal reciprocal sum"). Nothing else in the repository was opened. Placeholders (author contributions, AI statement, Zenodo DOI) are treated as known and not counted.

**Headline: desk-reject probability about 25%. If sent out: major revision (confidence about 60%; reject-and-resubmit-in-pieces is the realistic alternative, about 30%).**

MAJOR issues (details in section 4):
- M1. Theorem 1.3 / Section 6: the stability theory is incomplete and partly non-geometric (sharpness only for non-realisable data; realisable exponent settled only for a double order and for all orders equal; constants depend on the unknown orders; "orders" are real, not orbifold orders; no link to spectral error). Claims should be cut back or the realisable case settled.
- M2. Section 4 and the "What heat does not hear" claims are presented as results but are essentially classical (Selberg/Huber/McKean, Dryden, Uçar); Remark 4.7's "all but countably many points" claim is stated without proof. Novelty claims must be narrowed and the unproved assertion proved or removed.
- M3. The headline question "how fast must the number grow" is not answered: Theorem 1.1(ii) leaves sqrt(A) <= f(A) <= A/pi + 4, and the "iff" with the Prouhet-Tarry-Escott function is a reformulation of an open problem. This is acceptable only if the abstract and introduction say plainly that the growth rate is open; at present the framing overstates it.

## 1. Summary

For a closed orientable hyperbolic 2-orbifold O with signature (g; m_1,...,m_n), the heat trace has an expansion Z_O(t) ~ sum c_j t^{j-2}. The authors (re)derive, from the Selberg trace formula with elliptic terms, closed forms for every c_j (Prop. 2.8, Lemma 2.7, Lemma 2.9): c_1 = Area/4pi, and c_{j+2} = alpha_{j+1} Area/4pi + sum_i b_j(m_i), where b_j(m) is an explicit even polynomial in m of degree 2j+2 divided by m. The key structural observation is that, modulo the area, the (j)-th invariant brings in exactly one new odd power sum P_{2j-1} = sum m_i^{2j-1}, with the reciprocal sum R entering through the area. Comparing two orbifolds therefore becomes a moment problem for the signed multiset m ⊎ (-m'), i.e. an odd-power Prouhet-Tarry-Escott (PTE) system with a vanishing reciprocal sum.

Main results:
1. (Thm 1.1/3.x) The first floor(Area/pi)+4 heat invariants determine genus and cone-order multiset among all closed hyperbolic orientable 2-orbifolds (via a "mirror" argument: a signed multiset with too few elements for its vanishing odd moments must be symmetric under x -> -x; plus the elementary bound n+4g <= Area/pi + 4). No area-independent number works (PTE solutions + a "doubling" construction). With f(A) the worst case over area <= A, sqrt((A/2pi-1)/3)+2 <= f(A) <= A/pi+4, and f(A) >= cA^alpha iff N(k) <= C k^{1/alpha}; so linear growth iff N(k)=O(k) (open). For spheres with n cone points n invariants suffice, n-1 fail near n distinct real orders, and fail on integers for n=3,4 (witnesses {2,8,8}/{3,3,12}; {3,10,15,30}/{4,5,21,28}).
2. (Thm 1.2/Sect. 5) For the rigid triangle orbifolds O(p,q,r): c_1,c_2 <-> (R, S_1=p+q+r). Two invariants suffice for p+q+r <= 17; the first failure is O(2,8,8)/O(3,3,12) at sum 18, unique at that sum; three invariants always suffice. The proof of the threshold is a monotonicity analysis of "strata" (least order p fixed, sum S fixed); isolation of the minimal pair (and its scalings) follows from an explicit elliptic curve E: y^2=x(x+9)(x+384) with E(Q) = Z/2 x Z/6 (2-isogeny descent).
3. (Sect. 4) Since every c_j depends only on the signature, no finite number of heat invariants determines an orbifold with moduli (6g-6+2n>0). The difference of two heat traces of the same signature is bounded by C(A, systole, diameter) t^{-1/2} exp(-l^2/4t) for explicit C and explicit t-range, and this is sharp (leading term from the first differing weighted length).
4. (Sect. 6) Stability: for spheres with a known number n of cone points, an explicit recovery map (triangular front end, the Hurwitz-type linear system of Thm B, polynomial roots) is Lipschitz at simple orders, Holder of exponent 1/k at a k-fold order; 1/k is sharp for arbitrary data; for heat invariants of positive real orders the sharp exponent is 1/2 at a double order and when all orders coincide. Integer orders are recovered by rounding below an explicit threshold (Table 1), with certificates in rational arithmetic.
5. (Sect. 7, supplement) FEM computation of about 2850 eigenvalues of the minimal pair and of a family in (0;3,3,3,3), illustrating the expansion/locality statements and a "blind recovery".

## 2. Significance, novelty, scope

**Literature checked (verified to exist/metadata via arXiv/Crossref):** Dryden-Strohmaier, "Huber's theorem for hyperbolic orbisurfaces" (Canad. Math. Bull. 52 (2009)), which proves that the Laplace spectrum determines genus and cone orders; Dryden-Gordon-Greenwald-Webb, "Asymptotic expansion of the heat kernel for orbifolds" (arXiv 2008, Michigan Math. J. 56); Doyle-Rossetti arXiv:1103.4372; Schueth, "Heat coefficients of surfaces with curved conical singularities" (Ann. Global Anal. Geom. 2025/26, DOI 10.1007/s10455-025-10024-1); Chen's PTE survey arXiv:2506.11429; Croot-Mao-Yip arXiv:2609.05061 (Sept. 2026). The arXiv search for "heat invariants" with "orbifold" and with "Prouhet" found no prior paper that counts heat invariants needed on hyperbolic orbifolds, consistent with the authors' "to our knowledge" claim. I could not check Uçar's thesis text (not fetched), so the statement that his cone formulas agree with the paper's is taken from the authors (but see section 3: I verified the formulas independently).

**What is new (credibly):** (a) the explicit, adaptive count floor(Area/pi)+4; (b) the PTE reformulation of the growth question, with the genuine lower bound sqrt(A); (c) the exact threshold 17 for triangle orbifolds and the isolation of the minimal pair; (d) the explicit constants in the locality estimate; (e) the quantitative recovery map. Items (a)-(c) are clean and of a type I have not seen before.

**What is not new / thin:** the expansion itself (Donnelly, DGGW, Uçar, Schueth) and signature-locality of the heat invariants (Selberg, McKean, Huber, Dryden) are classical; Theorem A is a re-proof of a Newton-identity/Steinig-type uniqueness with a small twist (replace the top odd power sum by R); finiteness for fixed area is trivial (finitely many signatures of bounded area, each pair separated by some invariant because the full sequence determines the signature), so the content of Thm 1.1(i) is the explicit linear bound. The growth question, the one the title poses, is left between sqrt(A) and A (see M3).

**Scope fit for JGA:** spectral geometry of orbifolds is within the journal's remit, and the Selberg trace formula/heat-kernel input is analytic. But by page count the paper is mostly algebra and arithmetic (power sums, Hurwitz determinants, PTE, elliptic-curve descent, Diophantine enumeration) plus numerical linear algebra and finite-element experiments. The geometric-analytic content proper (Sect. 2, Sect. 4) is, as above, largely classical. A reader of JGA would find sections 3 and 5 interesting but would not find a new geometric-analytic technique. Scope is borderline-acceptable, not a clear fit.

## 4. MAJOR issues

**M1. Stability theorem (Thm 1.3, Section 6): what is proved is weaker than what is advertised.**
Location: abstract, Thm 1.3, Thm 6.5, Prop. 6.6, Remark 6.7, Remark 6.1.
Problem: (i) The Holder exponent 1/k is shown sharp only "for arbitrary data" (Prop. 6.6(ii)), and the data used for k >= 3 are not heat invariants of any real multiset (the authors say so). For the geometrically meaningful (realisable, integer-order) data the true exponent at a k-fold order with 2 < k < n, or at mixed clusters, is left "not settled". The realisable sharp cases are exactly two (double order; all n equal). So the claim that the exponent 1/k is the correct stability exponent for orbifolds is not established. (ii) The constants C_a, the radii r_a and the thresholds delta are evaluated at the unknown true orders; Remark 6.1 concedes the logic is a-posteriori. As a stability theorem for recovery this is a conditional statement. (iii) "Heat invariants of positive real orders" are used throughout, but a real order is not an orbifold; the identification of the formal polynomial extension with the heat coefficient of a hyperbolic cone surface of cone angle 2pi/m (needed for the claim to be about geometry) is not stated or cited precisely. (iv) The error model is on c_1..c_n, whereas nothing connects it to errors in eigenvalues (the authors say the bounds "concern errors in heat invariants, not eigenvalues"); the thresholds delta_thm in Table 1 are 1e-7 to 1e-12, and the practical certificate delta_cert 1e-3 to 1e-5, with the extreme conditioning of n=5 (cond growth) suggesting little robustness. (v) Genus 0 and known n are assumed, whereas Theorem 1.1 is about all genera.
Why blocking: the abstract's "stability estimates for recovering the cone orders" and Lipschitz/Holder-sharp language create an expectation that is only met in part, and the Hadamard-based bound (Thm 6.4(a)) is admitted to be lossy.
Resolve: either (a) settle the realisable exponent (is it always 1/2 at any cluster of real orders?), or (b) restate Thm 1.3 and the abstract as an upper-bound result with sharpness only for the listed cases and for non-realisable data, state the a-posteriori nature in the theorem, cite/prove the real-order identification, and move the n>=4 certificate machinery to the supplement.

**M2. Novelty of the "what heat cannot hear" part is overstated, and one assertion is unproved.**
Location: Section 1.1 paragraph "What heat does not hear", Section 4, Remark 4.7.
Problem: Prop. 4.1, Prop. 4.2, Cor. 4.3, Theorem 4.4 (a) are classical in substance (Selberg, McKean, Huber, Dryden's proof of Thm 4.5, Uçar), as the authors themselves say. The new parts are the explicit constants in 4.4(b) and the sharpness statement 4.5. The text nevertheless lists these under main results and in the abstract ("we bound this term explicitly") as a feature. Moreover Remark 4.7 asserts "as a weak orbifold substitute for Wolpert's theorem, along a Thurston length coordinate all but countably many points have heat traces different from a given one" with no proof or reference, and in a paper whose stated virtue is rigour this should not stand. Also Cor. 4.3 (uncountably many isometry classes) should cite the real-analyticity of nothing, but fine.
Why blocking: an unproved assertion in the final text, and a mismatch between claimed and actual novelty.
Resolve: prove the Remark (or delete it), and recast Section 4 explicitly as "quantitative version of a classical fact", with the explicit constants (and their sharpness) as the contribution.

**M3. The growth question is not resolved; framing in abstract/title overstates.**
Location: abstract, Thm 1.1(ii), Thm 3.11, intro paragraph after Thm 1.1.
Problem: The paper poses "how fast that number must grow" but only proves sqrt(A) <= f(A) <= A/pi+4. The lower bound is pigeonhole (the quadratic PTE bound N(k) <= (k^2+4)/2 of Wright/Melzak); the "iff" with N(k)=O(k^alpha-type) is an exact translation into an open problem of PTE theory and carries no new information on either side. Any improvement of the lower exponent past 1/2 would, by the authors' own remark, prove N(k)=o(k^2), a famous open problem, so the lower bound cannot be improved by current methods; the upper bound is the trivial cone-count. The abstract's wording "grows at least like the square root of the area, and it grows linearly if and only if..." is accurate but reads as if a dichotomy had been obtained.
Why blocking: the headline contribution is a conditional equivalence; the reader must be told clearly (in the abstract and at the start of Section 3.3) that the true growth rate is open and that the equivalence is a reformulation. In addition the upper bound is for worst-case O; no information is given for typical O (Fig. 2 for 525 area classes hints at far smaller K_mult; the authors should say if the data suggest anything).
Resolve: sharpen the abstract and introduction; either add a non-trivial improvement of one side (e.g. an upper bound better than A/pi+4, or any lower bound beating sqrt(A) for the restricted classes f_g, f_n), or present the PTE equivalence explicitly as the main finding with the open question stated as Problem 1 at the same prominence as the theorem.

## 5. MINOR issues

- m1. Thm 1.1(iii) and Thm C(2): "near n distinct positive real orders" refers to formal real orders, which are not orbifolds. State this where used, and add "only integer orders are geometric" near the statement; the only geometric sharpness is n=3,4. For n >= 5 integer sharpness is open (Problem 3), so "n-1 never suffice" is not an orbifold statement for n >= 5.
- m2. Prop. 5.9 (collision-free sums to 4800) is purely computational and uses a large enumeration shared with the companion note (3.07e9 triples to 4800). It is not needed for any theorem; label it clearly as computational (done) and cite the note only for the enumeration, not re-present the data.
- m3. Proof of Thm 5.10: the descent paragraph (mod 5, mod 3 case analysis) is compressed; the reader must redo the local analysis. I confirmed it numerically but a referee should be offered a table of local conditions or a database label (LMFDB/Cremona) for E and E'.
- m4. Fig. 2 caption reads "s = Area/2pi <= 7/5" for the 525 area classes, while the horizontal axis runs to 10^3. Presumably a typo or a different quantity; fix. Also state how the K_mult computation is cut off (it is exact only for the finite list of signatures with that area).
- m5. Lemma 2.5/Thm 2.3: the admissibility of the heat function in the trace formula is given by a dense twelve-line argument; it is standard, so cite McKean/Hejhal for the statement and keep only what differs for orbifolds with elliptic elements.
- m6. Definition 2.2 compares O only with members of the class P, whereas Theorem 1.1(i) is claimed "among all closed orientable hyperbolic 2-orbifolds" (Sig). Fine, but non-orientable or reflector orbifolds are not covered; mention that explicitly in the abstract/intro ("orientable, cone points only").
- m7. Abstract: "cone-order sum 17 together with its minimal, isolated failure": the failure is at sum 18; rephrase to avoid reading as sum 17.
- m8. Theorem 1.2(ii) says "they are the only such pair with cone-order sum 18 and no pair has a smaller sum": add "up to permutation of the triads".
- m9. In the Introduction the claim "To our knowledge ... first sharp threshold for a finite number of heat invariants on a family of hyperbolic orbifolds" is fine, but the reader should be told that finiteness for fixed area follows trivially from known determination results; mention in one line.
- m10. Numerics: error bars labelled "heuristic" and "not certified"; "within 0.42 and 0.53 of these heuristic error bars" is a statement about a fit, not a proof of anything; downgrade the wording in Section 7 or move to the supplement.
- m11. Reference [20] (companion) is "in preparation" and is cited for the arithmetic of the two-coefficient degeneracies; the note is also cited the other way round ("treated in the companion paper [7]"). Reviewers cannot check mutual statements until both exist as preprints; give an arXiv identifier or include the needed statements in the submission.
- m12. The heat trace expansion factor (4pi t)^{-1} and the convention of c_j (coefficient of t^{j-2}) is used from (1); once c_1 = Area/4pi appears with t^{-1}, a one-line reminder of this normalisation next to Thm 1.1 would help.

## 6. Presentation

- P1. **Length and structure.** 35 pages in the journal class and four distinct stories: (a) the algebraic count and PTE, (b) the triangle threshold and its elliptic-curve isolation, (c) quantitative locality, (d) stability with certificates and numerics. They are linked by the one mechanism (one new odd power sum per coefficient), but each is long, and (b) and (d) are the bulk of the technical verification. I recommend splitting: a paper on Sections 2-3 (+ Section 4 shortened), and a paper on the triangle orbifolds with the arithmetic (which would also absorb the companion note), and moving Section 6's certificate machinery elsewhere. At present there is no discussion of why the pieces belong together beyond the mechanism.
- P2. **Overlap with the companion note.** The note proves counting bounds for coincident triples (N(X) >= (3/128pi^4+o(1)) X (log X)^2, upper bound X^{3+eps}, classes of every size) and an exact enumeration to S=6000. The submission shares: the cubic C_Lambda, the identification of the minimal pair with a point of order 6 in the note vs. the elliptic-curve isolation here (the note's Remark 3.3 and the manuscript's Thm 5.10 treat the same curve C_{27/2}; the note's table row 2, 18, 27/2, rank 0 repeats it), the enumeration to 4800 (Prop. 5.9 vs note Section 6), and the isosceles pairs scaling O(2k,8k,8k)/O(3k,3k,12k). The note is genuinely different mathematics (number theory counting, MSC 11D25/11G05), so it is not salami in the strict sense, and the manuscript properly credits it. But the manuscript's Sections 5.1-5.2 contain material that belongs to a joint treatment with the note, and both documents cite each other as "in preparation". The submission does stand on its own for Theorems 1.1, 1.2, 1.3 as stated (all proofs are inside), but I would ask the authors to consolidate (see P1).
- P3. **Checkability for a referee.** Sections 2-5 are checkable by a competent referee in a few days (I did so, see section 3). Section 6 (Hurwitz factorisation, Theorem 6.4, Prop. 6.9, Table 1) is dense, with parameters (ampr, cond, zeta_n, rn, Xi_mu) defined in-line and not tabulated; the "certified" thresholds are the output of code run on rational arithmetic and depend on the repository. A referee cannot realistically verify Table 1 on paper. The repository exists and is described, but the Zenodo archive is a placeholder (known).
- P4. **Figures.** Fig. 1 (mirror argument) and Fig. 3 (strata) are useful. Fig. 2 has the caption problem in m4. Fig. 4 (recovery slopes 1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/2) is good. Fig. 5 is fine. Figures carry some colour-only distinctions (light/dark grey), partially mitigated by the stated colour-blind-safe maps.
- P5. **Supplement.** The supplement is used appropriately for the experiments, the blind recovery and a table of all 83 triads; the manuscript says "nothing here is used in a proof". The blind recovery is "easy by design" (c_3 differs by about 10^4 error bars), so it adds little evidence; I would shorten the corresponding Section 7 to a half page and rely on the supplement. The protocol commit date (1 October 2026) is later than the main computations, so "blind" means label-blind only; this is fine but should be stated as such.
- P6. **Style.** The paper is carefully written and honest about limitations (open problems, a-posteriori logic, heuristic error bars). The introduction is heavy on citations of exact theorem numbers, which is good for verification but makes the first read slow; Theorem 1.1 and 1.2 are crisp and credible on first read.

## 7. Desk decision and expected recommendation

**Desk-reject probability: about 25%.**
Reasons against desk rejection: the main theorems are clearly stated, credible on a first read, and I confirmed the numerical/arithmetical claims I tested; the topic (hearing orbifold invariants from heat coefficients) is within spectral geometry; the paper is honest about what is open; the computational claims are archived. Reasons for desk rejection (the 25%): the paper is long and heterogeneous, roughly half of it outside geometric analysis (number theory, certificate algebra, FEM); the analytic core is classical; the central growth question is left open and reduces to an open problem in additive number theory; the companion note is unpublished, and the arithmetic of Section 5 overlaps with it. An editor who requires clearer geometric-analytic novelty or who dislikes the multi-topic structure could reasonably return it, suggesting a split.

**Expected recommendation after review: major revision (confidence about 60%).** Alternatives: reject with encouragement to split and resubmit (about 30%); minor revision (about 10%, if the referees only check Sections 2-5).

What would resolve each item:
- M1: either prove sharpness/optimal exponents for realisable data (at least show whether the exponent for realisable data is always 1/2) or restate Thm 1.3 and the abstract precisely; make the a-posteriori nature part of the theorem; connect (or explicitly disclaim) the link with eigenvalue errors.
- M2: prove or delete the Remark 4.7 claim; recast Section 4 as a quantitative refinement of known results.
- M3: reframe the growth statement honestly; add at least one genuine new inequality (any improvement of an end of the interval for f, f_g or f_n) or give the PTE equivalence full prominence with the open status stated in the abstract.
- P1/P2: split or consolidate with the companion note; shorten Section 7 and Section 6; move certificate machinery and numerics to the supplement.
- m1-m12: editorial and clarity fixes listed above (cheap).

What is good (plainly): the one-power-sum-per-coefficient structure and the parity (Newton-identity) lemma give a clean and correct uniform bound floor(Area/pi)+4, and the sqrt(A) lower bound through PTE is a real and unexpected link; the exact threshold 17 for triangle orbifolds and the proof of isolation of (2,8,8)/(3,3,12) (all scalings, via a rank-0 curve) is neat and verified; closed forms for all cone coefficients are correct and independently checked; the locality theorem with explicit constants and exact sharpness is a useful quantitative statement; the manuscript is candid about gaps; code and data are public.
