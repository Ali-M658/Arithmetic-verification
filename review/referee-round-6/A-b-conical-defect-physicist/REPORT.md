# Referee report: "How much of a hyperbolic orbifold does heat hear?" (Annals of Global Analysis and Geometry)

Referee profile: mathematical physicist (heat-kernel coefficients and zeta-regularised quantities on spaces with conical defects). My assigned coverage is Section 2 in full (2.1–2.4), Appendix B and Supplement S8. I read the rest of the paper only far enough to see how Section 2 is used.

## Summary

The paper studies closed orientable hyperbolic 2-orbifolds with cone points. It asks how many heat invariants c_1, c_2, ... are needed to determine the signature (genus and cone orders).

Section 2 provides the analytic input:
- **Theorem 2.3** is the Selberg trace formula for the heat function, justified by an approximation argument (Lemma B.1).
- **Lemmas 2.4–2.5** give an explicit bound on the hyperbolic (closed-geodesic) term.
- **Proposition 2.7** gives the full small-t expansion in closed form. The cone coefficients are b_l(m) = (−1)^l p_l(m)/m, where the even polynomials p_l are generated by Φ_m(u) = (cot u − m cot mu)/(4m sin u) (Lemma 2.6). Appendix B (Proposition B.2) shows that the cone series and the area series *separately* have enveloping remainders.
- **Lemma 2.10** is the triangular structure: each order in t brings in one new odd power sum P_{2k−1} − R. Every counting result of Sections 3–6 rests on this.
- **Section 2.4** treats variable curvature:
  - Proposition 2.11 decomposes the cone term as b_l = Σ_i β_{l,i} Π_i(m) when m ≥ l−1. It gives a closed form for the top coefficient β_{l,l+1} and for the part linear in the jet of K.
  - Proposition 2.13 shows that outside constant curvature the counts lose their meaning. Equal c_2 allows metrics with equal H_L for every L. The minimal pair O(2,8,8), O(3,3,12) carries metrics, flat near the cone points, whose heat invariants agree to all orders.
- **Supplement S8** describes the computations and lists the statements that rest on computer search alone.

## Significance

- **The constant-curvature part (Sections 2.1–2.3 and Appendix B) is correct, carefully done and useful.**
  - The closed-form generating function Φ_m and the formula (4)–(5) are a clean repackaging of Uçar's all-order coefficients at curvature −1.
  - Deriving them directly from the trace formula makes the paper self-contained.
  - The enveloping-remainder estimate (Proposition B.2) is, to my knowledge, new. It is what one wants when using the expansion quantitatively; the explicit hyperbolic-term bound of Lemma 2.5 is the same kind of tool.
- **Lemma 2.10 is essentially in Uçar.** The authors say so honestly. Its use with a fixed finite number of coefficients is the paper's own idea.
- **Section 2.4 is interesting, but its novelty is overstated relative to the physics literature on curved conical defects.** See M2. The genuinely new items are:
  - the radial reduction for non-symmetric germs when m ≥ l−1;
  - the closed form β_{l,l+1} = 4^l l! [v^{2l}](f^{-1})';
  - the all-order linear part (2/(l−1)!) Π_{l+1}(m) (−Δ)^{l−1}K;
  - the extension/obstruction constructions of Proposition 2.13.
- **On the physics framing:** the slogan "heat explores a disc of radius √t" is not right near a high-order cone point (m1). The paper never connects its own analytic time scale (π/m)² to the physical picture, although the connection is simple and illuminating.

## MAJOR

**M1 (p. 5, "The analysis" paragraph; also the opening of p. 42). The bracketing claim is false for the heat trace.**

The Introduction says Proposition 2.7 gives the expansion "with remainders that alternate in sign and are bounded by the next term (Proposition B.2), so that consecutive truncations bracket the heat trace for t below the scale (π/µ)²".

Proposition B.2 proves this for the area series and the cone series *separately*, and their remainders have **opposite** signs: (−1)^{K+1} and (−1)^K. Each c_j = α_{j−1}·Area/4π + Σ b_{j−2}(m_i) is itself a sum of terms of opposite signs. So for R_K := Z − Hyp − Σ_{j≤K+1} c_j t^{j−2} neither the sign nor the "next term" bound follows. Both actually fail. Exact quadrature of I + E gives:
- **(2;2), t = 0.05, K = 1:** R = 1.0775·10⁻³ but the next term is c_3 t = 1.0417·10⁻³, so |R| exceeds the next term.
- **(4;2), t = 0.05, K = 2:** R = −2.684·10⁻⁵, next term −2.505·10⁻⁵.
- **(3;2), t = 0.2, K = 2:** R = −7.08·10⁻⁶ while the next term is +1.07·10⁻⁴. The signs are opposite, so the consecutive truncations do not bracket.

All three are far below (π/µ)² = π²/4. In my scan of (g; m^n) with m ≤ 4, n ≤ 12, g ≤ 4 there were 199 failures. In the paper's own examples (O(2,8,8), O(3,3,12), (0;3,3,3,3)) bracketing happens to hold, because the cone part dominates.

What is true is the bound (6). It can be sharpened, because the two remainders have opposite signs: |R_K| ≤ max(Area·|α_{K+1}|/4π, Σ|b_K(m_i)|)·t^K. Bracketing holds for each piece separately, or for the full trace whenever one piece's remainder dominates.

**M2 (p. 10, paragraph after Proposition 2.7; p. 6 "Prior work"; Section 2.4 and Problem 5(a)). The statement about non-integral cone angles is incorrect, and prior work on curved cones is missing.**

The paper says the real-m extension of b_l "is algebraic only, since for a cone of non-integral angle and nonzero curvature the heat expansion has a different form [20]". This misreads [20].
- Schueth (arXiv:2511.22255, Introduction, pp. 2–3) attributes half-powers of t and log t terms to f''(0) ≠ 0 in g = dr² + f(r)²dθ², i.e. to curvature that is unbounded at the tip.
- She states that when f''(0) = 0 the coefficients b_ℓ depend rationally on f'(0), and that "it plays no role at all whether n = 1/f'(0) is a natural number or not". She does not prove this.
- A constant-curvature cone of any angle has f = α s_κ, so f''(0) = 0.

The physics literature computed exactly these coefficients for arbitrary angle decades ago:
- **Fursaev**, Phys. Lett. B 334 (1994) 53, hep-th/9405143, eqs. (3.26)–(3.28). For cone angle α, a_{α,n} = Σ_{k≤n} α c_k(α) G_{nk}. Here c_k(α) = (i/4α)∫_Γ cot(πw/α)(sin w/2)^{−2k}dw is a polynomial of degree 2k in 2π/α, and c_2 ∝ ((2π/α)²−1)((2π/α)²+11). Fursaev also states agreement with Donnelly at α = 2π/n.
  - With m = 2π/α this is the factor (m²−1)(m²+11) of p_1.
  - It is also precisely the decomposition b_l = Σ_i β_{l,i} Π_i(m) of Proposition 2.11(i): Π_i(m) is the integer-angle value of Fursaev's c_i, and β_{l,i} corresponds to his G_{li}.
- **Dowker**, "Heat kernels on curved cones", Class. Quantum Grav. 11 (1994) L137, hep-th/9406002, eqs. (2)–(3).
- **Fursaev and Solodukhin**, Phys. Rev. D 52 (1995) 2133, hep-th/9501127.
- These results are widely used in entanglement and Rényi-entropy computations; Solodukhin's Living Reviews article (2011) is a standard entry point.

The authors must:
- (a) correct or properly justify the "algebraic only" sentence;
- (b) cite this literature in Section 1.2 and Section 2.4, and restate what is new in Proposition 2.11. In my reading that is the radial reduction for non-symmetric germs when m ≥ l−1, the closed form for β_{l,l+1}, and the all-order linear part. The decomposition in (i) is not new in its rotationally symmetric form.
- (c) Note that if the real-m extension is the heat coefficient of a constant-curvature cone of angle 2π/m, then the "real orders" of Sections 3 and 6, and the stability theorem, describe genuine geometric objects (curved cones / cosmic strings in H²). Proving or citing this would strengthen Section 6. Otherwise it should be flagged as open.

**M3 (pp. 12–13, proof of Proposition 2.11). Several steps of an advertised main analytic result are only sketched.**
- **(a) Pole order.** "Following Schueth's proof of [19, Thm 3.7] to all orders ... so the t^l term has pole order at most 2l+2 in C, with equality only through the Gaussian and the area element". This is the heart of (ii), and it is a sketch. A lemma should state the Laplace-method expansion of the fixed-point integral in the variables (√t, C), the bookkeeping of C-powers, and why the van Vleck factor and the corrections to d_u(r) = C r W_u^{1/2} lower the pole order by at least 2 at fixed t-power.
- **(b) Radial reduction.** The argument needs 𝓑_l(Φ) to be given by an SO(2)-invariant polynomial in the jet. Donnelly's polynomial is a priori only Z_m-equivariant on Z_m-invariant jets; averaging over SO(2) fixes this and should be stated.
  - It should also be said which jet is meant (metric in normal coordinates or curvature jet).
  - The metric with averaged curvature K̄ agrees with the radial projection of the metric jet only up to terms quadratic in non-radial modes; that is enough, but it needs saying.
- **(c) Duhamel computation in (iii).** It uses ψ = |x|^{2l} on all of R² and takes "tr" of a non-trace-class operator. A cut-off ψ and an O(t^∞) locality statement are needed.

I stress that the *conclusions* of 2.11(ii)–(iii) agree with every independent check I made (item 8 above), including Schueth's l = 2 coefficient. This is a rigor issue, not a suspected error. Because the Introduction presents Section 2.4 as one of three analytic pillars, it must be brought to AGAG standard or explicitly labelled as a sketch.

## MINOR

**m1 (Abstract; p. 2; Fig. 1 caption; p. 3). The physics framing of cone points.**

"Heat ... explores a disc of radius about √t and learns only what lies in that disc" is not accurate near a cone point of order m.
- In the orbifold chart, a point at distance r from the cone point sees its j-th image at distance 2r sin(πj/m); for j = 1 that is about 2πr/m.
- So heat feels the cone point out to r ~ m√t/π, not √t. A flat-cone image sum at t = 0.02, m = 12 gives 4πt·h_t ≈ 7.8, 3.4, 2.0, 1.2 at r = 0.14, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8. The caption's "about 1 at smooth points" fails within roughly 4√t of the order-12 point.
- This is exactly the physical origin of the paper's analytic scale (π/m)². The moment generating function 1/(2 sin((a−s)/2)) has its nearest singularity at s = ±2π/m, giving remainders e^{−(2π/m)²/4t} = e^{−π²/(m²t)}. The local expansion in curvature × (radius of influence)² breaks down once m√t/π reaches the curvature radius.

I recommend replacing the √t-disc slogan near cone points by this picture and linking it to Section 4's (π/µ)². Separately, "large orders are heard late" (p. 3) conflicts with "describes diffusion only for t small compared with (π/m)²". "Late" presumably means at high order in t; say so.

**m2 (p. 14, proof of Proposition 2.13(i)).** "By locality b_l(p) = K_0^l p_l(m)/m [13, Thm 4.20]" reintroduces the dependence on [13] that Appendix B was written to remove. The paper can argue internally instead: b_l(p) is a weight-2l polynomial in the jet, so on constant-curvature jets it equals c·K_0^l, and Proposition 2.7 fixes c at K_0 = −1. The same remark applies to the K^l rule at the end of Section 2.3 (p. 12). For K < 0 that rule follows from scaling; for K > 0 it needs the polynomial argument.

**m3 (p. 14, last paragraph of Proposition 2.13).** "Theorems A, 3.4, 3.8 and 3.11 and Corollary 3.5 ... therefore hold in that class" is too loose.
- The derivative hypothesis ∇^jK = 0 for j ≤ 2L−6 depends on L, which differs from theorem to theorem: L = n for Theorem A, L = M for 3.8(i), and L ≥ max(n+g−g′, ...) for Corollary 3.5, which makes the order of vanishing depend on the area.
- Theorem 3.8(ii),(iv) are constructions, and it is unclear what "hold" means for them.
- With Area read as −2πχ, Corollary 3.5's "computable from c_1" fails: χ is not a heat invariant in that class, since c_2 = χ/6 + Σb_0.
- State precisely which conclusions survive, or delete the paragraph.

**m4 (p. 12, end of Section 2.3).** "At K = 0, +1 the t^0 coefficient already determines the orbifold type (Section 1.2)": state whether c_1 is also used, and give the exact statement from [2, Thms 5.14–5.15].

**m5 (p. 8, Theorem 2.3 and Lemma 2.4).** Hyp sums over hyperbolic *conjugacy classes*, while Lemma 2.4 counts "closed geodesics, primitive or not". The proof counts classes, which is what is needed. Say so, because γ and γ^{-1} give the same geometric geodesic, and geodesics through cone points can have several classes.

**m6 (p. 41, Lemma B.1).** The heat function is already admissible in the standard Selberg class (Hejhal, Iwaniec Thm 10.2: holomorphic in |Im r| ≤ 1/2 + ε, with enough decay). Lemma B.1 is correct but could be replaced by a citation, or kept with a remark that it is included only for self-containedness.

**m7 (p. 11, Remark 2.9).** I could not check the identity mΦ_m^{(2k)}(0) = 2·4^k k! m c_{S_k}(π/m) because the thesis [13] was not accessible headlessly. Please state Uçar's normalisation of c_{S_k} explicitly so a reader can check it without the thesis. My independent derivation from the trace formula confirms (5) itself.

**m8 (p. 14, Remark 2.12(b)).** The lower-order KΔK coefficients of b_3 rest on machine computation alone. I confirmed the K³ part, the Δ²K part (exactly mΠ_4/m) and the top KΔK coefficient, but not the rest. Either give an independent check (for example a contour-integral evaluation at non-integer angle à la Fursaev/Dowker, which would also address M2(c)) or say explicitly that these coefficients are unverified by a second method.

**m9 (Supplement S8(ii)).** The soundness of the modular rejection also needs every used prime to be coprime to the denominators of R(V), e_1 and P_3. This holds because all entries are at most 220 < 223, but the text mentions only the skip condition 3(1 − e_1R) ≡ 0. Add one sentence.

**m10 (p. 10, (6)).** For clarity, write out that Σ_{j≤K+1} c_j t^{j−2} contains the area terms α_0..α_K and the cone terms b_0..b_{K−1}, so the right side of (6) consists of the first omitted area term and the first omitted cone term.

## Presentation

- **P1.** The letter B carries at least four meanings: the Bernoulli numbers B_{2n}, the Bernoulli polynomials B_{2l}(x), the fixed-point coefficients 𝓑_l(γ), and the bound B(ℓ, diam, t). Likewise b_l(m) (constant curvature) and b_l(p) (variable curvature), and fraktur 𝔪_n (moments, p. 41) against the order m. Rename some of these.
- **P2.** Section 2.4 is very dense. Proposition 2.11 would read better with "radial part of the jet" defined in a displayed sentence before the statement. The weight convention (∇^jK has weight j+2) should be stated once.
- **P3.** Proposition 2.13(ii): point out that the example (0;2^8), (0;3,3,3) has χ = −2 against χ = 0. It is a striking illustration that in variable curvature c_2 does not even fix χ, and it currently goes unremarked.
- **P4.** p. 13, end of the proof of (ii): "At weight 2 and 4 the only monomials of degree at least 2 are K²" is correct but terse. Say that ∇K(p) = 0 (by rotation invariance, m ≥ 2) and that |∇K|² has weight 6.
- **P5.** p. 2: the Fig. 1 caption is accurate as far as it goes, but given m1 it should mention the extended region of enhanced heat density around high-order cone points.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** Confidence is high for Sections 2.1–2.3, Appendix B and S8, which I checked line by line and by independent computation, and moderate-to-high for Section 2.4.

The constant-curvature machinery on which the whole paper rests is correct, and in places (the closed form via Φ_m, the enveloping remainders, the explicit hyperbolic-term bound) it is a genuine improvement on what is in the literature. Acceptance from my side requires three things:
- withdrawal or correction of the false bracketing claim (M1);
- correction of the non-integral-angle statement, and an honest positioning of Section 2.4 against Fursaev, Dowker, Fursaev–Solodukhin and Schueth (M2);
- complete proofs, or explicit labelling as sketches, of the pole-order and radial-reduction steps of Proposition 2.11 (M3).

None of these threatens the main theorems, which use only Sections 2.1–2.3.
