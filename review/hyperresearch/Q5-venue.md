## 2. What a JGA paper in this area looks like

**Length.** Substantial. Of the records with page ranges: 32 pp (#1), 28 pp (#4), 58 pp (#5),
34 pp (#6), 53 pp (#8), 26 pp (#9). The exemplar (#2) runs about 18,000 words. **The
manuscript at 44 typeset pages double-spaced is comfortably in range**, and would be
mid-range at single spacing.

**Exact vs asymptotic.** Both, but the papers closest to this manuscript are the *exact* ones.
#4 gives a closed-form determinant; #11 computes second-term coefficients explicitly; #2
proves finiteness *with explicit bounds*. The venue clearly rewards results that produce a
number rather than an asymptotic regime. This is favourable: the manuscript's sharp threshold
at 17 and explicit minimal pair are exactly this flavour.

**How much computation.** Computation is welcome as *support*, not as the result. #10 is
largely explicit spectral computation, #4 is a closed-form evaluation. But in every case the
theorem is structural and the computation certifies or instantiates it. The manuscript's
architecture — interval-separation proof in §3, exhaustive enumeration relegated to an
appendix as corroboration — matches the venue's expectations well. §3's own framing ("this is
a structural argument, not an exhaustive search") is the right instinct.

**Tone.** None of the twelve claims priority. #2 — the closest analogue, proving a finiteness
theorem with explicit bounds — states its result without any "first" language.

## 4. What this paper has to look like to be a JGA paper

The manuscript is already close. Concretely:

1. **Keep the structural proof primary and the enumeration in the appendix.** This is the
   single strongest architectural match to the venue, and §3 already does it.

2. **Replace the priority sentence in §1.3(b).** None of the twelve comparators claims to
   be first at anything, including the one proving a finiteness theorem with explicit bounds.
   The result stands on its own; the "to our knowledge this is the first…" is the sentence
   most likely to draw a referee's fire, and removing it costs nothing. See
   `Q3-priority-claim.md`.

3. **Fix the bibliography.** Several works cited as preprints are published — Bari–Hunsicker
   (Canad. J. Math. 2020), Schueth (Ann. Global Anal. Geom. 2026), Gómez-Serrano–Orriols
   (J. Differential Equations), Proctor–Stanhope (Diff. Geom. Appl. 2010) — and the DGGW
   erratum is uncited. At a Springer journal with a copy-editing pass, stale preprint
   citations read as a paper prepared without checking. Full list in `refs/sources.bib`.

4. **The planned stability section fits the venue**, and #6 (Daudé–Kamran–Nicoleau) is the
   precedent to cite for that. But frame it as *setting*-novel, not technique-novel — see
   `Q4-stability.md`.

5. **The $n$-cone extension is cheaper than the manuscript assumes**, and would materially
   raise the paper's reach. Remark 5.6's open lower bound is settled for $n=4$ by an explicit
   witness, using coefficients already in the literature — see `Q2-cone-coefficients.md`.

6. **Engage Guy's D16 and Schinzel in §5.** The venue rewards situated results; §5 currently
   reads as isolated when it has a well-studied structural neighbour — see
   `Q1-diophantine-novelty.md`.

7. **Length is fine.** No need to expand for the venue's sake; if the $n$-cone material goes
   in, the paper will land squarely mid-range.
